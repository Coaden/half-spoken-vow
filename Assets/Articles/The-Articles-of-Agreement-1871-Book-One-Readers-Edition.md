# Appendix: The Articles of Agreement

## Book One Reader’s Edition

*The Half-Spoken Vow*

> This appendix reproduces the portions of the 1871 Instrument available to the parties by the close of Book One. It preserves the original wording, not the present legal disposition: by the close of Book One, Clauses the First, Second, and Third have been lawfully defeated and remain visible but barred from execution. The Fourth through Ninth Clauses remain under seal and are not reproduced here.

# ARTICLES OF AGREEMENT

## Made and Entered in the Year of Our Lord One Thousand Eight Hundred and Seventy-One

### Between Local Nine and the Marrow Trust, for the Keeping of the Peace and the Preservation of Their Several Records

When two Bodies, differing in Blood, Duration, Necessity, and Proof, are nevertheless obliged to inhabit one Country, cross the same Waters, hold Estates under the same civil Peace, and answer for Injuries which neither can judge by the Record of the other, Prudence requires that their Concord be neither entrusted to Affection nor abandoned to Memory.

The one Party bears Truth in the living Body, in Kinship, in Presence, in the witnessed Change, and in the Voice openly given. The other Party preserves Truth in Writing, in Seal, in Appointment, in Succession, and in the Instrument which survives the Hand. Neither Party shall be compelled to call the other’s Record sufficient in matters wholly its own; yet each Party, by these presents, acknowledges these Articles as the place wherein the two Records meet.

Therefore Local Nine, speaking in open assembly by those competent to bear its assent, and the Marrow Trust, acting by its officers under hand and seal, do establish these Articles as their entire compact; not for dominion of either over the other, nor for the making of any Person into Estate or Chattel, but for the certain naming of Duties, the limitation of Remedies, and the continuance of Peace when private inclination fails.

## Declaration of Reserved Rights

And because a command concealed is but an ambush, and an obligation unnamed cannot be answered, it is first declared:

1. That no supernatural Duty, Office, Forfeiture, or Claim arising hereunder shall take hold until its operative words are named aloud before Witnesses competent to both Records.
2. That a Refusal requires neither writing, witness, seal, cause, nor ceremony; that Silence shall be taken for Refusal and never for Assent; and that no Entry may convert Refusal into Speech.
3. That bodily attraction, fear, pain, hunger, change, nearness, direction, or other Symptom shall be evidence only of itself, and shall grant no title to the Body, Dwelling, Labor, Affection, or Answer of another.
4. That the word **Bride** describes the Person named to the operation of these Articles and creates no species, caste, rank, ownership, civil marriage, or lesser capacity.
5. That whatever is required shall be performed as named, and whatever is not named shall not be supplied by custom, convenience, office, kinship, implication, or the supposed necessity of Peace.

## Constitution and Operation of These Articles

The foregoing Recitals, the Reserved Rights, and the following Rules of Constitution shall govern every Clause and every proceeding hereunder. They are not additional Clauses. The operative Sequence consists of exactly nine Clauses, numbered the First through the Ninth, fixed in that Order and incapable of acceleration, substitution, enlargement, or rearrangement by any Party acting alone.

### I. Of Naming and the Standing Thereby Created

In each Generation there may be named one living Person as Bride, according to the concurrence of the written and borne Records. The Naming shall identify the Person by a name sufficient to distinguish her, shall state the Generation and Line through which she is named, and shall be read aloud before an accepted Witness while she is present or thereafter personally served upon her.

Naming makes the Person a Party to these Articles with standing to receive Notice, inspect the Record concerning her, retain Counsel, state Refusal, offer Performance, file Objection, demand Read-Back, challenge Construction, and seek Satisfaction, Waiver, Release, or Defeat. Naming does not itself:

1. activate any numbered Clause;
2. create Residence, Surety, Custody, Invitation, Office, Membership, civil Marriage, or completed Vow;
3. bind the Bride to the half of a Vow spoken by another;
4. transfer her Body, Labor, Property, Threshold, Kinship, or Affection; or
5. make every Recital or Clause presently due.

The Naming and the numbered Clauses are separate acts in one compact. No Clerk, Reader, Officer, or Witness shall describe Naming as the performance of the Third Clause or of any other Clause.

### II. Of the Persons and Offices Competent to Act

The following capacities shall be distinct, and no Person shall acquire one merely by performing the work of another:

1. **The Bride** is the named Party. She may inspect, answer, refuse, perform, file, challenge, and elect relief in her own standing. She is not thereby a member of Local Nine, a trustee, an officer, a Surety, or property of either Party.
2. **An Authorized Reader** may present a due Clause in the words of the executed text. The Reader may explain no omitted obligation into existence and may not become a Witness merely by reading.
3. **An Accepted Witness** is a Person or continuing Office recognized by both Records as competent to bear the act in question. The Witness certifies identity, presence, words, capacity, and those conditions personally observed; the Witness does not choose the Answer or consequence.
4. **A Recorder or Reporter** may take down, transcribe, compare, and read back what was said. Accurate transcription supplies evidence of the proceeding but gives the Recorder no lineage Office and creates no obligation beyond the words lawfully named.
5. **A Filing Officer** may receive, number, preserve, and serve an Instrument. Acceptance for filing is not acceptance upon the merits and shall not be confused with witnessing a Vow.
6. **Counsel** may advise, draft, examine, object, and argue by ordinary authority. Counsel may not speak a personal Answer for the Bride, create a borne fact, or become a trustee by service in daylight.
7. **The Custodial Holder** shall preserve the third Counterpart; present what that Office is required to present; convene or certify the joint proceeding when permitted; and preserve every Refusal and objection. Custody grants no power to compel Speech or select the consequence.
8. **Local Nine** may bear Kinship, Presence, Change, open Vote, and other bodily facts through competent members. It may enforce only those consequences its charter and these Articles recognize.
9. **The Marrow Trust** may prove Writing, Seal, Appointment, Office, Instrument, Estate, and Succession through a serving trustee or duly authorized human agent. No agent’s service shall silently elevate that agent into lineage Office.

No Claim shall be enforced merely because the Person who reads it also keeps the file, or because the Person who witnesses it desires the result.

### III. Of the Hybrid Record

For Local Nine, a valid Record may be borne in the Body and established in open presence by Kinship, Scent, witnessed Change, or spoken Vote. For the Marrow Trust, a valid Record may be written, sealed, filed, registered, or preserved in lawful succession. For purposes of these Articles, a shared Entry exists only where:

1. the same operative fact is received in both Records; or
2. one Record contains the fact proper to it and the other contains an independent acknowledgment of that fact’s receipt and limited effect; or
3. the executed Articles expressly designate the Custodial Holder’s certification as sufficient pending joint review.

One Record may prove what lies within its own competence. It may not lend its conclusion to fill an element the other Record was required independently to establish. Writing cannot manufacture Kinship. Borne Kinship cannot appoint an Office. Repetition between them is not independent proof where each merely repeats the other.

Every Entry shall preserve the exact words spoken, the writing received, the capacity in which each Person acted, the time, the place, the Witnesses, and every contemporaneous Refusal, qualification, or objection. An omission shall remain an omission until lawfully supplied; it shall not be made clean by inference.

### IV. Of Due Date, Activation, and the Fixed Sequence

A Clause becomes **due** only upon the occurrence of the condition stated in the preceding Clause or in the Schedule executed with these Articles. The existence of a Clause is not its activation. Knowledge of its words is not Notice. Preparation to answer it is not acceptance.

A due Clause becomes **active** only when an Authorized Reader reads its operative demand, without material omission or addition, before an accepted Witness, and when the Bride has been given an immediate opportunity to answer, refuse, request read-back, or object to the Reader’s authority and text.

Presentation may be required by either original Institution acting in its proper capacity; by the Custodial Holder upon independently verified maturity; or by a Claimant whose existing standing has already been received in both Records and will be directly altered by disposition of the due Clause. A person possessing information, kinship, grievance, preparation, or an expected benefit but no such standing may advise or supply evidence to a qualified Claimant, but may not require presentation in that person’s own name. The Reader and Custodial Holder shall verify maturity independently; a qualified petition requests performance of their Offices and does not delegate those Offices to the petitioner.

No later Clause shall activate while an earlier Clause remains active, suspended, or undisposed, except where the earlier Clause itself expressly names the later act as its consequence. A Clause defeated or satisfied shall not revive merely because a later Clause fails.

### V. Of Interruption, Suspension, and Resumption

An active reading may be interrupted by a superior Act recognized in both Records, by joint Order, or by an impossibility which the Reader and Witness are bound to acknowledge. Interruption shall stop execution at the word reached and shall preserve everything already spoken without converting preparation into performance.

The freely spoken first Half of a binding Vow is one such superior Act. It may interrupt an Article because it places the Speaker personally into both Records and strips the Speaker’s protections. It binds only the Speaker. It shall impose no Vow, Answer, Debt, gratitude, custody, or personal obligation upon the Bride, and it shall neither complete nor satisfy the interrupted Clause unless that Clause expressly requires and lawfully receives completed performance.

Unless another term is expressly stated, an interrupted Clause shall remain **suspended** and shall resume on the thirtieth Day after interruption, at the same operative demand and not at a newly enlarged construction. Resumption requires personal Notice stating the date, hour, Reader, Witness, text to be resumed, and every lawful means of Satisfaction, Waiver, Release, or Challenge then available.

Formal abandonment of the interrupting Half shall end that Half and the Speaker’s exposure, but only upon the Speaker’s excommunication: the Speaker’s name shall be struck from every lineage Office, succession, institutional protection, and standing to enforce a supernatural Claim. Such excommunication shall be permanent. It shall not erase the Speaker’s ordinary civil identity, divest property held in an individual civil capacity, sever the involuntary Attachment, erase the historical interruption, speak for the Bride, or retroactively validate any act taken during suspension. Only the Speaker may perform abandonment. The Person to whom the Half was spoken has no words to abandon, though an exposed Speaker remains subject to whatever lawful compulsion the exposure otherwise permits.

### VI. Of Satisfaction, Waiver, Release, Defeat, and Forfeiture

A Clause is **satisfied** only by performance of what its executed words require, entered in both Records according to the nature of the act. Performance of a later custom shall not be demanded where the original text permits a narrower act.

A Clause is **waived** only by every Person and Institution whose standing or protection the waived demand affects, each acting within its own authority after the price is stated. No officer may waive a bodily right belonging to another Person. Waiver of one demand shall not waive the Sequence or any unrelated objection.

A Person is **released** where the Institutions relinquish a Claim against that Person and the release is entered in both Records. Release of a Person is not amendment of a Clause.

A Clause is **defeated** only by the process and grounds stated in the Ninth Clause. Defeat bars execution but leaves the original words visible. It does not renumber the Articles.

A **forfeiture** arises only where a numbered Clause expressly identifies the Party, standing or Office forfeited; the act or omission producing it; the exact time of maturity; and what survives the loss. No forfeiture shall be implied from refusal, silence, delay, kinship, affection, poverty, illness, or the convenience of either Institution. Refusal survives every forfeiture.

### VII. Of Challenge and Enforcement

The Bride, a Person whose standing is directly altered, either Institution in its proper capacity, the Custodial Holder, or Counsel acting for such a Party may file a Challenge. A person in custody may challenge the custody but may not be required to challenge through the Office that holds that person.

A Challenge shall identify the exact executed words, the act complained of, the Record relied upon, the standing of the challenger, and the relief requested. Filing shall stay new execution only where these Articles say so, where both Institutions consent, or where the Custodial Holder certifies that proceeding would make lawful review impossible.

Where a timely Challenge identifies a material difference among the Counterparts in a number, term, or consequence, execution of the disputed consequence shall be stayed for ten calendar Days. The stay shall begin at the time the earlier disputed term would otherwise mature, shall neither restart nor amend that term, and shall preserve all Parties in their pre-forfeiture standing while joint examination proceeds. At the stay’s expiration the common executed text, or failing common text the authenticated original, shall control unless joint adjudication has already entered another lawful disposition.

No single trustee, member, reader, witness, custodian, clerk, reporter, counsel, or rival claimant may enforce a disputed supernatural obligation alone. Enforcement requires an active Clause, a competent Entry, proper standing, and the concurrence prescribed for the fact being enforced. Ordinary civil rights and remedies remain available without lineage permission.

### VIII. Of Sealing, Inspection, and Construction

All nine Clauses exist from the execution of these Articles. Sealing governs disclosure and custody; it does not postpone existence, create text, or permit a later officer to write what was absent in 1871.

The Parties may seal a Clause, Schedule, name, commencement condition, or custodial direction until the time fixed for inspection, provided the person against whom it is to operate receives the complete operative text before activation and sufficient opportunity to refuse, object, or challenge. A sealed portion may not execute while withheld from that Person.

A later deposition, statement, declaration, interpretation, construction, precedent, disposition, margin, or history of performance may show how an executed word was understood or misapplied. It may not become an amendment by age, repetition, institutional convenience, or the death of those who objected.

Where ambiguity exists, authority shall be considered in this order: the common executed text; a Schedule executed with it; a contemporaneous definition accepted in both Records; an accepted joint adjudication; and only thereafter later constructions and histories, which remain contestable. No construction may contradict the Reserved Rights or supply an obligation the original text did not name.

### IX. Of the Counterparts and Custody Before Activation

The three Counterparts shall be separately kept but shall constitute one Instrument. The Trust shall preserve one under seal; Local Nine shall preserve one with its witnessed acceptance; and the Custodial Holder shall preserve the third in civil succession independent of both Parties.

Inspection shall be logged by date, purpose, Persons present, condition of seal, and every page exposed. No Person who inspects may scrape, overwrite, lift a seal, substitute a page, alter a number, or make a private correction. A suspected difference shall be photographed or otherwise preserved, read aloud, and compared without first changing either object.

Nothing in this Section shall be called a tenth Clause. Upon these Reservations and constitutional Rules, the Parties covenant in the following fixed Sequence.

## Clause the First — Of Residence

The Bride shall **Reside** beneath the roof of the Trust within thirty Days of Naming.

For the purposes of this Clause, to Reside shall bear the meaning held at the execution of these Articles: to lodge beneath such roof for a stated number of several Nights within the appointed Period, every such Night to be entered by the Steward of the House and confirmed by one Witness competent to bear the presence of the Bride. The Bride may tender a stated number; any number accepted in both Records shall constitute the complete measure of her performance.

Continuous habitation shall not be required. No civil Domicile shall thereby be changed; no Lease, Dwelling, Threshold, Estate, or former Shelter shall be surrendered; no Invitation shall arise except as separately and freely given by a Resident; and no officer shall enlarge Residence into custody merely because custody would make location more certain.

Residence is demanded so that the Bride’s location during the stated Nights may exist in both Records. It conveys no Estate and grants no dominion over her Person.

Upon performance being entered in the written Record and borne by the living Witness, this Clause shall be satisfied and shall not be twice demanded of the same Bride.

## Clause the Second — Of Surety and the Limitation of Collective Punishment

Upon the satisfaction, waiver, or lawful defeat of the First Clause, the Line from which the Bride is named shall answer by one fit Person standing as Surety, and not by forfeiture laid indiscriminately upon the whole Line.

The Line shall bear in open assembly the Kinship and fitness of the Person proposed. The Trust shall enter the Surety as an Office. The Person so entered shall stand answerable for the Deed, if any, expressly named against that Office under a succeeding Clause; and while one Surety lawfully stands, no other Person of the Line shall be charged for the same default.

No presumption of fitness shall establish understanding. No writing of Office shall create bodily Kinship, and no bearing of Kinship shall, without more, prove acceptance of Office. If the two Records rely each upon the conclusion of the other and neither contains the Person’s own understanding, the Surety shall be defective and no derivative Claim shall issue from it.

No Surety may be required to speak for the Bride, complete a Vow, surrender another Person’s refusal, or perform a Deed not named before the Office was entered.

## Clause the Third — Of the Term Certain and the Witnessed Vow

When a Surety has been lawfully entered, a Term of nine calendar Days shall commence upon the Bride’s personal hearing of this Clause and the entry of her Answer or Refusal before the Custodial Holder.

Within that Term there shall be entered one of the following: a completed Vow freely and knowingly spoken in its two several Halves before an accepted Witness; a Release accepted in both Records; or a lawful Defeat of the demand then executing.

A Vow shall not be complete unless the two Halves are separated by an Interval and unless each Speaker, without borrowed words, can state these five permanent Consequences:

1. each shall thereafter know the direction and approximate distance of the other;
2. each shall know when the other is dying;
3. the mortal Party shall cease to age yet shall remain wholly capable of being killed;
4. neither shall thereafter form another Attachment; and
5. both shall stand permanently recorded as bonded, with standing that cannot be struck.

Completion shall further end each Party’s capacity to swear another binding Oath. It shall guarantee no love, obedience, fidelity, agreement, allegiance, proximity, shared thought, shared emotion, or happiness. It shall never be dissolved, severed, annulled, or erased.

No accepted Witness shall certify Completion where either Speaker is compelled, exposed and compellable, intoxicated, dying, ignorant of permanence, or subject at that moment to a Clause executing against that Speaker. A true desire expressed under defective Conditions remains a true desire, but it shall not form the permanent Vow.

If, at Midnight of the ninth Day, neither Completion, Release, nor lawful Defeat has been entered, the Bride’s provisional standing under the pending Sequence shall be forfeited, saving always her bodily authority to refuse; and the Surety shall pass into custodial keeping solely for the purpose of answering the next Deed lawfully named. Custody shall not make the Surety property, shall not erase Kinship, and shall not authorize bodily harm.

No consequence of this Clause shall be represented as consent to the Vow.

## Sealed Provisions

Clauses the Fourth through the Ninth form part of the executed Instrument and remain fixed in its original sequence. Their text is not reproduced in this Book One edition. Under Article VIII, no sealed provision may operate against a Person until that Person has received its complete operative text and a sufficient opportunity to refuse, object, or challenge.
