# Episode Sixty: The Unreported Cut

Episode Sixty: The Unreported Cut

On the Smithfield Street span, Elias asked whether June had brought him there to answer, and she said no before he could finish the question.

"No. I am not answering the vow tonight."

The words left no interval in which hope could disguise itself as misunderstanding. June had practiced them once in her apartment and disliked the sound. On the span they belonged to her.

Rain had stopped. The river moved below them, and traffic kept the bridge public without making the conversation anyone else's.

The pedestrian pavement shone under the lamps. Tires hissed over wet steel behind them. Far below, the Monongahela carried broken reflections west toward the point. The bridge was neutral ground by custom because members needed bodies present and trustees needed terms they could carry away. Tonight no institution had called the meeting. June had.

She chose the hour carefully. Dusk had deepened enough that Elias could stand beyond the shadow of the downtown buildings without risking direct sun. It was not an accommodation extracted from her body. It was how two people with different physical facts managed to occupy the same public place.

Elias stood six feet away. "Understood."

"Say what you understand," June said.

"That your refusal is current, complete, and requires no reason. That asking whether tonight was chosen for an answer placed pressure on the meeting before you stated its purpose. I will not ask again."

"Good."

"I am not abandoning it either."

"Understood."

"You are allowed more than one word."

"I am attempting not to bargain."

"You can speak without offering terms."

"I am learning the difference."

That was not an apology. It was more useful than one offered to make her reassure him.

June held the permanent-party election inside her coat. "I chose the Articles. Not their ownership of me. The obligation of keeping the record, carrying Tess's case, and making the remaining clauses answer."

"Six clauses remain," she continued. "The first three are lawfully defeated and permanently barred from execution, though their original words remain. Milo is released. The Local keeps its charter. Marta is no longer business agent. Kenneth is exiled. You hold no office. Those are not temporary measures pending my answer."

"Agreed."

"The Trust register and Hall ledger both carry the count."

"They do."

"And Constance?"

"Will administer what remains. She believes the Articles can survive correction."

"She may be right."

Elias did not mistake that for surrender. "Yes."

"That choice does not require me."

"The half-vow is part of the record. Your testimony is part of the case. You are required in practical ways. Not entitled ones."

"A difficult distinction."

"You like those."

"I do."

June took the typed page from her coat. "Then listen to all five facts."

She read them, not as an invitation but as the permanent consequence that made any future answer meaningful. Direction and rough distance, always. Knowledge when the other was dying. The mortal party no longer aging and remaining entirely killable. No attachment with anyone else. Permanent registration with standing that could not be struck.

"And the lost capacities," she said.

"Neither of us could swear another binding oath. I could never hold trustee office. You could never be initiated as a member."

"Completion guarantees?"

"None of the things the institutions imply. Not love, obedience, fidelity, compatibility, allegiance, proximity, or happiness. No shared thoughts. No shared emotion. No compulsion."

"Can it be dissolved?"

"Never."

She folded the page. He knew the facts. So did she. Knowledge did not create consent, but without knowledge consent would be void.

She stated the boundary completely. She would neither answer nor abandon while pressure made either choice serve an institution. Remaining undecided was not the election. Keeping both options under her authority was.

"The symptoms continue," she said. "I know your direction. You know mine. Your spoken half leaves you exposed. None of that makes waiting an injury I am committing against you."

"Correct."

"If exposure becomes intolerable, formal abandonment remains yours. You do not need my permission."

The muscles beside his mouth tightened. "I know."

"Knowing is not agreeing."

"Then: I retain the choice and do not make it tonight. I will not use the cost of that choice as a claim against you."

The bridge traffic filled the silence after him. June let the statement stand without rewarding it. An ethical minimum was not romance. It was still necessary ground.

"If I answer later, it will be because I choose the five permanent facts with you after every present clause is quiet," she said. "If I abandon later, it will not be payment for your office or protection from somebody else's threat."

"And if neither happens?" he asked.

The question was permitted because it did not demand a timetable.

"Then neither happens."

"Yes."

"Until then, working alliance. Full disclosure of any claim that can reach the other. No compulsion. No interpreting symptoms as consent."

"Agreed."

"The alliance does not grant access to my apartment."

"Any invitation remains separately given and revocable."

"It does not make my agency Trust counsel."

"No."

"It does not make you a Local representative."

"Certainly not."

"That answer sounded almost happy."

"I have attended enough Local meetings to value the distinction."

The laugh surprised her. It was small and gone quickly, but it belonged on the bridge with everything else. No institution had ordered it. No symptom had caused it.

June handed him a copy of the proposed alliance terms. He read beneath the lamp. The agreement was human and revocable. It required notice, truthful disclosure, and confidentiality enforceable through ordinary remedies. It expressly created no office, oath, invitation, fiduciary duty, vow answer, or supernatural obligation.

"Cyd drafted this sentence," he said.

"Which one?"

"For avoidance of doubt, neither party is the other's weird husband."

"She negotiated hard for it."

"I accept the legal substance and object to the adjective."

"Noted. It stays."

They signed two copies against the bridge railing. No accepted witness attended. No lineage record took hold. The agreement mattered anyway.

"And you tell Pratt the truth."

"Tomorrow."

"Cyd says tomorrow has lost value."

"At ten in the morning, in writing and aloud."

That was better.

"He may leave," Elias said.

"Yes."

"The Trust may fail less carefully."

"Also yes."

"I am discovering that ordinary consequences lack the dignity of curses."

"They still send invoices."

"So I have learned."

The principal question that began in a deposition suite had an answer for now. June would not complete the vow under the Articles' pressure or as payment for anyone's rescue. She would not abandon the half-spoken state by transferring its cost to Elias. She would remain the bride because the position gave her a hand on the document, and she chose the responsibility of keeping it. Whether she ever answered freely remained hers and unanswered.

The word still angered her. Bride described what the Articles had made of her situation; it did not name her kind, her rank, or the whole of her future. Remaining did not mean submitting to the meaning Constance gave it or the one Marta feared. June would occupy the recorded position long enough to make every surviving clause meet a person who could read.

She had standing without membership. A claim without office. Work, wages, a six-month lease, and an aunt who would vote against her from an ordinary folding chair. Milo was home. Cyd's agency would open Friday. None of it was undamaged. All of it was real.

Elias accepted the boundary without asking when it might change.

He moved to the railing beside her only after she shifted her bag and left the space open. Not touching. Near enough that she could hear his coat sleeve brush wet iron when he leaned forward.

"What happens to Tess's case?" he asked.

"Clause four gets notice first. We determine whether counted residence still reaches her descendants after the definition ruling. Then five. One at a time."

"You expect me to help defeat the rest."

"I expect you to disclose what they do. Whether you help defeat them will be a choice you make each time."

"That is less flattering."

"It is more accurate."

Across the river, office lights came on floor by floor. Elias looked toward them as though the city had become newly difficult to read.

"Then I choose the first disclosure," he said. "Clause four has a notice form the Trust has never filed with the parish. Constance considers it unnecessary. I consider Delisle likely to disagree."

"Send it tomorrow after Pratt's call."

"I will."

An operational gain, offered without purchasing intimacy. June could work with that.

Only after the decision had settled did June remove Yolanda's unexplained entry.

She had waited until their principal business was finished because an unexplained danger could not steal the shape of her decision. The Articles had been answered for now: three clauses defeated, six operative, the forced wedding stopped, June's place chosen rather than imposed. What remained on the page did not reverse those facts.

"One more record," she said.

She read it aloud. Date this month. Incomplete attachment severed. Both registers annotated. Cutter's form consistent with Vera Odell. No principal named. No person identified. No corresponding commission or deed.

Elias asked to hold the copy. June gave it to him. He read the Trust notation first and then the Hall version Yolanda had transcribed beneath it.

"The witnesses?"

"They witnessed the resulting absence, not the act. We notify them tomorrow. No accusation."

"The instrument number is valid in form."

"Does it belong to another instrument?"

"I do not know. I no longer have authority to search the Trust register. I can request access as a permanent party's disclosed ally, or you can request it directly."

The correction was immediate. A week earlier he might have reached for a key that no longer belonged to him and called the result protection.

"I request it," June said. "You can identify the indexing practice if they grant access."

"Agreed."

"Somebody paid her price," Elias said.

"Yes."

"And both records lost the name."

"Or preserved its absence," June said.

She would not decide which before the record did. The blank could conceal a victim, protect a principal, or reproduce a form someone wanted blamed on Vera. The buyer, if there was one, remained unknown. The deed remained unknown. Even the identity of the cut person remained outside both systems that claimed standing had to be carried by a name.

She and Elias turned toward the downtown end of the span. They walked on the same side for the first time, with wet pavement between them and enough room for either to stop.

June folded the entry.

Somebody had paid Vera Odell's price that month, and the cut was performed on a person neither register could name.
