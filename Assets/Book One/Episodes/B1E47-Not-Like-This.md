# Episode Forty-Seven: Not Like This

Episode Forty-Seven: Not Like This

June revoked Elias's permission to touch her before she said another word on the span.

"You may not touch me. Not to stop speech, not to protect me, not because you think the result is invalid."

"Understood."

"Repeat the scope."

"I may not touch you to interrupt speech, prevent harm, correct an invalid act, or for any reason I believe protective. Emergency permission is not implied."

"If I fall?"

"I ask. If you cannot answer, I call human help unless contact is the only way to prevent death."

"You do not have emergency permission now."

"Understood."

June started the recorder and put it on the parapet. The red light made no magic. It meant neither could replace the fight with a kinder memory.

Traffic moved behind them. Before dawn, the river was black enough to erase both banks.

They chose the span because June wanted no institution's walls around the argument. A bus crossed and briefly drowned everything except the directional fact of Elias eight feet away.

He stood downriver where the bridge shaded him from the paling sky. Dawn would force him into the tubes soon. June did not care yet.

"Say what you did."

"I covered your mouth to stop you completing a sentence."

"Without permission."

"Did you believe I was incapable?"

"No."

"Confused?"

"No."

"Did you believe the attachment spoke instead of me?"

"No."

"That I did not know the five facts?"

"No."

Each no removed an excuse. He had not mistaken her condition. He had disagreed with her choice.

"Without permission."

"You decided my informed choice was less important than your legal judgment."

"Yes. I also believed the sentence would create false evidence of completion."

"Explanation, not correction."

"Yes."

"You could have spoken."

"Yes."

"Stepped between me and a recorder."

"Yes."

"Let me finish and filed the defects."

"Yes."

"You chose my mouth because it was fastest."

"Yes."

Elias did not reach for a distinction. "Yes."

"Wrong."

"Not merely procedurally wrong."

"No. I overrode your physical autonomy because I believed your speech dangerous. That belief gave me no authority over your body."

"Wanting the same words made it worse."

"Yes. I stopped a thing I wanted because I believed I knew when you were allowed to choose it."

June had needed him to understand the injury. Hearing it did not reduce it.

"Yes."

The agreement did not restore her body to the moment before his hand. It did not bring Milo back.

"Why stop me?"

"There are two answers. Neither excuses the act."

"Give both."

"I believed allowing the sentence would create evidence Constance could use despite the defects. And I believed that if you finished, I would answer as if witness and clause did not matter."

"You were afraid of yourself."

"Yes."

"So you controlled me."

"Yes."

"Completion requires an accepted witness. None exists. You also had clause three executing against you. Either defect would void the completion."

"Then let me finish and let the record reject it."

"I should have."

"Even if you answered?"

"I should have stepped away, stated my refusal to proceed without a witness, and let your speech remain yours."

"Would you have been able to?"

"I do not know."

The uncertainty was why boundaries had to exist before desire tested them.

"That was your right."

"What do you do now?"

"Accept revocation. Preserve this recording. Enter my act as a breach of your stated boundary. Submit to any ordinary complaint you make."

"And magically?"

"No supernatural remedy exists because your refusal needed no record to be valid. The absence of a magical penalty does not make the act less real."

"You keep saying I win."

"I am not awarding victory. I am agreeing you are right."

"Difference?"

"Victory suggests the argument repairs something. Agreement only prevents me defending the injury."

June looked at the river. "Good distinction. I hate it."

"You do."

"Nothing changes."

"No."

June gripped the wet rail. She had won the moral argument completely. Elias did not excuse himself, and the victory was useless.

"Was my consent real?"

"Your consent to speak was real. Your desire to answer was real. Your knowledge was sufficient."

"Then why invalid?"

"The vow is a supernatural obligation with strict formation conditions. An accepted witness must bear both halves. An executing clause makes you exposed and compellable even without a spoken command. Those defects prevent binding. They do not make your choice false."

"Law can reject an act without owning the person who attempted it."

"Yes."

"The Articles rarely remember that."

"No."

"Yes."

"Even under pressure?"

"You knew the five facts and spoke freely. But the clause executing against you makes valid completion impossible. Consent can be real while an institutional act remains invalid."

"Milo is in custody because I refused."

"Milo accepted surety. Delisle refused witnessing. Someone invoked clause three. An earlier hand lengthened the parish term to trigger a stay. The Hall preserved its charter. The Trust executed. You refused completion. Causation has more names than yours."

"Mine is still one."

"Yes. Do not turn one into only."

June wanted guilt because guilt could instruct her to pay. Shared causation demanded judgment.

"Do you apologize?" she asked.

"Yes. I am sorry I touched you without permission and stopped your speech."

"Do not add why."

"I will not."

"Do you expect forgiveness?"

"No."

"Will you use the recording against your office?"

"If your filing requires it, yes. If you want it preserved only, I will not choose its use."

"I decide whether the evidence leaves my custody."

"Yes."

June picked up the recorder. The file belonged to her because she had started it and named its purpose. She locked it before putting it in her pocket.

"Your invitation to my apartment remains absent," she said. "Your permission to stand near me is limited to this conversation. When it ends, you leave."

"Understood."

Accountability did not require intimacy as a reward. It required him to live inside the boundary after apology.

"Milo is in custody because the Articles placed consequence around your refusal. Do not improve their argument for them."

She looked at him. "What route remains?"

"Before I answer, do you want the route or every reason I withheld it until now?"

"The route first. Reasons after Milo is safe."

For once, he answered in the order asked.

"Tess Renn's case."

"Its theory died."

"The bad-faith theory died. The challenge to execution did not."

"Tess wanted forfeiture."

"Yes. Her preparation is useful and her concealment tells you whether to trust her. Neither changes the filings she assembled."

"What can they claim?"

"Clause two accepted surety through circular reliance. Clause three executed from an altered counterpart. Your standing was struck by the disputed consequence, creating injury to challenge both."

"Its evidence did not. A perfected forfeiture supplies injury. Constance's corrected surety procedure supplies admission that the prior construction was defective. The altered term supplies a record conflict."

"Tess cannot file."

"She can draft, argue, and serve as ordinary counsel. She cannot supply the supernatural standing required for an internal challenge."

"No."

"You cannot."

"My office is adverse and my exposure makes any filing vulnerable to compulsion."

"Constance?"

"Administers the instrument."

"Milo?"

"Custody cannot challenge the sequence through the office it created."

"Marta?"

"The Hall voted to honor it. She can join after a proper party files, not originate the bride's injury."

Every door had a person behind it and a rule preventing that person turning the handle.

"No."

"Who can?"

The sky behind Elias lightened. He moved one step into deeper shadow without approaching June.

"If you file, you elect to remain the named bride for the action."

"After standing was struck?"

"The injury preserves enough standing to contest the strike. It is the gap Tess prepared."

"Can I file and refuse the vow?"

"Absolutely. Filing preserves position, not completion."

"Can I seek release through it?"

"No. You would use the position to defeat the clauses."

June understood why Tess concealed the full case. Filing was choosing to remain the thing June had spent weeks escaping.

"How long?"

"Until disposition and every ordered correction enters both records."

"Can the filing itself bind me more deeply?"

"It can establish permanent party standing if you ask for relief that depends on it. It cannot complete the vow or initiate you."

"Tess's case asks for permanent standing."

"Yes."

"Then saving Milo may close my exit."

"From the bride position under the Articles, yes. Not from Elias Marrow."

He used his own full name to keep the two obligations separate.

"What is the filing deadline?"

"A narrow protective notice must enter before clause four can name a deed against Milo. Pratt estimates dawn, but the custodian may act sooner. The notice preserves the bride forum and stays new execution. It does not free Milo, defeat clause three, or elect permanent standing. The complete pleading is due at the second Trust docket closure after lodging."

"How long until dawn?"

"One hour and forty-six minutes."

June had enough time to preserve the forum without pretending she had read a hundred-page case. Not enough time to make the permanent election safely.

She called Tess on speaker. Tess read a one-page notice identifying the forfeiture, June's preserved injury, the disputed counterpart, and the request that no new deed issue before a complete bride-filed challenge could be reviewed. June struck every sentence implying that she had adopted Tess's full pleading or elected permanent standing.

Delisle joined the call from the parish house. He stated that he witnessed only June's authorization to lodge protective notice, not a vow, completion, or permanent election. June repeated the limit in her own words. Pratt received the notice for the Trust. Marta received it for the Hall. Delisle entered the parish copy.

Seven minutes before dawn, all three offices read back the same filing number. Clause four could not name Milo's deed before the second Trust docket closure after lodging. Clause three continued executing, and Milo remained in custody.

June had preserved the right to choose after reading the price. She had not yet chosen it.

"Do you want me to file the full action?" she asked.

"Yes."

"Because it saves Milo?"

"Yes."

"Because it keeps me bride?"

"That desire also exists. I do not offer it as a reason."

"If I refuse?"

"I will seek every remaining route that does not use your body or speech. I know of none."

He did not turn absence of alternatives into entitlement.

The first bus of the morning crossed behind them. Elias looked toward the stair descending to the tubes but did not leave before June ended the conversation.

"You may go," she said.

"Yes."

He stepped away without asking when he could see her again.

June remained at the rail with the river moving under her and the recorder heavy in her pocket. She had been right about her body, right about her consent, and right that Elias had crossed a line. None of those truths opened Milo's door.

Only the position she hated could do that.

Elias met her eyes across the distance she had required.

"The only thing left that could save Milo is a filing," he said. "I cannot make it. The only person with standing to file is the bride."
