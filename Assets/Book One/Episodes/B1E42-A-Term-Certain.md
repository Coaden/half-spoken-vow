# Episode Forty-Two: A Term Certain

Episode Forty-Two: A Term Certain

Delisle read the forfeiture date, and Elias interrupted with the only two words nobody expected from him.

"That is wrong."

Delisle did not stop. The parish office required a complete reading once begun.

His eyes lifted once toward Elias, then returned to the line. Interrupting a required presentation did not suspend it. The bell had marked commencement, June's refusal had already been entered, and every word that followed would become a fact she heard rather than an obligation she accepted.

Elias forced himself not to repeat the objection. A second interruption would create noise where June needed a clean record.

"A witnessed completion of the vow shall be entered before the accepted witness within the stated term. Failing completion, release, or lawful defeat by midnight on the nineteenth day, the named bride forfeits surety and standing under the sequence."

Delisle read the commencement date and counted nineteen calendar days aloud from June's personal hearing. He named midnight in Pittsburgh, not an abstract end of day. He read that illness, absence, institutional closure, and refusal did not pause the term. Refusal remained valid; consequence matured around it.

He read what forfeiture meant. The surety passed into custodial control for disposition under the next clause. Any provisional standing held by the bride under the Articles ceased, except the bodily authority to refuse completion. The institutions could remove recognition. They could not convert removal into speech.

June heard only the number.

Nineteen days.

Elias remembered nine because Constance had required him to memorize the sealed text before serving June. He had recited the term in a windowless room at Fourth Avenue until he could reproduce punctuation. Nine days had seemed designed to outrun review. Nineteen was not merely a different degree of cruelty. It was exactly the time produced when the authentic term met the Articles' ten-day protection for a disputed counterpart.

A joint tribunal needed notice, response, two institutional calendars, and an accepted neutral chair. Under ordinary procedure, ten days was the shortest lawful convening. The Articles supplied those ten days only if somebody timely challenged a material difference among counterparts.

Marta stood beside the parish register. Elias remained near the interior wall, away from the windows. Delisle completed the witness certification and set the copy down.

Rain softened the daylight but did not make it safe. Elias had mapped every reflection before entering and chosen a wall the windows could not reach. His injured forearm rested beneath fresh bandaging. June noticed it once and returned her attention to the page.

Marta stated for the Hall that June's refusal remained borne and witnessed. Elias stated for the Trust that required presentation did not constitute acceptance. Delisle entered both before closing the certification.

"Now," June said. "Why is it wrong?"

Elias answered from memory. "The sealed original says nine days."

"Exact wording."

He recited it. The sentences matched until the term. Nine in the original. Nineteen in the parish copy.

June made him begin three sentences earlier. Elias did, preserving every article and conjunction. Delisle followed with one finger on the parish text. Marta listened for whether confidence outran accuracy. Elias repeated the passage backward by sentence, a clerk's method for proving memory rather than narrative momentum.

The only disagreement remained the first syllable of the number.

Marta said, "The Local schedule says term certain without a number."

"The certified copy omitted custodial terms," Delisle said.

"Why?" June asked.

"The numeric term was sealed for the holder so neither side could time preparations around enforcement. The public schedule says only term certain."

"Then the original party copies should match each other."

"Yes."

"Does the Local possess a sealed schedule?"

Marta said, "Not in the Hall. Our acceptance refers to the original term without reproducing it."

The trustee record remembered nine. The member record remembered that a witnessed number existed. The neutral record displayed nineteen. Each system had preserved a different piece of the contradiction.

June put both versions into the recorder: Elias's remembered text and Delisle's spoken copy.

"Could your memory be wrong?" she asked.

"No."

"That is not evidence."

"I will take you to the original."

"The vault seal is broken."

Elias looked at her. He understood who had broken it and did not ask.

"Then you have seen the number."

"I saw nine."

Elias heard the trespass admission she did not make. He could have demanded when, how, and with whom. The question before them was the term. Motive could wait; forfeiture would not.

"Did you photograph it?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Is the image time-stamped?"

"Yes."

"Preserve the original file and metadata. Do not transmit it until we establish custody."

June's expression warned him not to command her.

"That is advice," he said. "You may refuse."

"I will preserve it. Cyd has a copy of the device backup, not the photograph itself."

Delisle examined the word through a magnifying glass. "The ink at the first character is darker."

Elias asked permission to touch the edge only. Delisle granted it. Elias did not place a finger on the ink.

"The paper was scraped," he said. "One stroke removed, the surface sized again, and the word rewritten."

The original word *nine* had been scraped across its full width and replaced with *nineteen*. Under the magnifier, fibers leaned in one direction where a blade had passed. The replacement letters sat over sizing that did not absorb ink like the surrounding paper.

"Could age cause that?" June asked.

"Age darkens and cracks. It does not align fibers beneath one word."

"Could the original scribe have corrected an error before execution?"

Delisle checked the seal overlap. "The fold and lower parish seal cross the edge of the repair. The repair predates this seal or the seal was lifted and replaced."

"Can you tell which?"

"Not here," Elias said. "A conservator could."

"When?" Marta asked.

"Not recently. The replacement ink has oxidized through the fibers."

"Range?" June asked.

"Older than any person in this room except me," Elias said. "Possibly near execution. Possibly decades later. I will not narrow it without analysis."

The unknown hand belonged to history, not necessarily the present adversary. Someone now had merely known which altered copy to wake.

June said, "Does alteration void the copy?"

Delisle read the custodial rule. A counterpart remained enforceable unless alteration changed the shared obligation without acceptance by both original parties.

"Did they accept nineteen?"

"There is no notation here."

Marta asked Delisle to search succession entries for any spoken acceptance incorporated by reference. He read each notation aloud. Custody transferred. Seals inspected. No term changed. No Local roll call or Trust filing ratified nineteen.

"Then the shared obligation remains nine," Elias said.

"And the parish counterpart remains evidence of a material difference," Delisle replied. "It cannot amend the clause. It can invoke the Articles' preservation rule if challenged in time."

Two accurate statements produced one cruel term and one narrow protection.

"I challenge it," June said. "The parish counterpart differs materially from the authenticated original in a number controlling forfeiture. I challenge before the authentic term matures."

Delisle made her identify both readings, the consequence, her standing, and the relief requested. June asked for the mandatory ten-calendar-day stay of forfeiture beginning when the ninth day would otherwise mature. She did not ask him to validate nineteen.

Marta bore the challenge for the Hall. Elias entered it for the Trust. Delisle certified it as timely.

The authentic term remained nine days. If nothing cured or defeated it, forfeiture would mature at that midnight and immediately be stayed for ten more calendar days while a joint forum examined the counterparts. The stay did not restart the term or make altered ink law. It preserved June's and Milo's pre-forfeiture standing through the nineteenth practical day.

"Earliest ordinary hearing?" June asked.

Marta named the Hall's notice period. Elias named the Trust's. Delisle added the parish requirement. The tenth day was the first uncontested date, exactly when the stay expired.

"No margin," June said.

"None," Elias answered. "Any challenge to the chair, service, or standing can consume it."

Elias opened the service notice Pratt had sent. "The Marrow Trust did not invoke clause three."

He read the Trust's denial in full. No office, trustee, human agent, or authorized contractor had transmitted the custodial notice. The denial did not cover a rival trust, a private person with the filing number, or someone acting without Trust authority.

"Can Constance deny personal knowledge?" June asked.

"Not through this paper. I will ask her."

Marta produced the Hall minutes. "Neither did Local Nine."

June turned to Delisle. "Did the parish?"

"The office responded to notice. It did not originate it."

An unknown party had activated the third holder at the precise moment surety made forfeiture useful.

The timing required access to both entries within hours of each other. Milo's acceptance entered the Hall minutes first and the Trust register eleven minutes later. The custodial notice was prepared before the public index updated the next morning.

Whoever sent it either watched both records or received notice from someone who did.

"Who knew the clause was mature?" June asked.

"Every party with access to the filed sequence," Elias said. "Including Kearse."

"And Tess."

Nobody accused either without evidence.

Tess knew custodial gaps from eleven years of litigation. Kearse possessed analysts, access, and an assignable claim. Constance knew the original but denied Trust invocation. Delisle held the copy and had delayed its use. Capability was not authorship.

June wrote each name under *access to fact*, not *sender*.

Delisle read both dates again so nobody could claim uncertainty. Nine days to the authentic term. Then ten days of mandatory stay because June had challenged the counterpart difference in time. At midnight on the nineteenth practical day, absent completion, release, lawful defeat, or joint disposition, Milo forfeited into custody and June lost provisional standing under the sequence.

June made each witness state what the deadline did not do. It did not revoke her refusal. It did not make symptoms consent. It did not authorize Elias to request the second half under pressure. It did not validate the altered number. The clock could mature a consequence, never an answer.

Elias entered his objection beside the parish certification. Marta entered the Hall's reservation. Delisle accepted both and certified the future stay without changing the term.

Three offices now agreed which number governed. They had also acquired ten days in which one procedural failure could still make knowing the answer useless.

Elias looked at the parish copy, then at June.

"The Trust did not invoke this," he said. "We do not know who did."

He checked the parish wording against his verbatim memory one final time.

The term in this copy was longer than the term in the sealed text, and the difference itself had become the only reason forfeiture would wait.
