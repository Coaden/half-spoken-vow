# Episode Twenty-Six: Election

Episode Twenty-Six: Election

June put Vera on speaker in the Hall records room and asked Yolanda to witness a refusal nobody had a form for.

"We have forms for refusals," Yolanda said.

"You have forms for members declining benefits. I'm not a member and this isn't a benefit."

"It would feel like one for about twenty minutes."

Vera's voice came through the phone. "A cut is not a benefit. It is commissioned damage with informed consent. Please record me accurately."

Yolanda drew the minute book closer. "I like her."

"Most people do initially," Vera said.

June had drafted three sentences on the bus and rejected all of them. *I choose the attachment* implied more than she meant. *I refuse severance* could be construed as protecting Elias. *I decline Vera Odell's offer* left another principal free to commission the work.

The fourth version prohibited the Hall and Trust by name. Yolanda rejected it because naming two institutions implied permission for everyone else. The fifth declared June the only competent principal. Vera rejected that because Elias possessed an equal bodily interest and could not be erased by June's wording.

"I don't want to erase his right," June said. "I want to prevent somebody using it against mine."

"Then say exactly that," Vera replied.

"If exact language were easy, neither of you would have jobs."

Yolanda uncapped her pen. "Mine also involves folding chairs."

"Read what you have," Yolanda said.

June read. "I, June Havlik, decline any cutting, severance, interruption, or removal of the incomplete attachment recorded between me and Elias Marrow. This refusal authorizes no completion, waives no objection to the Articles, and creates no duty toward Elias Marrow or the Marrow Trust. No person may commission the work on my behalf."

Vera said, "Add *at this time.*"

"That weakens it."

"It preserves your right to change your mind. A permanent refusal would bind future consent. I will not accept it."

Yolanda nodded. "She's right."

June added the words.

"Now define on my behalf," Vera said.

June frowned. "It means without my direct, current instruction."

"Put that into the sentence. A person can claim agency, guardianship, necessity, or prior permission."

Yolanda wrote while June dictated the revision. No guardian, relative, officeholder, surety, trustee, member, agent, or person claiming emergency authority could commission the cut for her. Her consent could not be inferred from silence, symptoms, incapacity, or a prior consultation.

"You forgot Elias," Yolanda said.

June had postponed him to the end because saying his name changed her pulse where a member could smell it.

"Including Elias Marrow," she said.

Yolanda's pen did not hesitate.

"What makes institutions honor this?" she asked.

"The Local records your spoken election because the attachment is borne," Yolanda said. "The Trust records Vera's declined commission because its consequence touches their register."

"I have no standing with the Local."

"You have a body. Elections concerning what may be done to it do not require membership."

That answer should have existed for the safe room.

June looked at her. Yolanda smelled the anger and did not evade it.

"The charter distinguishes bodily refusal from benefit eligibility," she said. "I didn't write it."

"You keep it."

"Yes."

"Then put that in the minute."

"The charter is already incorporated by reference."

"I don't want a reference. I want anyone reading this entry to hear that I did not borrow standing from a member to refuse."

Yolanda considered her, then wrote: *The speaker's lack of membership does not diminish bodily authority to refuse.*

"Read it back."

She did.

The sentence could not restore the safe-room nights June had been denied. It could prevent someone later claiming the denial had reduced her ownership of herself.

Both statements remained in the room.

June started the recorder. She stated her name and read the election. Yolanda repeated it verbatim. Vera identified herself and acknowledged the refusal.

Then Vera tested it.

"If Elias Marrow asks me to sever the attachment tomorrow, what is your answer?"

"No."

"If he is dying?"

The directional pull sharpened at the word. "No."

"If severance is the only way to protect him from the half-vow he spoke?"

"No one gets to remove something from my body as treatment for his choice."

"If the Articles order it?"

"The Articles don't supply my consent."

"If you are unconscious?"

"Silence is refusal."

Vera's voice warmed slightly. "That is sufficiently clear."

"I will not cut June Havlik's attachment at the commission of any other principal," Vera said. "I will accept no proxy consent, institutional instruction, inferred permission, or emergency petition. June Havlik may return in person and request new terms."

"Does my refusal protect Elias from somebody commissioning his half?" June asked.

"An attachment is mutual. There are not two halves to sever. Either person may seek a cut, but nobody may perform one on both while either refuses. Mr. Marrow remains free to ask and you remain free to refuse."

June had not considered that Elias could want the cut. The thought produced relief first and injury second. Neither was an instruction.

"If he asks, do you notify me?" June said.

"Before discussing terms."

"Do you tell him why I refused?"

"No. Your reason belongs to you unless you put it in the commission record."

Yolanda glanced at the empty line for supplementary statements. June left it empty. A reason spoken too early could become a problem someone else believed they had authority to solve.

Yolanda entered the election in the minutes and called the Trust office. Pratt answered on speaker. She read the entry. He asked for the recording, then read back the Trust notation he would file.

"Named bride elects preservation of the incomplete attachment at this time. Election conveys no consent to completion and no waiver under the Articles. Third-party commissions barred."

June stopped him. "Preservation sounds affirmative."

"It is the registry term."

"Use decline of severance."

Pratt paused. "That is less elegant."

"Good."

He amended the notation and read it again.

"Add that the refusal controls notwithstanding exposure," June said.

Pratt did not answer immediately. Paper moved at his end.

"Exposure is relevant to the Trust's interest," he said.

"Not to my authority."

"It may become relevant if Mr. Marrow is unable to act."

"Then it becomes more important that nobody mistake his emergency for my consent."

Vera said, "The cutter agrees."

Yolanda said, "The Hall agrees."

Pratt exhaled quietly. "The notation will distinguish interest from authority."

Three record keepers had discovered that June's no forced them into the same sentence.

Within forty minutes, both records held her refusal. The Hall's was witnessed in a bound ledger. The Trust's arrived by email with a filing stamp. Yolanda printed it and read it aloud beside her own entry.

The two versions did not classify June the same way. The Hall called her a person of member blood outside the roster. The Trust called her the named bride. Each institution recognized only the status it had given her, but both recognized the same no.

June made Yolanda read the entries side by side.

"Does either one say I preserve the attachment for Elias?"

"No."

"Does either one say I accept benefit from it?"

"The Trust notation says the attachment remains available to answer."

"That is a fact, not my election."

"It is a disputed fact," Vera said. "The attachment remains. Whether it is available to answer is an institutional interpretation."

June looked at Yolanda. "Put that objection in the Hall entry."

"Whose objection?"

"Mine. Vera's if she'll join it."

"I attest only that severance was declined," Vera said. "I do not litigate the Articles on a commission call."

"Mine, then."

Yolanda recorded that June disputed any inference that bodily preservation made the attachment available for legal performance. It would not stop Constance arguing the opposite. It would stop her saying nobody had objected.

Yolanda called Pratt again. He resisted removing the sentence because registers recorded consequence as well as speech. June required him to separate them into two paragraphs: her refusal first, institutional consequence second.

Pratt read the corrected entry. The distinction sounded small. It was the distance between June choosing what happened to her body and the Trust claiming she had chosen what the body meant.

"File that version," she said.

"Already filed."

The corrected notice arrived while they were still on speaker. June made Yolanda verify the timestamp.

"That's an election," she said.

"It's a refusal."

"Both can be true."

"What did I elect?"

"To leave the attachment in place when a lawful way to remove it was offered."

"A price I wouldn't make Cyd pay."

"The reason isn't in either entry."

June understood the consequence. Anyone reading the registers would see a bride who had declined an exit. Kearse could argue she accepted strategic benefit. Constance could call it evidence that completion remained possible. Elias could mistake it for a choice about him.

"Can I add the reason?"

"You can file a statement. It will not change the election."

June dictated one anyway. She refused because Vera's price required an uninvolved human to sacrifice a business day and public confidence. She made no judgment about future terms. Yolanda wrote every word.

"Do you want Elias named as a reason you declined?" Yolanda asked.

June answered too quickly. "No."

Yolanda smelled whatever followed and kept her expression neutral.

"Do you want it stated that he is not the reason?"

That would be another claim June could not make cleanly. She could refuse to buy freedom with Cyd's agency and still care what the cut did to Elias. Motive was not a ballot with one marked box.

"No," June said again. "The record gets the reason I can prove."

"That's not always the whole reason."

"You sound like a transcriptionist."

"Occupational contamination."

Yolanda read the supplementary statement aloud. June approved it without adding a word.

On the bus home, June's phone rang.

"Both notices cleared," Yolanda said. "The Trust noted it at ten thirty-eight. We entered it at ten forty-one."

"Why are you calling?"

"Because a woman from Kearse's trust requested inspection at eleven twelve."

June watched the Hall disappear behind wet glass.

"She asked for the election by filing number," Yolanda said. "They knew it was there within the hour."
