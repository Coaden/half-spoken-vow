# Episode Fifty: Selfishly

Episode Fifty: Selfishly

June revoked Elias's threshold invitation while he was standing inside her apartment and made him step back into the hall.

She had invited him in ten minutes earlier for the stated purpose of returning Tess's index and no Trust business. Cyd recorded the entry from the kitchen. Elias crossed the sill only after repeating the limit.

Then June revoked it before he reached the table.

This was not a trap. It was a test of whether authority in her dwelling operated when she used it against something she wanted.

"Invitation revoked," she said.

The threshold took hold. Elias moved backward without argument until both shoes rested beyond the sill.

The movement was not violent. Space became unavailable to him in the direction of the room. He could have resisted June physically before the rule completed; he did not. Once the words landed, remaining was no longer a choice his body could execute.

"Do you feel pain?" June asked.

"No. The interior ceases to be a place I can occupy."

"Can Constance's invitation through Cyd override mine?"

"Only Cyd can reissue her own invitation. Yours is revoked."

June looked at Cyd. "Do not invite him."

"Wasn't planning to."

For the first time, a supernatural rule answered June's speech instantly and did not require standing, witness, or a form.

Cyd watched from the kitchen with a wrench in one hand. She had been fixing the sink and refused to leave for a supernatural boundary demonstration in a home where she now resided three nights a week.

The pipe beneath the sink dripped into a stockpot at irregular intervals. Cyd had shut off the valve twice and called the landlord once. Ordinary water ignored every lineage institution equally.

"If this turns into a reconciliation scene, keep it out of the kitchen until I finish," she said. "I need to know whether emotional breakthroughs affect plumber access."

"They do not," June said.

"Best rule so far."

"Good," Cyd said. "Again, but with terms."

"Not again," June said. "Next is a new invitation. Revocation doesn't become rehearsal just because I intend to let him back."

Cyd nodded. "Better."

Elias remained in the hall, where a neighbor's television murmured behind another door. He did not ask June to hurry before the building woke.

June faced Elias across her own doorway.

"You watched me for two years under lawful authorizations and did not disclose personal interest. You spoke the vow to prevent a lethal clause and because you wanted me. You withheld the second reason because the first made you look better."

"Do you dispute any part?" June asked.

"No."

"Do you claim authorization made the watching harmless?"

"No. It made the presence lawful for its stated purpose. It did not authorize personal use."

"Do you claim rescue earns entry now?"

"No."

The questions were not punishment. They identified which facts the new invitation would not erase.

"Yes."

"You touched me without permission to stop my answer."

"Yes."

"You also stopped an invalid completion and gave me time I used to defeat clause one. Both readings are true. Neither cancels the other."

"You lost office protection refusing Kearse. You held the hillside boundary. You told me I owed nothing. Those are also true."

"Yes."

"They don't purchase touch."

"No."

"They don't make your first motive complete."

"No."

"And the wrong things don't make the useful things false."

Elias took longer. "No."

That was the agreement June had been avoiding because it denied her a simple verdict.

"Yes."

"Stop agreeing like a deposition witness."

"I do not know what alternative response you want."

"I don't want one. That's the point."

June had spent the month searching for the reading that would free her from every debt. If Elias was only a rescuer, gratitude threatened consent. If he was only a claimant, anger justified isolation. The evidence refused both conveniences.

She had done the same with institutions. If the Articles were fraud, exposing them would free her. If the Hall was cruel, leaving would make loneliness righteous. If Cyd were fragile, secrecy would be protection. If Milo were confused, overruling him would become care.

Every clean exit required flattening another person into the role June needed.

Chosen obligation was not the same as supernatural binding. Cyd's terms were ordinary and real. Kenneth's promise had survived without magic. Milo's office was terrible and still his choice. June could owe action without owing her body, love, or vow.

"You may enter under these terms," she said. "No touch without permission. No Trust business unless named before entry. No treating hospitality as progress toward the vow. Cyd can revoke the invitation independently because she lives here."

"One more," June said. "If I revoke again, you leave before asking why. Any conversation happens from the hall or later."

"Accepted."

"No. Ordinary agreement first. Does the threshold require the word accepted?"

"No."

"Then say yes like a person."

"Yes."

"I accept."

"Do you invite him?" June asked Cyd.

"No. Your invitation is enough for him to enter under your authority. I retain my separate right to revoke mine from last time, which remains revoked."

Elias confirmed the two invitation records did not merge. June's yes opened the threshold. Cyd's prior no remained hers.

Cyd pointed the wrench. "I tolerate him under your terms. If he becomes antique about anything, he leaves."

"Define antique," Elias said.

"You will know it when I point the wrench."

"That standard is not objectively administrable."

Cyd pointed the wrench.

Elias looked at June. "I understand the practical meaning."

The laugh surprised all three of them.

Elias stepped inside because June had reissued authority, not because harm had been forgiven into silence.

He stopped on the entry mat. "May I place the index on the table?"

"Yes."

"May I sit?"

June pointed to the chair nearest the door. Permission arrived act by act until familiarity could be trusted not to assume it.

He placed Tess's index on the table. "Milo's custody remains reviewable if the forfeiture is challenged by a living bride."

"I know."

"The challenge will preserve your status under the Articles."

"For the case or permanently?"

"Tess's requested relief seeks standing that cannot later be struck. Read the pleading before electing."

"If I refuse to file, what happens to Milo?"

"The protective notice holds clause four until the second Trust docket closure after lodging. If the complete action is not filed by then, a deed may be named immediately."

"If I complete the vow later?"

"Delisle's refusal prevents completion until a new accepted witness exists. Filing does not change that."

June would not choose under a falsely improved summary.

"I know that too."

Cyd shut off the sink valve. "Does preserve mean she never gets out?"

"It means the case requires her to remain the named party."

"After the case?"

"If she wins the permanent-standing relief, the record cannot strike her. She remains a party to six surviving clauses unless the tribunal limits relief differently."

"And if she loses?"

"Her present forfeiture remains. Milo remains in custody."

Cyd set the wrench down. "Those are appalling choices."

"Yes," June said. "They are still mine."

June looked at the phone.

The clean exit had been the misbelief beneath every plan. Cut the attachment, escape the Articles, protect Cyd, release Milo, owe nobody. Each attempted exit had transferred cost to someone else.

Even Vera's cleanest escape had required Cyd's business day. Self-surety had ignored that June possessed no independent standing. The basement had put sleeping neighbors above failing mortar. Silence had cost the agency eleven thousand dollars before June admitted the truth.

Exit was not immoral. Pretending it had no destination for the cost was.

"Selfishly," she said, "I want Milo home, the clauses struck, the vow still mine, and the evidence alive."

"I also want my work back. Kenneth protected. Cyd's agency solvent. My mother explained. Your motive accountable. None is the single pure reason I file."

Elias did not tell her which wants were legally available. She was naming desire, not drafting relief.

"That is not selfish," Elias said.

"Don't improve it. I am choosing an obligation because it gets me something I want. That doesn't make it a trap."

"What would make it a trap?" Cyd asked.

"If I couldn't refuse after hearing the price. If the filing promised an exit it actually closed. If Tess hid the permanent standing until after my name entered."

"She hid it until now."

"And I am going to make her read every line before I sign anything. Her concealment changes trust. It doesn't erase the tool."

She called Tess.

June put the call on speaker and told Tess Cyd and Elias were present. Tess objected to Elias hearing privileged strategy.

"He leaves before you read the case," June said. "Right now I am asking for access, not advice."

"Access under what role?"

"Potential plaintiff. Living bride. Person who has not elected standing yet."

The distinctions held.

"You understand reading the complete pleading may expose protected strategy?" Tess asked.

"I understand. I am requesting it."

"You understand I will not summarize away the parts that harm you?"

"That is the only version I will read."

"Then come alone."

June looked at Cyd. "Cyd drives me and waits outside unless I invite her into the legal discussion. Elias leaves now."

"Agreed," Cyd said.

Elias stood. He did not ask whether the reissued invitation allowed him to remain while June prepared. She had just named departure.

At the sill, he paused without crossing back toward her. "The index identifies every source Tess should possess. If one is absent, do not sign until she states why."

"Advice heard."

He left. The invitation remained valid, but authority to be present had ended for the night. June understood at last that opening a door once did not surrender control of every later crossing.

"What changed?" Tess asked.

"I stopped looking for a clean exit."

The answer surprised June too.

"That took long enough."

"I want the eleven-year case. All of it. Tonight."
