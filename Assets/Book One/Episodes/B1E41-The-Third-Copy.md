# Episode Forty-One: The Third Copy

Episode Forty-One: The Third Copy

Delisle unlocked the parish safe and made June say her name before he would unfold the third copy.

"June Elizabeth Havlik," she said. "Named party under the Articles."

"And you are here freely?"

"Yes."

"Did anyone compel, threaten, or promise you a benefit for coming?"

"No. You told me you had information concerning the third copy. I chose to hear it."

"Inspection does not accept any clause. Leaving before a reading begins is refusal. Once a required reading begins, my office must complete it whether you remain or not. Do you understand?"

June looked at the safe. "Yes. Has the required reading begun?"

"No."

He wrote that answer too. The care made the unopened document more dangerous.

He entered both answers in the parish register. The house beside the span had one office, one narrow kitchen, and a safe bolted into plaster beneath a framed print of the river. Rain moved against the windows.

The church stood a block uphill, but its custodial office occupied the parish house nearest the bridge. June could hear tires crossing wet steel and the river against stone. Neutral ground had not been chosen for beauty. Either lineage could reach the door without entering the other's institution.

A brass handbell sat beside the register. Delisle rang it once before turning the dial.

"What does that do?"

"Marks opening in the parish record. The bell can be heard from the kitchen and public walk. Custody is never exercised in secret, even when the page is."

Delisle opened the safe.

The third copy was smaller than the Local's, folded into a leather cover with a parish seal at each corner.

Unlike the bound original, it was designed to travel. The cover had a strap for carrying beneath a coat and a pocket holding succession entries. Every custodian had entered the date custody began, the condition of the seals, and the parish office received.

Priests died. The parish remained.

"Why does a church hold it?" June asked.

"The parish is the office. The custodian happened to be a priest in eighteen seventy-one. Both parties needed a holder neither could appoint or inherit."

"Why accept?"

Delisle found the first entry. "The custodian wrote that a peace nobody could enforce except the parties was only a promise to behave while convenient. He accepted so failure would have a witness with no property in either side."

"The parish owns this house."

"Ordinary property. No Trust guarantee or Local lease touches it."

"Who paid for custody?"

"Nobody. That was part of neutrality."

An unpaid duty surviving a century and a half sounded noble until June saw it also meant no institution had funded help carrying it.

"Your successor gets it?"

"The parish successor. Not my blood, not my choice."

"Can you choose the next pastor?"

"No. The diocese appoints. Ordinary church law transfers the parish, then the register recognizes the successor after the duty is read aloud."

"Could a trustee be appointed?"

"Not under current law."

"A member?"

"Membership would not disqualify a priest, but Local Nine could challenge neutrality. None has held it."

The holder was independent not because priests were purer, but because neither lineage controlled succession.

He read the custodial entry aloud. The Trust deposited one counterpart. Local Nine witnessed receipt. The parish accepted a duty to preserve, notify, and enforce if either original party failed to invoke a matured clause.

"Define enforce."

"Present the text. Convene recognized witnesses. Certify response or refusal. If a consequence matures without action by either side, enter it so neither can erase failure by mutual convenience."

"Can you compel speech?"

"No."

"Supply silence as acceptance?"

"No."

"Choose the consequence?"

"No. The copy names it."

The parish could force a clause into daylight. It could not force June to perform it.

"Independent standing," June said.

"Yes."

"Kearse wrote to you."

"He wrote to the office. I answered only the procedure."

"Why didn't you identify yourself?"

"The arrangement protects the holder from pressure until enforcement becomes necessary."

"Protected how?"

"The public schedule acknowledges a third counterpart without naming its office. The Trust and Local keep sealed directions that open only if custody fails. Neither gets routine reports."

"Yet Kearse found you."

"He found a parish office that answered procedure. I do not know whether he knew what I held."

"How did the answer reach him?"

"By hand. A parish volunteer waited while his secretary signed receipt. Kearse's people followed her onto one bus and then another. She lost them in a hospital lobby."

"He investigated the messenger."

"That is one name for it. It is also why this office remained unnamed."

"What did he ask?"

Delisle read the letter: whether a neutral holder could recognize a bride's appearance independently of membership or Trust office. He had answered yes and cited no document.

Kearse had tested for standing without naming the copy.

"And now?"

Delisle looked at the folded document. Fear came off him with illness and old incense.

"Now a clause has matured."

June asked to inspect the seals. Delisle made her read each witness line. The Trust's seal, the Local's open acknowledgment, and the parish acceptance formed three legs of the same record. This copy could do more than confirm history. It could act.

June photographed nothing. Delisle permitted notes but not images until formal presentation. She dictated seal descriptions into her recorder, then made him state her record was contemporaneous and not a parish certification.

"You are careful," he said.

"You are frightened."

"Both can be true."

The phrase had become contagious.

"Has anyone read it in ninety years?"

"The custodian reads it at succession. I read it twenty-three years ago. Nobody outside the office has inspected it since."

"Not Constance?"

"No."

"Marta?"

"No."

"Then neither side knows its wording."

"They know the certified schedule. They do not know every custodial notation."

"Why omit them?"

"So neither side can shape conduct around the holder's last-resort duty. A fire alarm fails if everyone knows how long the inspector waits."

"Or it lets the inspector invent a fire."

"Which is why the copy carries all three acceptances and why I do not amend it."

June placed her recorder on the desk. "Why tell me at the fish fry?"

"Because I received a notice seven days ago and delayed acting."

"Seven days from when?"

Delisle read the receipt entry. The envelope arrived by hand at four seventeen. A parish volunteer signed for ordinary mail. Delisle opened it after evening service and rang the custodial bell at eight twelve.

"You knew that night."

"I knew a trigger was claimed. I spent two days authenticating the paper, one requesting confirmation, and four failing to do what remained."

"Do not count verification as delay if required."

"It required one day. Six belong to me."

"From whom?"

"The office notice carries no sender. It cites the custodial trigger."

"You withheld it."

"For a week."

"Why?"

"Because I knew what the passage would do to your choices."

"You don't know me."

"I have witnessed six women under this instrument. I recognized the pattern."

"Did your delay consume my term?"

"No. Clause three begins only when you personally hear it and your answer or refusal is entered here. I breached the duty to present. I did not move the commencement date backward."

"Then you spent a week risking removal to avoid hearing yourself read."

Delisle closed his eyes. "Yes."

"Can you withhold it longer?"

"No. The delay is already a breach of my office."

"Consequence?"

"Removal if either institution petitions successfully. A successor would read in my place."

"Could you resign now?"

"The duty transfers and the reading continues."

"Destroy the copy?"

His fear became anger. "No."

"I needed that spoken."

"Then record that destruction is neither lawful defeat nor protection. It is tampering with the one record neither side controls."

June did.

He turned to a marked page but kept one hand over the text.

"Who petitioned you to invoke the clause?"

"I do not know the principal's name. The notice came through a recognized rival-trust docket. Its filing code certifies that the petitioner holds a claim capable of requesting custodial presentation. The Marrow Trust says it did not petition. Local Nine says it did not."

"Show me validation."

Delisle read the notice's chain without exposing the clause: a rival-trust petition number, the Trust sequence filing, Hall certification of Milo's surety, dates both entered, and the parish receipt code. Each could be checked independently. The sealed docket authenticated standing without disclosing the petitioner to the recipient. The petition requested an act; it could not invent maturity or change the text Delisle was required to read.

"So an anonymous person can make you look at a public fact."

"Yes. They cannot invent it."

"They can choose when you act."

"Only after maturity."

That was enough to weaponize timing without forging anything.

"What clause?"

Delisle's hand trembled once.

"Clause three."

June felt the next part waiting in the room.

"Read the custodial duty first."

He did. Once qualified notice was received, the holder must present the operative passage to the named party and both institutional witnesses. Delay breached the custodian's office, but the term began only upon personal hearing and entry of the bride's answer or refusal.

"Both witnesses aren't here."

"They are coming."

"Did you summon them before telling me?"

"I asked them to arrive after the inspection period. You could have left before the reading."

"And lost my chance to hear the page affecting me."

"Yes."

"That is technically an exit."

"I know."

Delisle had preserved refusal and arranged consequence around it, the distinction everyone kept asking June to accept.

Footsteps sounded on the porch. Elias first, unmistakable through the attachment. Another set behind him.

Marta's kinship reached June a moment later through the door. Two systems arriving in bodies she could identify before either knocked.

The parish bell sounded when the outer door opened. Delisle checked the clock and entered both arrival times.

"Do I have time to add a statement before you begin?" June asked.

"Yes."

She stated that her presence was inspection, not consent; that hearing a term did not make the term valid; that no deadline, surety consequence, institutional loss, or witnessed desire could supply her half of the vow. Delisle read it back. June corrected *unwillingness* to *refusal* because unwillingness sounded like a feeling somebody might cure.

Elias and Marta entered while the correction was being made. Each heard June's no before either heard what the clause demanded.

Delisle certified the statement, then rang the bell a second time. Inspection ended. Required presentation began.

Delisle uncovered the page.

"I am required to read something from it today," he said. "I have put it off for a week. I am sorry."
