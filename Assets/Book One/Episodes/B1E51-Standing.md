# Episode Fifty-One: Standing

Episode Fifty-One: Standing

Tess had eleven years of boxes open when June arrived and warned that the first page made escape impossible.

"Read it anyway," June said.

"Before you do, say what you think you are here to buy."

"Milo's release. Defeat of clauses one through three. Preservation of my refusal."

"Not escape?"

"No."

Tess watched for hesitation she could not smell. "Then page one."

Tess's apartment contained one chair not occupied by records. June took the floor. The first page named the proposed plaintiff: *the living bride presently subject to the Articles.* Every prior filing had substituted Tess, Lenora's estate, or an interested member. Each had failed.

Boxes filled the wall beneath covered windows. Each bore a disposition: *dismissed for standing*, *moot at death*, *unripe before forfeiture*, *estate lacks borne capacity*. Tess had turned rejection into an index of what the next filing could not be.

June started a recorder and named every open box. Tess disclosed that no privilege protected the dead files and that consultation was ordinary legal preparation, not supernatural obligation.

"Dead brides lack continuing injury," Tess said. "Sisters lack standing. Estates cannot hold a borne position. The case needs the person the sequence is acting on now."

"Why not Milo?"

"Surety has injury but no authority to administer the bride's side. He can join later."

"The Hall?"

"It accepted the instrument and voted to honor it."

"Elias?"

"Adverse office. Useful witness. Wrong plaintiff."

"You knew forfeiture perfected my injury."

"Yes."

"At the Hall, you said you did not send the notice or sign the petition. You did not say you had not helped Kearse. Say the rest before I read another page."

Tess put both hands on the nearest box. "I gave him the custodial theory. I told him a forced presentation would expose a counterpart conflict and weaken the Marrow Trust. I knew he held a rival-trust claim capable of petitioning. He chose to use it, found the parish route through his own people, and signed through his own docket."

"Your motive."

"I needed a living bride with a completed injury. Lenora had been dead eleven years, and every case I filed for her was dismissed."

"Did you know Milo would become surety?"

"Not when I approached Kearse. I knew clause two would have to produce someone before clause three matured. Once Milo accepted, I knew what the petition could cost him. I did not withdraw the theory or warn you."

"Because you thought the case justified our risk."

"Yes."

Tess had not forged a notice, possessed Article standing, or made Kearse act. She had done something less cleanly separable: shown a man who wanted the Trust weakened which machinery to start, while preserving her own ability to say his hand signed the page.

"You decided my risk was justified by Lenora," June said.

"Yes. I was wrong to make that decision without you. I am not sorry the case now exists. Both facts are true."

June entered the disclosure into her recorder. Trustworthiness and utility remained separate, but neither could now hide behind an unnamed sender.

June opened the precedent boxes in order.

Bride one established residence could be performed under the period definition. Bride two separated borne kinship from appointed office. Lenora's case showed the institutions had inferred consent across the gap. Constance's new margin corrected that inference. Delisle's copy proved the term conflict. Elias's recorded statement supplied officer knowledge.

June made a source grid. Each proposition needed one written fact and one borne witness or admission explaining why only one record existed. Clause one had the definition and Nadia's counted nights. Clause two had Milo's speech but circular certification. Clause three had two numbers and no joint acceptance.

"Your exhibits are by bride," June said. "The tribunal will hear clauses."

"Bride order proves pattern."

"Clause order wins relief. Pattern goes in background."

Tess bristled, then moved three boxes.

"The evidence is good," June said.

"Good enough to void the instrument."

Tess opened a sealed folder containing proof the original signatories accepted incompatible operative understandings while certifying shared text.

"Mutual failure of assent," Tess said. "Lead with this and the Articles never formed."

"The charter guarantee?"

"Falls with them."

"Buildings, benefits, peace?"

"Contestable."

"Then not our opening argument."

"It is the largest win."

"Largest is not the same as mine."

June marked it *alternative relief—do not lodge without election*.

"The theory was wrong."

"Bad faith isn't required. A sincere instrument can execute unlawfully."

"Courts like villains," Tess said.

"This forum isn't ordinary court. Constance will concede an error she can see. Marta will bear an argument she believes. Give them a way to strike applications without denying the peace they need."

"You are building relief they can survive."

"I am building relief Milo can survive."

Tess watched her. "Say the remedy."

"Strike clauses one through three as defective applications while preserving the underlying peace. Release surety. Restore my standing for purposes of the remaining sequence."

"Permanent or provisional?"

"Permanent as to party status, limited as to every other right."

"Say what it does not grant."

"No membership, initiation, benefit, Trust office, property, control over Elias, or completed bond. Standing to administer and challenge only."

Tess wrote the limits into the relief.

"Restore means keep."

"Keep standing, not obedient identity."

"The record calls you bride permanently."

"Then the record learns bride does not mean married, completed, or owned."

"Yes."

"The plaintiff defect?"

June read her own election declining the cut. It proved she had chosen to preserve the incomplete attachment at that time, but not that she elected permanent party status.

"We need a second election," she said. "I remain the named bride for purposes of prosecuting and administering the Articles. I waive no refusal and promise no completion."

"Add continuing notice."

"At what address?"

June chose Cyd's agency post box, not her dwelling. An institution could serve paper without learning where she slept.

"Counsel copy to Tess and personal copy to me. No constructive notice through Elias."

Tess included all three.

"Once filed, you cannot argue the Articles do not reach you."

"I can argue what they may do."

"Can you refuse remaining clauses?"

"Yes, subject to any valid consequence."

"Abandon the action to erase standing?"

"No."

"Completion erase it?"

"No. Bonded standing would coexist."

"Initiation?"

Tess found the construction. Bride-standing alone did not bar initiation. Completion would.

"You never get out by denying party status."

"That route already failed."

"A cutter remains," Tess said. "Release may remain. You choose not to use either as denial of jurisdiction."

"Correct. A later cut concerns attachment. I am giving up the claim the Articles never reach me."

Permanent party status did not make the incomplete bond permanent. It made June's relationship to the document permanent.

Tess pulled a blank sheet from the box. "If you file, my case lives because your injury lives. If you abandon the position later, every ruling dependent on standing can be attacked."

"Then the election must survive this case."

"Permanently."

Tess turned over the election sheet. "No signature until you state the consequence without my words."

June walked to the covered window. Elias remained elsewhere by direction. Milo was closer, held in the same house. Neither fact instructed her.

"If I sign, I become permanent party to challenge and administer the Articles. Six clauses can still address me. I can refuse, but cannot escape by saying I am not their party. This does not complete the vow, promise Elias anything, grant membership, or waive bodily refusal."

"What do you lose?"

"A clean jurisdictional exit."

"Gain?"

"A voice they cannot strike. A chance to bring Milo home."

June considered the word. Not completion. No immortal life, no directional certainty, no exclusive attachment, no lost initiation because of the vow. But permanent party status meant six remaining clauses and a record she could never call somebody else's problem.

"This is the third outcome," she said.

Not vow. Not forfeiture. Adverse administration by the person the Articles treated as an object. The drafters anticipated brides who complied, fled, died, or obtained release. They had not anticipated one who kept the title and contested every verb.

"It is not an exit."

"That's why the document didn't anticipate it. Every bride before me had to satisfy, get waived, or escape. What happens if the bride stays and becomes adverse administration?"

"The Trust argues she cannot be beneficiary and opponent," Tess said. "The Hall argues she lacks membership to speak for a line."

"And we answer?"

"She speaks for her own standing, which both systems recognized when they named her."

"Put that on page one."

Tess rewrote the standing allegation. The case no longer asked either institution to recognize a new person. It asked them to live with the person their own record had created.

Tess's mouth almost smiled.

"We find out."

She read the permanent consequences again. June repeated them in her own words. Remaining party did not require answering Elias. It did require keeping the case, the count, and every future clause in a record she could defend.

Tess read the risks she once hid. Milo could remain held if they lost. Kearse could intervene. The whole-instrument evidence might tempt the forum toward a result June opposed. Elias's testimony, if filed, would end his office. June initialed each only after restating it.

"Do the initials bind?"

"Human evidence of informed election. Delisle's witnessing makes the election part of the shared record."

"Then no signature here. I sign before him after the final filing is assembled."

Tess paused, then put the pen down. "Correct."

They spent another hour checking relief against consequence. Release Milo immediately. Restore his membership standing without pretending custody never happened. Strike the three applications, not the nine-clause compact. Preserve every objection and the count at six.

June removed a sentence asking the forum to protect her from future distress. No tribunal could promise that. She replaced it with notice, counsel, and a requirement that each surviving clause be proved under both records before execution.

"You are making this harder to win," Tess said.

"I am making the win mean what I can live with."

Tess read the revised caption aloud: June Havlik, living bride and permanent adverse administrator under the Articles. June changed *under* to *of*. Under suggested obedience. Of described relationship.

"The tribunal may call that cosmetic," Tess said.

"Then it can explain why it cares which preposition I use for my own standing."

Tess made the correction.

The boxes around them stopped looking like a mausoleum for failed cases. They became components June could choose, reject, and reorder. Tess had preserved them through grief. June supplied the living injury. Neither woman alone had possessed the case now waiting on the floor.

"One more time," Tess said. "If you win, you do not become free of the Articles. You become impossible for them to remove."

"I understand."

She also understood permanence was not passivity. Remaining in the record meant every future notice reached someone prepared to answer it. The title would stop being a corridor others moved her through and become a desk she refused to abandon.

That was a price she could name herself.

And keep.

"If the attachment is cut later, standing remains."

"I understand."

"If Elias leaves, standing remains."

June felt his direction beyond the covered window. "I understand."

"Do you understand this means you never get out?" Tess asked.

"Yes," June said. "Give me a pen."
