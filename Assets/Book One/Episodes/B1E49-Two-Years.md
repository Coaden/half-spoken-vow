# Episode Forty-Nine: Two Years

Episode Forty-Nine: Two Years

Elias played the first deposition recording on which he heard June correct a date nobody else noticed.

Her voice came through the small speaker, flat and professional. "Clarification for the record. The witness said March in the earlier answer and November in the present one."

Counsel objected to the interruption. June read both answers back. Counsel withdrew the objection.

The recording continued: paper shifted, somebody coughed, June asked the witness to spell a street name counsel had pronounced three ways. Nothing announced that another person would remember the minute for years.

Elias stopped playback.

"That was when?" June asked.

"Twenty-six months before the Articles named you."

"Date."

He gave it without a note.

"I was in the records room behind that wall," Elias said.

The room had no window and one grille connected to counsel's space. Trust authority let him hear testimony involving a building whose title passed through an office that could not appear in daylight. June's transcript became the public record his hidden presence could not.

"Could I hear you?"

"No."

"Know anyone was there?"

"The engagement letter disclosed institutional observation. It did not name me."

Valid notice without practical knowledge.

They remained in the Sewickley library. The recorder between them still held his confession from the previous night.

"Authorized observation?" June asked.

"Yes. The property belonged to the Trust. I was reviewing testimony concerning succession."

"And the other six depositions?"

He listed each authorization. Estates, property, management claims. He had never arranged her assignment, entered a room because she was present, or contacted her outside the work.

June made him identify the two he requested after seeing her name on the vendor calendar.

"Could someone else have observed?"

"Yes."

"Did you say you wanted to hear me?"

"No. I cited continuity."

"True?"

"And incomplete."

"Did it change my work?"

"No."

June entered the boundaries before allowing romance to improve the facts.

"You kept the recordings."

"The Trust kept them. I remembered."

"All of them?"

"Every word I heard."

"That is not flattering."

"I know. Memory becomes surveillance when one person cannot choose what remains in another."

"Can you forget?"

"Not deliberately. Details lose ease over decades. They do not disappear on command."

"What did restraint look like?"

"Not replaying recordings. Not using facts outside assignment. Not learning more. Failing, twice, by requesting proximity under a true operational reason."

That distinction was worse in a way paper could not be.

"Why watch me?"

"At the second deposition, counsel instructed you to replace a witness's phrase with cleaner language. You refused because the witness had not agreed. The client complained about efficiency. You preserved the ugly sentence."

"That is my job."

"Many call duty a job only while it costs nothing."

"You were evaluating me."

"At first. Then I anticipated what you would protect."

"Still evaluation."

"Yes. Desire does not improve the word."

"At first, because you were competent. Later, because you remained precise when precision cost you approval."

He played a second excerpt. An attorney asked June to go off the record while continuing to dictate. June's recorded voice said, "If words are being supplied to the witness, my hands remain on the keys."

Silence followed. Then the attorney agreed.

"You listened to me irritate people professionally."

"It was the first attraction I could defend."

"Others?"

"Your laugh when someone mistook the stenotype case for a sewing machine. The way you ate almonds while proofreading. The fact that you swore only after disconnecting from counsel."

June stared. "That last one is false."

"You believed the line had disconnected."

Despite herself, she laughed. It did not acquit him. It made the past human.

"That sounds polished."

"I have had two years to fail to improve it."

Elias explained the arithmetic he had avoided. A mortal life was not brief while someone lived it. Calling it brief was an immortal's excuse for treating another person's years as a doomed investment.

At one hundred and fifty-eight, two years felt administratively short. For June they were one-fifteenth of her adult life. A month consumed less of him than a day consumed of her in proportion. Longevity let delay disguise itself as caution because Elias expected time after every failure.

"Did you expect me to die before you acted?"

"I expected to preserve the option until circumstance became appropriate. That assumed your life would wait inside mine."

"It wouldn't."

"No."

"I wanted your attention and believed the ethical response was to do nothing," he said. "Doing nothing became easy because I could expect another year. You could not. I used longevity to call cowardice patience."

"Was doing nothing wrong?"

"No. You did not know me or request approach. The wrong was treating silence as evidence of superior care instead of fear."

"You separate acts from stories you told about them."

"The stories are where I gave myself innocence."

"Then the emergency made action necessary."

"It made action lawful. Desire made it immediate."

"And the attachment?"

"Made the act available to both records. It did not tell me to speak."

"If it attached to someone you did not want?"

"The interruption might still be lawful. I cannot prove I would have chosen it."

June did not rescue him from the distinction.

"What does wanting a mortal cost you?"

The question was deliberately selfish. June did not ask what his desire cost her. They had named that. She wanted what immortality did to love before love became promise.

"Nothing you owe."

"I asked what it costs."

"The knowledge that every delay consumes a different proportion of our lives. The temptation to call urgency romance. The certainty that if completion ever occurred, your aging would stop because of a sentence involving me, and I would never know whether you later experienced that as gift or theft."

"You could ask later."

"You could answer. Neither answer would undo completion."

"Permanence frightens you."

"Yes. Not because I doubt wanting you now. Because sincerity does not predict a century."

"Without completion?"

"I watch you age. If you permit me to stay, I eventually know you are dying because I stand near enough to see it. Then I continue."

He did not romanticize outliving her as devotion. It was a cost he feared and had no right to make her solve.

He had written those words before speaking them. Elias took a document from the folder beside his chair.

The pages were unsigned. Each paragraph carried a source and a margin note identifying Trust record, witnessed Hall statement, or Elias's observation. He separated memory from institutional proof more carefully than motive.

"Testimony," he said. "The clause executions, the Renn record, the corrected surety procedure, the Trust's reliance on incompatible definitions, and my observation that your filing preserves the peace while curing the defects."

"First line."

He read: "I, Elias Marrow, state against the interest of my office that execution of clauses one through three relied on constructions no single accepted record can independently prove."

"Last."

"I submit these facts knowing testimony against the instrument disqualifies me from continuing office and cannot be withdrawn after filing."

The pages named their price before asking June to use them.

"Give it to me."

"If I lodge it in support of your case, I testify against an instrument I am sworn to execute. I become permanently ineligible for office."

"Not merely removed?"

"Ineligible. I cannot receive another trustee title."

"What protection goes?"

"Authority over property, resistance to rival claims aimed at office, access to sealed records, and the title by which trustees recognize my speech."

"Housing?"

"Constance may permit me to remain. I would have no right through office."

The sacrifice changed shelter, work, authority, and which pages opened when he asked.

"Constance knows?"

"She knows it exists."

"What happens if you give it to her?"

"She may preserve it as internal advice and keep me in office."

"And Milo?"

"The case loses an adverse officeholder's testimony. It may proceed. Kearse can attack it as the bride and her allies interpreting their injury."

"Your statement makes the defect speak in a Trust voice."

"Yes."

"That is why it costs office."

"Yes."

June did not compel him. She did not ask him to prove desire through self-destruction.

"If you give it to me because you want me, I will not owe completion."

"I know."

"If you give it to Constance, that does not prove tonight was a lie."

"I know."

"I will be angry."

"That is not compulsion."

They had reached a decision neither could purify for the other.

The pages remained in his hand.

"When do you decide?"

"When the challenge requires it."

"The challenge requires it now."

"The filing can lodge without my testimony. Kearse's opposition will determine whether the adverse admission is necessary to survive."

"You are waiting to see if the price is required."

"Yes. Losing office unnecessarily would not make the filing stronger."

"And giving it to Constance?"

"Internal lodging would preserve the analysis while preventing later use as testimony against Trust interest. Once she accepts it in that capacity, I cannot honestly offer the same pages as an independent adverse statement."

"So the choice closes whichever destination loses."

"Yes."

June could not solve that choice by telling him sacrifice was romantic. Office was the structure of his life, not a token he could lay at her feet.

"That's late."

"Yes."

"Give me a copy now."

"A copy in your custody could be filed without my final election."

"Then read it onto my recorder."

"That would constitute testimony."

Every route that gave June the evidence also spent its price. Elias had designed no theatrical halfway measure.

He placed the testimony between them without surrendering possession.

The pages remained in his keeping for now.

"I have already written it for the filing," Elias said. "It will end my office permanently. I have not decided whether to give it to you or to Constance."
