# Episode Twenty-Seven: The Thing That Would Hurt Her

Episode Twenty-Seven: The Thing That Would Hurt Her

June arrived for the deposition to find her access badge dead and Sondra already typing at her machine.

The security light flashed red. Inside the glass room, counsel was swearing the witness. Sondra's hands moved over June's stenotype with the careful irritation of somebody driving another person's car.

Cyd came down the corridor fast.

"Don't hit the reader again. Security says your badge was deactivated at seven twelve."

"Why is Sondra on my machine?"

"Because the witness arrived early and I was not donating another client to surprise."

The security manager appeared with a printed notice. "Miss Havlik, your site access is suspended pending resolution of a reported conflict."

"Read the report."

"I can't release it."

"Then state the reason you're excluding me while my equipment is inside."

Cyd took out her phone and began recording. The manager noticed.

"The client was advised that your unresolved personal matter creates a risk of sudden unavailability and divided loyalty."

"Was the client advised I missed a session?"

"No."

"Delivered a late transcript?"

"Not according to the file."

"Disclosed confidential material?"

"No allegation of that kind was made."

"Then the reported risk is not based on my work."

The manager looked toward the conference room. "I don't make the determinations."

"But you're performing one. Did you review my performance history before deactivating the badge?"

Cyd held the phone steady. The manager understood the difference between a difficult worker and one manufacturing the record for a claim.

"No," he said. "I reviewed the consultant's recommendation."

It was not enough to reopen the door. It was enough to prevent the closed door becoming proof she had deserved it.

The phrase struck twice.

Sudden unavailability was generic. Divided loyalty was not. Elias had used it at Sewickley when June asked why the Trust cared about her employment. *Your ordinary life gives you loyalties the Articles cannot predict.*

Nobody else had heard him.

"Who advised you?"

"A risk consultant retained by ownership."

"Name."

"Kearse Advisory."

June asked when the report arrived, who authorized deactivation, and whether the underlying recording was in the building. The manager answered the first two and refused the third. Cyd recorded each refusal with the patience she used when counsel instructed a witness not to answer.

At eight fourteen, June's scheduled start time passed. Through the glass, the witness spelled his surname for Sondra. The case proceeded without June so efficiently that the sound felt like an argument against her existence.

Cyd's breath changed. "The man from the bridge."

June had told her only that much.

"I want the termination stated in writing," June said. "Not suspended access. If you are removing me from a scheduled assignment because of an anonymous lineage dispute, say it."

The manager frowned at the unfamiliar word. "I didn't say lineage."

"Correct. Put the words you did say on the page."

He left to call counsel. June stood outside the glass and listened to Sondra take a clean record. Her own machine made the same small key sounds under somebody else's hands.

"How much?" June asked.

Cyd named the day rate, copy estimate, and remaining calendar. Rent disappeared inside the total before she finished.

"That's only this site," Cyd added. "If ownership circulates the notice, I have three law firms that use this building and two insurers with the same security contractor."

"Pull my assignments at all five before they do."

"No. One client acted. I am not volunteering four more losses so you can call the damage contained."

June heard the echo of Cyd's accusation during the surety filing: handling it alone, which meant assigning everyone else their share without asking.

"Then flag them," June said. "If another badge changes, we know the notice traveled."

"Already done."

"Kearse promised not to use my information against employment."

"Did he promise not to use somebody else's?"

June looked at her.

Cyd swore once, quietly. "That's the gap, isn't it?"

"Yes."

Kearse had compelled Elias to name what would hurt her. The concession protected information June supplied. Elias had supplied this.

The manager returned with a letter and read it aloud because June required the spoken record. Site access revoked. All assignments reassigned. Reason: unacceptable risk of interruption arising from external obligations and divided loyalty to undisclosed parties.

"Does revoked mean permanent?" June asked.

"Until ownership determines the risk is resolved."

"What evidence would establish resolution?"

"The letter doesn't specify."

"Who receives evidence?"

He supplied the name and address of ownership's counsel. June made Cyd record both. A remedy nobody could describe was not a remedy, but an address gave her somewhere to force the question.

"Who used those exact words?" June asked.

"The consultant's report."

"Did the report identify its source?"

"A recorded interview with a party having direct knowledge."

Elias.

June signed only for receipt. Cyd retrieved her equipment after the session and made the manager witness that nothing had been altered. Sondra carried the machine to the elevator.

The manager offered a cardboard property box. June declined it and dictated an inventory instead: stenotype, power supply, two data cables, backup recorder, tripod, paper tray, paper, and the emergency protein bar in the side pocket. Cyd checked every item. Sondra read the machine's job count aloud before shutting it down.

Ordinary custody mattered. A supernatural dispute did not make a missing memory card less missing.

"I didn't take this from you," she said.

"I know."

"You smell like you don't."

June looked up.

Sondra's expression sharpened. "Figure of speech. You look furious."

The elevator arrived.

Back at the agency, June laid Kearse's concession beside the termination notice. Cyd read both aloud. The protection and the injury fit without contradiction.

June built a chronology on the whiteboard. Bridge bargain. Service on Elias. Recorded interview. Consultant report. Badge deactivation. She left a blank beside the interview.

"Why does the time matter?" Sondra asked.

"If Kearse questioned him before promising not to use my information, the concession was theater. If afterward, he designed the question around the bargain."

"Both sound bad."

"One is breach. The other is compliance. I need to know which weapon I'm holding."

Cyd preserved the badge notice with its email headers. Sondra exported the job log from June's machine. June wrote ownership's counsel requesting the report, source recording, decision standard, and every recipient of the risk notice.

She could not make the door open. She could make its closing expensive to misdescribe.

Sondra placed June's stenotype on the table. "The transcript is clean through the morning break. I used my own dictionary, so your machine has an untranslated job in memory."

"Thank you."

"Don't thank me like I carried you out of a fire. I did an assignment."

She named the practical consequences. Sondra now held two overlapping jobs. Another contractor would lose an expedited session to cover her. Copy delivery moved by one day. The client's security report would follow June to any vendor sharing the building.

"Can we challenge it?" Sondra asked.

Cyd said, "We can spend money asking a client to admit a security decision was pretext."

"So no."

"So not yet."

"We challenge the facts for free first," June said. "No missed assignments. No breach. No disclosed conflict. Make them state what conduct they're preventing."

Cyd watched her. "And if they say sudden unavailability?"

"We ask why every independent contractor isn't barred for being mortal."

Sondra smiled without humor. "There she is."

June read the phrase *divided loyalty* from the notice and then played a saved message from Elias in which he had used the same two words. Sondra heard the match but not its origin.

"The man in the corridor," she said.

"Yes."

"Did he call them?"

"He was forced to answer somebody who did."

Sondra waited for the sentence to become less impossible. It did not.

"That explanation is overdue," she said.

"I know."

"Knowing appears to be doing very little work around here."

"He kept his promise," Cyd said.

"Exactly."

"I hate this world."

"You know six percent of it."

"Then I am pacing myself."

June called Kearse. He answered warmly.

She started the recorder and told him. He consented before she asked her first question.

"When did you compel the source interview?"

"After our bridge meeting."

"Was the party Elias Marrow?"

"Yes."

"What did you ask?"

"What ordinary structure, if removed, would most impair your ability to resist the Articles without bodily harm."

June wrote the words down. Not what she loved. Not what she needed. What made resistance possible.

"The concession remains honored," he said after she read the notice. "No information you supplied was used."

"You compelled Elias."

"Yes."

"He named my work."

"He named your ordinary life. My advisers selected a point of lawful pressure."

"Why?"

"You need to understand that his exposure is not romantic sacrifice. It is an open channel into everything he knows."

"You could have explained that without taking my job."

"You understood the explanation on the bridge. You sold the interval anyway."

"Did you direct the client to remove me?"

"My advisers described the risk. Ownership chose its response."

"Did you know what response they were likely to choose?"

"Yes."

"Then don't rent their agency to clean your hands."

For once, his warmth disappeared. "Noted."

"Will you provide the source recording?"

"No."

"Will you preserve it?"

"It is already a trust record."

"That isn't an answer about preservation."

"I will preserve it through disposition of the Articles."

June wrote that promise into her chronology. Ordinary words were not supernatural obligations. They were still evidence.

The truth did not excuse him. It did prevent June from simplifying the injury.

"Restore the access."

"No."

"Then our alliance ends."

"It was one bargain, not an alliance. You were exact about that."

He ended the call after wishing her a good afternoon.

June stared at the two documents. Elias knew her lease, her work, Cyd, Milo, the safe room, Tess. Any party with standing could open him and take those facts. Protecting herself could no longer mean keeping distance from him. Distance left the information inside him and removed her chance to anticipate its use.

She had to protect the man who had claimed her without asking because hostile hands could reach her through his mouth.

She had no idea how.
