# Episode Forty-Five: The Shorter Term

Episode Forty-Five: The Shorter Term

Elias found the altered numeral by touch, where one ink stroke had been scraped away and rewritten.

Under magnification, the parish copy showed disturbed fibers beneath *nineteen.* The sealed original in the archive read *nine.*

Delisle had permitted the counterpart to travel only inside its leather cover, beneath two parish seals and with Elias named as temporary conservator. Pratt had transported it after sunset in a locked document case. Every threshold, hand, and opening time had been read into the custody log.

Now both terms lay six inches apart without touching. Elias read them aloud into separate recorders so no later transcript could make the discrepancy typographical.

"Original: nine days. Parish counterpart: nineteen days. All other words in the operative sentence correspond."

He compared ink oxidation, filing sequence, and the repair method against his private ledger. The alteration predated Lenora Renn. It predated Elias's first execution.

He began with chemistry. The replacement ink contained the same iron imbalance as a batch used in Trust annotations during a narrow span of years. Then paper. The sizing over the scrape matched material purchased after a flood damaged the archive workroom. Finally custody. The parish copy had been opened only three times during that span, each recorded in the succession pocket.

One opening corresponded to an inspection after a bride's first refusal.

Elias found the private ledger entry in his predecessor's hand: *Counterpart reviewed for duration of pressure; no institutional amendment sought.* The wording concealed an act without lying about the concern.

Constance stood across the archive table. "You recognize the hand."

"Yes."

The altered first *n* carried a slight rightward hook. So did the anonymous correction that preserved June's roster gap, visible in a certified facsimile Elias had reviewed years earlier without understanding its future importance. The same hand had touched two records decades apart—or someone had copied it well enough to fool a trustee who remembered ink better than faces.

He would not state certainty beyond evidence.

"Will you name it?"

"No."

"Because the identity is protected?"

"Because naming the hand would imply facts the documents do not yet prove. I recognize a correspondence. I do not possess lawful attribution."

"You possess memory."

"Memory is a record for me. It is not automatically a record the Hall accepts."

Constance could have used his exposure to force the name. Her refusal to do so was not mercy. It preserved a protected question she believed should mature later.

The hand connected to a protected record June's mother had touched. Naming it now would answer a question the available evidence could not yet support.

What Elias could prove was narrower. Someone with access to maternal-era records had known how to scrape one syllable without breaking the seal line. The same era produced the instruction protecting June from final exclusion. Protection and tampering touched the same edge. Why, by whom, and what else had moved remained unanswered.

Constance did not compel him.

"Motive," she said.

Elias read the earlier bride's disposition. Under the authentic nine-day term, forfeiture should have matured before a review forum could lawfully assemble. A material discrepancy challenge had stayed that consequence for ten days.

He read every act separately. The bride kept her room through the stay. Her surety retained membership. A sibling's assignment and an employer's access remained unchanged because pre-forfeiture standing had to be preserved while joint examination proceeded. None of that made the underlying pressure harmless. It made review physically possible.

The altered counterpart did not grant those protections. Its false nineteen created a material disagreement with the original nine, and the Articles themselves supplied the stay once somebody challenged in time.

Whoever changed the word had understood that distinction. They did not try to amend supernatural law with ink. They manufactured the conflict required to wake a safeguard already inside it.

"Protection," Elias said.

"Or delayed harm," Constance replied.

"Both. The hand selected ten days of preserved standing and review, knowing forfeiture would still arrive if the forum failed."

"Without the parties' acceptance."

"Yes."

"Do you admire it?"

"No."

"Do you condemn it?"

Elias looked at the two numbers. "I understand the choice. That is not acquittal."

"At a price."

"All protection in this instrument has one."

"And every unauthorized correction gives one person authority over which price another pays," Constance said.

"As the Articles do."

"The Articles were accepted by both institutions."

"Not by the bride now paying."

Constance did not answer because repetition would not settle the moral difference.

The alteration was old, deliberate, and unauthorized. It was also made to protect a bride, not harm one.

"Can we simply read nineteen?" he asked.

"Would you?"

"For June, the stay creates ten days to convene a tribunal. For the earlier bride, it preserved the same chance. A term cannot be classified apart from the procedure around it."

"Then do not mistake restoration for rescue."

"I do not. I mistake time for something June can use."

"We cannot read nineteen as law at all. The original nine is active. June's challenge stays forfeiture for ten days when nine would otherwise mature."

"Can the conservation finding support adjudication before the stay expires?"

"It can support the petition. It cannot prevent a proper challenge to the forum or enlarge the stay."

"Will you waive Trust notice?"

"If the Hall does."

"That saves days."

"Not Milo, unless June files the correct action."

Constance knew the route and withheld it only in the sense that she had not volunteered a strategy to the person opposing her. Elias entered the exchange in his notes for June.

"June must know the motive."

"Tell her what you can prove. Not the hand."

"If she asks whether the hand touches her mother's record?"

"Tell her that the evidence is insufficient."

"It is sufficient to state contact."

"Then state contact and protect attribution."

Constance's rule did not require Elias to lie. It required the uncertainty to remain the size of the evidence.

Elias carried the comparison through the river tubes, keeping both copies out of open air. At June's building the pull led him upstairs.

The document case struck his injured forearm at each stair. He held it with the other hand and rehearsed what he could say: protective purpose, unauthorized method, shared archival edge, no proven identity. June would hear the boundary as another institution protecting her mother's secrets. She would be right.

He reached the landing and smelled coffee he could not drink through the apartment door. Human voices moved inside. One belonged to Cyd. The other should not have been able to cross the threshold.

He stopped outside her apartment.

"June," he called. "Do I have permission to remain on the landing?"

"Yes," she answered from inside. "Not to enter."

The precision told him she was afraid.

Constance was already inside.

She sat in the chair nearest the door, far from every window, hands visible on June's small kitchen table. Her coat remained buttoned. An unopened folder lay before her like a promise nobody had accepted.

The domestic scale made her more alarming. Constance belonged among ledgers, staff, and inherited rooms. June's dish towel hung from the oven handle behind her.

The threshold had admitted her. June stood near the kitchen with fury and fear in equal measure.

"Who invited you?" Elias asked.

"Ask me," Cyd called from the back room.

"Did you invite Constance Marrow into this dwelling?"

Cyd appeared holding a phone and a copy of June's lease. "I did, after she told me what invitation meant and I told her it could be revoked. I recorded the whole thing."

"Did June authorize you?"

"I don't need June's authority to invite somebody into a place where I presently reside. That appears to be the problem."

From the back room, Cyd said, "I did. She said she had terms that might save Milo."

"Were the terms stated before invitation?" Elias asked.

"Only the subject. I decided hearing them was an ordinary consequence reaching me. New terms with June."

June's fury sharpened. "New terms did not authorize you to bring a trustee into my apartment."

"No," Cyd said. "My residency did. We can fight about my judgment after she leaves."

Cyd had not been tricked. That made the valid invitation harder to defeat.

Constance sat at June's table with an unopened folder.

"The invitation is valid," she said. "Ms. Toomey is presently residing here to protect the lease."

"June can revoke it," Elias said.

"June did not give it."

Cyd answered, "I can revoke it, and I will the second she departs or I decide this meeting is unsafe."

The rule followed the inviter, not the owner. June's lease could control Cyd through human law later. It could not retroactively make a supernatural threshold refuse someone a resident had admitted.

Elias had warned June that mercy was coming.

"Before you open that folder," June said, "state whether your offer requires completion."

"It does not require speech," Constance answered.

"Does it make completion the only practical way to protect Milo?"

"No. It offers a Trust concession in exchange for an act you are already entitled to take or refuse."

"Whose price?"

Constance looked at Elias. "Primarily his."

The burn beneath his bandage tightened as if the body understood before the office did.

"Then he hears it before I do," June said.

"That is why I waited after being admitted."

Constance had entered through Cyd's invitation and still declined to spend Elias outside his presence. Scrupulous restraint did not make the surrounding pressure kind. It made every route harder to reject as illegitimate.

Elias set the document case on the floor without entering. "The authentic shorter term remains active. Somebody wrote nineteen into the parish copy to create a discrepancy and trigger the Articles' own ten-day stay, not to amend the clause. The alteration was unauthorized. The hand touches a protected record associated with June's maternal line. I cannot prove attribution."

June absorbed the last sentence without asking the question he could not answer. For now, Milo's clock was louder.

He went to tell her why the shorter term still governed and found Constance already waiting at her door, invited in by someone who lived there.
