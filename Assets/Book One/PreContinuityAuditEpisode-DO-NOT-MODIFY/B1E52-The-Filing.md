# Episode Fifty-Two: The Filing

Episode Fifty-Two: The Filing

Pratt began closing the Trust docket while June was still dictating the final paragraph from Delisle's parish phone.

"The office closes at midnight," Pratt said through speaker. "It is eleven thirty-seven."

"Then stop narrating the clock."

"The clock is a jurisdictional fact."

"Then state it only when it changes something."

Pratt was silent for one useful second. "Agreed."

The parish phone sat on speaker between the register and a stack of charging cables. Rain struck the kitchen window. From Fourth Avenue came the sounds of file drawers closing one at a time as human staff ended the Trust's business day.

Tess spread exhibits across Delisle's desk. Delisle held the parish register open but had refused any role resembling vow witness.

June arranged the filing in clause order. Tess tried twice to return the bride-pattern exhibits to the front. June moved them back. Delisle wrote each change in the parish preparation log so nobody could later say the witness had certified a different packet.

"Page count," June said.

"Petition, nineteen," Tess answered. "Election, two. Exhibits, one hundred twelve. Index, six."

"One hundred thirty-nine total."

Pratt repeated the count from the speaker. Transmission would fail if his office received any other number.

"You are witnessing a filing," June told him. "Not completion, consent, or the vow."

"Does the filing contain the words of either half?" Delisle asked.

"Elias's first half appears in the deposition exhibit."

"Then my receipt could be mischaracterized as witnessing that speech."

June found the transcript page. "You witness attachment of a preexisting record, not the speech transcribed in it. Put the limitation beside the exhibit number."

Tess objected that annotations cluttered the certification.

"Clutter is cheaper than invalid completion," June said.

Delisle entered the limitation.

"State the distinction."

"You attest that I signed, understood the election language, and delivered a document. You do not validate any bond."

"Do I attest to truth of the petition?"

"No. Only identity, signature, delivery, and your own read-back of the election."

"Do I accept the requested relief?"

"No."

"Do I become counsel?"

"No."

"Then the duty remains one I did not refuse at the graves."

June heard relief in him and did not mistake it for eagerness.

Delisle read that limitation into his register. "I accept the narrower duty."

June dictated the election. She remained the living named bride and permanent party for administration and challenge. She answered no vow, waived no refusal, and accepted no clause consequence not lawfully proved.

She added the service address, the limit on constructive notice, and the negative grants: no membership, office, property, completion, obedience, or authority over Elias. Permanent standing meant standing only.

"State what happens if the attachment is later cut," Delisle said.

"Party status survives."

"If Elias abandons his half?"

"Party status survives."

"If Milo is released?"

"Party status survives."

The election did not borrow permanence from any person June might lose.

Tess supplied the exhibit list. The original residence definition. Rival objections. Renn disposition. Constance's corrected instruction. Parish term. Elias's statement held in reserve.

"Not filed?" Pratt asked when Tess named the reserve.

"Indexed but sealed pending opposition," June said.

"An indexed document not delivered is not an exhibit. Call it anticipated evidence or remove it."

"Anticipated evidence," Tess said.

"Source?"

June answered, "Unsigned testimony in Elias Marrow's custody."

The case could not pretend to possess a sacrifice Elias had not chosen.

At eleven forty-nine they transmitted the filing to Pratt. He read the caption, standing allegation, and requested remedy.

The scanner fed pages in batches of twenty. After each, Pratt read the last received line and Tess answered with the first line of the next batch. On page seventy-three, a staple shadow obscured a date. June rescanned it rather than argue legibility.

At eleven forty-six—before transmission began—Delisle had witnessed June's election separately. The time sat in the register, but the petition still described standing as relief created after filing.

"One defect," he said.

"Name it."

"You ask restoration of standing before the election creating continuing standing is entered. The remedy depends on a fact that becomes operative only after filing."

"The forfeiture struck existing standing," Tess said. "Restoration is proper."

"Temporary standing, yes. Permanent standing arises from election. Your prayer conflates them. Opposing counsel will say she lacked capacity to request the permanence that grants capacity."

"Circularity," June said.

"Precisely."

The same defect that had destroyed self-surety waited in a different suit.

"Make the election exhibit one and state it takes effect upon Delisle's witness, before delivery."

"Then ask restoration of provisional standing nunc pro tunc and recognition of already-effective permanent party status," Tess said.

Pratt replied, "Do not say nunc pro tunc in a hybrid forum unless you want the Hall to spend an hour asking why Latin smells dishonest. Say from the moment forfeiture entered."

June used the plain language.

"That creates the fact at eleven forty-six," Pratt said. "Yes."

Tess stared at the phone. "You're helping."

"I am preventing an excellent filing from failing for an avoidable sequencing error. Institutional loyalty does not require bad drafting."

"Does institutional loyalty permit you to help an adverse filer?" Tess asked.

"The Trust benefits when challenges reach merits instead of returning as preventable procedural grievances."

"And you enjoy correcting us."

"That fact is not dispositive."

June could hear his satisfaction through the speaker.

June corrected the paragraph. Delisle witnessed the election separately, then witnessed attachment of the remaining case. Tess read every exhibit number. Pratt received the final version at eleven fifty-eight.

"Page count?" Pratt asked.

"One hundred forty-one after correction and certification," June said.

"I have one hundred forty."

The room stopped.

Tess checked the scanner stack. Delisle checked his register. June followed page numbers until she found exhibit four's cover sheet stuck to the back of its final page.

"Missing page eighty-two. Transmitting now."

The scanner took six seconds. Pratt said nothing until the file arrived.

"One hundred forty-one. Complete at eleven fifty-eight and thirty-four seconds."

The clock had changed something. He stated it.

"Accepted," he said.

"Accepted for filing only," June said.

"Yes. No merits determined, no vow witnessed, no relief granted."

Delisle rang the parish bell once. Tess put both hands flat on the desk. For eleven years, her case had been boxes. Now it had a docket number.

The word changed the room. Milo's custody did not end, but it became challenged. The forfeiture could no longer complete silently.

Pratt assigned a docket number and read it aloud. He scheduled the joint hearing at the Hall because both registers had to recognize the forum.

"Emergency stay of clause four?" June asked.

"The filing prevents new execution until the hearing opens. Milo remains in custody under the already-entered order."

"Can counsel see him?"

"Yes. I will transmit access before closing."

"Time?"

"Eleven fifty-nine. That changes something."

Keys moved at his end. Milo's counsel access entered before midnight.

"Is the docket closed now?" she asked.

"Yes. Your filing is the final entry."

"Read the last three entries in order."

Pratt read Milo's custody, June's permanent election, and the challenge staying new execution. Loss, choice, action. The sequence had not reversed, but it had stopped moving without her.

Delisle closed the parish register and rang the bell. Tess stacked exhibits in docket order rather than bride order. June sent Cyd the filing number and one sentence: *Milo is still held. They cannot add a deed before the hearing.*

Cyd answered immediately: *That is not home, but it is not nothing.*

June checked the clock. Midnight passed. For the first time, a deadline ended with her document already inside it.

"When is the hearing?" Tess asked.

Pratt gave the time: nine in the evening, after the Hall could assemble and before daylight complicated Trust attendance. Both institutions had waived ordinary notice.

"Who presides?" June asked.

"No one alone. Delisle holds the custodial register. Marta bears the Hall response. Constance answers for the Trust. You control your case."

"Kearse?"

"He filed intervention while page eighty-two was transmitting."

Tess swore.

Pratt added, "His intervention is limited to rival-trust interest. It does not let him compel Elias during testimony without leave."

June assigned Tess to handle his objections. The filing was finished. Preparation began immediately, because acceptance was not relief and a docket number was not Milo walking through a door.

"Mr. Pratt," June said. "Why help me?"

"Because the distinction between witnessing a filing and witnessing a vow is sound. Because your election cures the defect Tess Renn has spent eleven years failing to cure. And because the Trust's affairs may soon require settlement by people who appreciate accurate records."

"What affairs?"

"That is not necessary to your filing."

"Your future office?"

"My present employment and possible future duties are ordinary interests I am permitted to hold."

Pratt had watched Elias approach permanent disqualification and Constance prepare succession without naming it. Helping June also demonstrated the skill a changing Trust might need.

"You want something."

"Yes. An ordinary favor considered later with full freedom to refuse. No deed named, no supernatural obligation created."

"You are careful."

"I work for Constance Marrow."

That explained the craft, not the choice.

"If I remember it, what do you believe memory buys you?" June asked.

"Nothing by itself. I hope it affects whether you hear a request later."

"Not whether I grant it."

"Correct."

June required him to state the limit for everyone on the call. The help created no lineage standing, office, priority, promise of repayment, or claim upon her vote. It did not make Pratt her counsel or Tess his ally. Gratitude could inform a later choice. It could not answer for June.

Tess and Delisle each gave their names as witnesses to that ordinary understanding. Pratt objected that the recital was unnecessary, then conceded that unnecessary clarity had become the evening's governing practice.

"Accurate," he said when June read the limit back.

The filing had ended one silent execution. Pratt was already looking toward the next settlement, where accuracy might preserve his employment or cost it. June could acknowledge the motive without turning it into a debt.

"I would like it noted that I helped. I would like you to remember it when the Trust's affairs are being settled."
