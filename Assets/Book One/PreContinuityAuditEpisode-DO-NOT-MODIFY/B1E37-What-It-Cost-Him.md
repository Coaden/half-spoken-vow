# Episode Thirty-Seven: What It Cost Him

Episode Thirty-Seven: What It Cost Him

Sunlight crossed Elias's sleeve while June was still changing back, and he counted the seconds instead of moving.

One. The lower path remained empty.

The service road beyond it did not. A human shape moved behind the parked utility truck, hidden from Kenneth by the rise. Elias could not tell whether the person belonged to the crew or had followed the sounds from the hillside.

Two. Kenneth reached June with the coat.

June had regained speech but not balance. Kenneth's attention narrowed to her. The upper gate remained latched and unwatched.

Three. Direct light penetrated wool and began destroying the skin beneath it.

The first sensation was not heat. It was absence: a line of flesh ceased to belong to his body before pain arrived to report the loss. Every story that called sunlight a burn made the destruction sound survivable by degree. There was no tolerant exposure. Light touched; tissue died.

At four seconds, the human stepped around the truck.

At five, Elias called, "Lower path closed. Turn back."

The person stopped.

At six seconds Elias stepped behind the retaining wall. His forearm smoked. The injury continued through shadow because direct sun did not merely heat a trustee. It initiated destruction, and destruction took time to stop.

He could have moved at one second. The plan required it. He could have called Kenneth at three and divided the safety they had designed. He could have entered June's boundary at five, using his body to turn her away from the stranger. Every available correction breached a different term.

He chose the breach that spent only him and knew that description was incomplete. If he died, his office would transfer, his half-vow would remain unresolved, and June would inherit consequences he could no longer explain. Self-damage was never entirely self-contained.

He pressed the arm against wet stone and listened uphill.

June said yes when Kenneth asked permission to approach. She spoke one complete word. The safety plan remained intact.

Elias stayed until she could walk.

The person below proved to be a utility worker retrieving a meter. Elias kept him on the street until Kenneth cleared the upper gate and June confirmed she could leave. The danger was ordinary and real. It was also smaller than Elias had judged it from thirty-four feet away.

He recorded that error before they left the hillside.

In the building basement, June sat wrapped in her coat while Kenneth filled a basin from the utility sink. Elias stood across the room. The burned sleeve had fused to his arm.

Shadow reached the basement through a covered areaway, but dawn pressed white against the glass blocks. Elias positioned himself where no reflected beam could find him. Kenneth hung a moving blanket over the opening without being asked.

"That is not an invitation upstairs," June said.

"I know."

Even injured, he did not let practical shelter blur a threshold.

"Take it off," June said.

"No."

"Why?"

"Removing it will take skin."

"That's an argument for doing it correctly, not leaving it there."

Kenneth put scissors beside Elias and backed away. June did not cross the room.

"May I help?" she asked.

The question struck harder than the light.

"No," he said. "Nadia will manage it."

"Nadia is across the county."

"Twenty-seven minutes."

"The cloth is retaining contamination."

"It is no longer hot."

"You know what I mean."

"Yes."

June looked toward Kenneth. He smelled the fear beneath her anger and did not translate it. The question had been asked. The answer remained no.

June accepted the refusal. "Then tell me why you stayed."

"Because you are entitled to the operational reason for a breach of your plan. You are not entitled to make treatment the price of hearing it."

June's mouth closed. "Fine. Operational reason first."

"The lower path was not clear."

"The man left before dawn."

"A second person approached from the service road."

"Kenneth didn't see anyone."

"He was above you."

"You could have called him."

"You were changing back. Moving his attention would have left the upper gate unchecked."

Every answer was factual and insufficient. June looked at his arm.

"Did you stay because of the attachment?"

"Partly."

"Because you wanted me?"

"Partly."

"Because the Trust needs me alive?"

"Partly."

"Choose one."

"I cannot."

"Cannot or won't?"

"Both, but not for the reason you mean. None existed alone long enough to become the answer. I saw a person approaching. I knew you were exposed. I wanted you alive. The Trust requires you alive. The attachment made distance painful. I chose while all were true."

"Which one decided it?"

"The person on the road."

"That's circumstance, not motive."

"Then I do not possess the clean account you want."

A selfless answer would have made a debt. A purely institutional one would have made anger easy. Elias gave her neither escape.

He would not convert mixed motive into clean sacrifice for her comfort.

"You owe me nothing," Elias said.

"You got burned protecting me."

"I chose to hold the boundary after the agreed departure time. You ordered me to leave. I did not. That breach belongs to me."

"You disobeyed my safety plan."

"Yes."

"Then don't make it noble."

"I am not. I made a judgment that another person approaching you was more dangerous than six seconds of light. I was wrong about the duration and right about the person."

"That is an unbearable answer."

"It is the accurate one."

"Your accuracy left you in direct sun."

"For six seconds."

"You say that as if seconds are small."

"They are measurable. Not small."

"Will the arm heal?"

"Most of it. Directly destroyed tissue will scar. Function depends on depth."

"What capability did you lose?"

Elias flexed his fingers. Two responded late. "I do not yet know."

Last night June had gained a boundary and Elias had lost the certainty of his right hand.

Kenneth left to bring the truck around. June and Elias remained with the failed chain on the floor between them.

"Last night I wanted to go toward you," June said.

"Your changed body moved toward a known direction."

"Don't reduce it for me."

"I am separating observable fact from your interpretation."

"I remember wanting."

"Then wanting is yours to name. It still did not authorize me to meet you."

"I know."

"Do you?"

June looked at him sharply.

"You are asking whether the movement meant something," he said. "It may. Meaning is not permission either."

The distinction hurt both of them. That did not make it cold.

"I know."

"That wasn't consent."

"No."

"It also wasn't nothing."

Elias looked at her. The desire she had begun reading in him was now returned in the sentence, but return did not make it safe.

"No," he said. "It was not nothing."

He used the scissors himself. The sleeve came away in sections. June did not look aside and did not move closer.

Fabric pulled dead skin with it. Elias stopped twice until his hand steadied. June narrated what he could not see: blackened margin, exposed red tissue, two fingers with delayed movement, no pulsing blood.

"May I slide the basin across the floor?"

"Yes."

She pushed it with her boot. He lowered the arm into cool water and the room acquired the mineral smell of damaged trustee blood. Kenneth's posture changed, member senses registering injury.

"Do you need me farther away?" Kenneth asked.

"No. Thank you for asking."

Need did not erase manners. Injury did not create access.

"Constance said completion could replace the Articles," he told her.

"That sounds like mercy."

"It would protect Milo, secure the Trust, and give you permanent standing. It would also bar your initiation, end my office, and bind five facts forever."

"I know the price."

"State it."

June did, one fact at a time. Direction and rough distance. Knowledge of dying. Her aging stopped but she remained killable. Neither could form another attachment. Permanent bonded standing. Then the forfeitures: no initiation for her, no office for him, no other binding oath for either.

"Does completion guarantee I stay with you?" Elias asked.

"No."

"Love you?"

"No."

"Obey you?"

"No."

"Does your knowledge make this a request?"

"No."

Only after she answered did he continue.

"More parties know you are considering it."

"I didn't say I was."

"No. Wanting speech and considering consequence are different facts. Others will conflate them because completion serves them."

"Including Constance."

"Especially Constance. She believes the result can end an instrument that has harmed people for generations. Her sincerity will make every sign of your desire look like a reason to arrange the choice more efficiently."

"And you?"

"My desire makes me less reliable, not more entitled."

"Could Kearse compel you to say I want it?"

"He could compel what I observed. He could not make my interpretation yours."

"Could the Hall enter what they smelled?"

"As witnessed feeling, yes. Not as speech."

"Could Constance use either to satisfy my half?"

"No. Nobody can. But they can use both to justify pressure they call assistance."

June folded the wet rag around his discarded sleeve without touching him. "Then we make a record first. I wanted proximity during the change. I did not consent to completion, request it, or authorize anyone to arrange it."

"I witness that statement."

"And you?"

"I want completion. I do not request your answer, and I authorize no pressure toward it."

Their statements did not cancel desire. They prevented somebody else entering desire as permission.

"You wanted to come toward me during the change. Kearse can compel what I observe. Members can smell what you feel. The private fact is becoming public evidence."

June's expression closed.

Elias wrapped his arm in a clean rag. "The vow's second half is worth more now than it was a month ago, to more people. Be careful who learns you have started wanting to say it."
