# Episode Forty-Six: Lawfully

Episode Forty-Six: Lawfully

Four hours before forfeiture, Constance opened the folder in June's apartment and offered the Trust's signature on an emergency-hearing waiver.

"No notice period," she said. "No objection to the conservation evidence. No challenge to June's appearance for the limited purpose of contesting the term."

June read from her side of the table. Elias remained on the landing because she had not invited him in. Cyd stood beside the door, ready to revoke Constance's invitation.

"Price," June said.

"Elias submits to examination as an exposed officeholder and waives objection to questions concerning his knowledge."

"Including the protected hand?"

"Including contact between records. He may state attribution is unproved."

Elias said through the doorway, "I accept."

June looked at him. "I didn't ask you."

"The price belongs primarily to me."

June called Marta. The Hall could waive notice only by emergency vote, and not enough members could assemble before midnight after three moon nights. Remote votes did not exist because the room could not smell a telephone.

The Trust's mercy solved one half of a joint procedure. The other remained a body problem.

June returned the folder. "Insufficient."

Constance did not argue. "Yes."

"You knew the Hall couldn't convene."

"I knew it was unlikely. I did not possess authority to make it certain."

Scrupulous, useless, and real.

Cyd revoked the invitation after Constance stood. Constance acknowledged revocation and crossed the threshold. On the landing, she handed the forfeiture schedule to Elias without touching him.

June spent the remaining hours testing every line.

At the named minute, Constance asked Milo whether he understood what surety meant and waited for his yes before taking him.

The Hall had arranged one chair for Milo inside the speaking circle and left every path to the door open. No restraints waited. No Trust guard stood near enough to imply force. Three hundred members filled the room because forfeiture altered their charter obligations, and every one could smell Milo's fear.

"I understand," Milo said.

"State it in your words," June said.

Constance waited.

Milo faced the room. "I accepted surety for June. The deadline ended without completion, release, or lawful defeat. My standing converts to custody. Going with the Trust does not mean I agree the altered deadline was valid."

Pratt added the reservation to the order.

The Hall clock marked midnight. Marta held the minutes. Pratt held the forfeiture order. June held every objection she had prepared and found no defect in the first sentence.

"Notice?" she asked.

Pratt read the service dates.

Parish presentation nine days earlier. Personal service on Milo within the hour. Hall notice entered before the roster locked. Trust order served after sunset and no later than permitted. June checked each date.

"Defect in the shortened term remains."

"Preserved for challenge," Pratt replied. "Not suspended."

"Authority?"

Delisle's parish certification was read.

He had not attended. His office was required to certify the term, not witness forfeiture. His absence prevented anyone using the event to reopen his vow refusal.

"Was anonymous invocation authenticated?"

"As notice of an existing fact, not a new obligation," Pratt said.

"Objection maintained."

"Entered."

"Opportunity to cure?"

No accepted witness existed. The record stated it.

"Opportunity for lawful defeat?"

Marta read the failed emergency-vote count. Pratt read the Trust waiver. June entered that relief failed because the Hall could not convene under its own rules.

No bad faith. Structures built to prevent rushed decisions had made a rushed consequence unstoppable.

"Milo can still refuse custody."

"He can refuse movement," Pratt clarified. "Conversion occurs regardless."

"Can he remain in Hall housing?"

"Not after custody names another location."

"Choose counsel?"

"Yes."

"Can counsel accompany him?"

"To the property, not into lawfully closed rooms."

"Contact me?"

"Unless a later clause names communication as interference. None does now."

June recorded each surviving freedom before anyone called custody total.

Marta said, "He can resist physically. That does not dissolve surety."

Milo looked at three hundred members whose charter would be challenged if the Hall obstructed a lawful execution.

His gaze reached Kenneth near the rear wall, Yolanda beside the minutes, then June. The room smelled of people hoping he would comply and hating what their hope required.

"If I refuse to walk," Milo asked, "what happens?"

Pratt answered, "Your refusal alone is not Hall obstruction. If members prevent execution, the Trust may petition against the charter."

Milo could force delay. The cost would be a new petition aimed at the bodies surrounding him.

"I will comply," he said.

"You don't have to make it easy," June told him.

"I'm not making it easy for them. I'm keeping the rooms open next month."

"That is still pressure," June said.

"Yes. Mine to judge."

"You can change your mind before the door."

"Yes."

Marta asked whether anyone had threatened Milo outside the stated consequence or promised improved standing. Nobody had. She made the room bear the difference between pressure and secret bargain.

Constance asked his permission before touching his sleeve. He gave it. Courtesy made every step worse because there was nothing June could expose as abuse.

"Why touch him?" June asked.

"To mark transfer from Hall presence to Trust custody," Constance said. "He may refuse contact and state transfer himself."

Milo withdrew his sleeve. "I state it myself."

Constance lowered her hand. "Accepted."

Even ceremonial touch remained optional when challenged. The outcome did not.

The surety converted into custody when Pratt read the final line. June felt the loss in the room's bodies. Milo remained her kin. His standing now answered to the Trust.

The change arrived as absence from Hall recognition. Members nearest Milo shifted as if a familiar door closed. His scent remained kin, fear, coffee, and soap. None restored his vote or benefits.

Marta called his name from the roster. Yolanda answered, "Removed to Articles custody, membership standing suspended, kinship unchanged."

The minute book received what every body knew.

"Where are you taking him?"

"Sewickley," Constance said. "He will be housed, fed, and allowed counsel."

"Room?"

Constance named it. Ground floor, no exterior window, ordinary lock operable from inside unless a later clause lawfully changed access. Milo could invite or revoke visitors while resident.

"That sounds humane," June said.

"It is intended to be."

"It is still custody."

"Yes."

"Release him."

"Defeat the clause."

Milo walked out between Pratt and Constance without restraints.

At the door he turned. "Call counsel before you call me. If they monitor one and not the other, I want the objection in the room first."

June nodded because speech had become unreliable.

Nobody applauded. Nobody called him brave. The Hall let the sound of his shoes crossing wood remain what the record carried.

June demanded a stay before the outer door shut. Pratt stated he lacked authority after conversion. Marta called for any member with a charter basis to object. Three hundred people remained silent, not because they approved, but because nobody could honestly name one.

Kenneth stepped forward from the back wall. His coming exile gave him no vote against an executed clause. He said, "I witness that Milo walked under protest to the altered term."

Yolanda entered it.

June added, "I witness that the Hall chose not to obstruct because its rooms and benefits were exposed."

Marta's fear sharpened. "Entered."

"I witness that Constance used no physical force."

"Entered."

The record would carry the whole loss: valid procedure, coercive surroundings, preserved objection, voluntary movement, no abuse capable of voiding the result. June could not rescue Milo by making any participant worse than they were.

The outer door closed. Through kinship she still knew Milo was alive. Through no magic at all, she knew he was afraid.

June found Elias in the corridor. The attachment struck hard at close range. There were no options left except the sentence only she could speak.

He held the document case in his injured hand, fingers stiff around the handle. She smelled no motive from him. She did not need scent to know he wanted her answer. He had said so.

"Is Delisle here?"

"No."

"Any accepted witness?"

"No."

"Is a clause executing against me?"

"Yes."

The answers should have stopped her. Instead they stripped the moment to the one act institutions could not supply.

"I am not doing this because Constance asked."

"I know."

"Not because the Hall voted."

"I know."

"Not because the attachment moved me."

"June—"

"Do not make my reason safer for yourself."

"I speak the second half," she said. "Before—"

She had chosen the words while Milo walked out. Not a sentence from a guide, but the answering form Elias had spoken first. She knew the five facts and every forfeiture. She knew no witness could validate it and meant to speak anyway because no institution would say she had waited for permission.

Elias crossed the distance and put his hand over her mouth.

His burned fingers did not close. His palm pressed across her lips for less than a second. It was enough.

The act stopped language, not intent. June meant the words. She had chosen them. His palm over her face turned rescue into violation before the sentence could finish.

She struck his arm away.

"Do not touch me."

Elias stepped back at once.

"There is no accepted witness," he said. "A clause is executing against you. Completion would be void."

"That was my sentence."

"Yes."

"You don't get to stop it with your hand."

"No."

Milo was already gone. Every procedure had been correct. June's freely chosen answer had no lawful place to land.

The Hall clock struck the minute after midnight. Nothing reset. No official announced a new remedy. June's unfinished words remained hers, and the corridor retained no power to make them enough.

Milo remained physically gone.

The answer remained hers, along with the fury of having chosen freely and been stopped.

Elias had put his hand over her mouth before she could finish it.
