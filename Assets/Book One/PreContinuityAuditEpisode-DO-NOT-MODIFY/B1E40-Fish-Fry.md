# Episode Forty: Fish Fry

Episode Forty: Fish Fry

A folding chair collapsed under June at the fish fry, and half the Hall argued over whose repair duty it had been before anyone helped her up.

"Maintenance committee," Yolanda said.

"Hospitality bought the chairs," Milo said.

"In nineteen ninety-eight. Maintenance has had twenty-eight years."

June sat on the floor with a paper plate intact in both hands. "I'm fine."

"Nobody asked," Yolanda said. "We're assigning blame."

Milo crouched over the broken rivet. "This was marked for disposal."

"Then hospitality put it back in circulation."

"Maintenance stacked it beside the good ones."

"Disposal means dumpster, not an interpretive pile near the mop sink."

June raised the plate. The fish had not shifted. "I want the minutes to reflect competent fall management."

Yolanda looked at the plate. "Motion recognized."

Milo helped June stand only after she held out a hand. Yolanda tied a red napkin around the chair so nobody could reinterpret it before dessert.

The Hall smelled of frying oil, coffee, wet coats, and three hundred people temporarily occupied by food instead of policy. Someone brought June another chair. Kenneth tested it before allowing her to sit, which restarted the committee argument.

He put one boot on the brace and leaned until the chair protested.

"If you break that one, it's your duty," Milo said.

"That's testing."

"Destructive testing is still destruction."

"Only if it fails."

Yolanda wrote *Kenneth inspected* on the red napkin, though it belonged to the first chair. "There. Now everyone is nervous."

For the first time since the vote, laughter moved across the Hall without turning away from June.

For one hour, nobody read a clause.

The room still ran on procedure. Tickets were counted, fryer shifts posted, allergy plates covered in blue foil, and the burial-fund jar kept separate from the cash box. But none of those records claimed June's body. They existed so three hundred dinners arrived hot and nobody with an allergy trusted the wrong spoon.

She had forgotten rules could be care made repeatable.

June worked the serving line beside Milo. He portioned fish badly and gave children too many fries. She collected tickets despite not being a member because the cash box did not care about standing.

Yolanda corrected Milo's scoop twice. The third time she replaced it with a smaller one.

"Authoritarian," Milo said.

"Solvent. We have forty-seven portions and sixty people pretending they haven't eaten."

June found six pieces hidden under the warming tray.

"Emergency reserve," Milo said.

"For what emergency?"

"Fish shortage."

"You caused the shortage."

"Then the reserve was farsighted."

June divided the pieces and made the count work. Milo slid two extra fries onto her plate as an unrecorded consulting fee.

"You look happy," she said.

"I have tartar sauce on my cuff."

"Those can coexist."

Milo glanced toward the surety notation posted by the office door. "I am happy."

"Because you're bound to answer for my defaults."

"Not because of the consequences. Because I chose something in front of everybody and nobody could call it a mistake I made by accident."

"They can call it a mistake."

"You do. Frequently."

"They can't call it an accident," June conceded.

"Before this, I was the cousin who leaked your plan and the member told to wait outside while important people corrected it. Surety is terrible. It is also the first office I chose that the room had to recognize."

"You had membership."

"Inherited before I could speak."

"You kept it. Paid dues. Showed up."

"That was choosing it later. This was choosing while everyone watched. I needed to know I could."

June disliked the answer because it belonged to him.

"It was still a mistake."

"Maybe. Mine."

June wanted him frightened enough to withdraw from a thing that no longer permitted withdrawal. Instead he had acquired purpose from the same office she experienced as hostage-taking.

"If enforcement comes, don't make it easy for them," she said.

"I talked to Yolanda about counsel."

"When?"

"This morning."

"You didn't tell me."

"I am telling you before the catastrophe. Cyd's rule is spreading."

Milo had found an advocate willing to review any executing clause before he performed a deed. The Articles could bind him. They did not require him to volunteer interpretation too.

"I won't."

"Don't protect me by staying quiet."

"I won't."

"And don't volunteer for anything else."

"That one feels targeted."

"It is."

"For clarity, does carrying coffee count?"

"Only if you declare an office."

Milo raised the pot. "I accept the temporary office of—"

June covered the lid. "Finish that and I pour it into your shoes."

"Refusal noted."

The language of offices belonged to a joke for once.

Yolanda pushed a tray between them. "Less family repair, more fish."

June served a member who had voted yes. The woman thanked her and passed a slice of cake back across the line. Practical kinship without apology. It did not erase the vote. It made the thing June was fighting for present enough to hurt.

Nobody asked her to forgive the roll call before accepting cake. Nobody required her to say the Hall was family. She served people who had chosen rooms over her argument, and they trusted her to count tickets without pretending the choice had not happened.

Belonging was not acquittal. Sometimes it was being handed the good spatula by someone who still thought you were wrong.

Kenneth sat alone near the wall. People greeted him, but nobody joined him. Exile had begun socially before the formal act. June carried him a plate.

She included coleslaw though he disliked it because the line had a policy against custom plates during rush. Kenneth regarded it as institutional retaliation.

"Stop looking tragic," he said. "It ruins the batter."

"I'm bringing fish."

"With a funeral face."

"Your hearing happened."

"Continued until after the moon window. They don't remove housing coverage the week somebody changes."

"Compassionate."

"Practical. Putting an angry member out before three nights creates repair bills."

"Can both be true?"

Kenneth tried the coleslaw and made the face of a man testing poison. "Frequently."

The borrowed word sounded enough like Elias that June nearly choked on a fry.

She sat beside him. The chair held.

Milo came over when the line slowed and divided the last piece of cake three ways. They talked about June's car failing inspection, Kenneth's garage heater, and whether Cyd could sue a building for having hostile doors. None of it advanced a case.

Kenneth said the car needed brakes, not legal theory. June argued the station had displayed prejudice against a completely adequate exhaust bracket. Milo offered to testify that the muffler remained attached for most of the drive.

"Most is not a vehicle standard," Kenneth said.

"It is a relationship standard," Milo replied.

June laughed hard enough to smell salt and fryer oil at once.

Across the room, Marta carried trays without approaching. She did not use hospitality to reopen the vote. June found herself grateful and refused to punish the gratitude merely because Marta remained wrong.

That was the gift.

Not innocence. Not restored membership. An hour in which June's kinship meant somebody knew which cake she preferred and no one treated the knowledge as jurisdiction.

She helped clear plates after the last serving. Yolanda let her reconcile tickets, then made her do it again after discovering Milo's emergency reserve had produced three undocumented portions.

"They were eaten," Milo said. "Documentation by disappearance."

"Trustee reasoning," Yolanda said.

"Cruel."

"Accurate."

The count balanced only after Milo admitted he had not collected tickets from two children and Kenneth. Yolanda entered three complimentary meals and made him initial the correction.

June watched the minute become harmless because the record named what happened.

Milo carried the broken chair toward the dumpster with the red napkin tied around one leg. Yolanda stopped him at the door.

"Photograph first."

"Why?"

"Maintenance will claim hospitality discarded evidence."

"It is a chair, not a homicide."

"Then it should be easy to document."

June photographed the failed rivet, the inventory stencil, and Milo's offended expression. Kenneth dictated that the metal had fatigued through age rather than sabotage. Yolanda entered disposal and made both men witness it.

"Now can I throw it away?" Milo asked.

"Yes."

"I withdraw my candidacy for coffee office."

"It never existed," June said.

"That's what failed institutions always say."

He carried the chair out, and three people applauded because nobody had applauded the vote.

June stayed to wipe tables. A member slid the burial jar toward her without comment, trusting her to count bills. June passed it back.

"I can count tickets. Fund custody belongs to an office I don't hold."

Yolanda accepted the limit. "Correct. Take the coins, then. They go to kitchen replacement, not benefits."

June counted forty-three dollars and eighteen cents in quarters, dimes, nickels, and one Canadian coin Milo denied contributing. Yolanda made him exchange it for an American quarter at a wildly unfavorable rate.

The Hall was ridiculous because it was alive. Its rules could exclude her, save a first-changing child, erase a person socially, and prevent twenty-three cents of kitchen money from becoming an accounting dispute. June wanted the good without consenting to the harm. For one hour, wanting both did not require her to solve either.

She put the balanced coin sheet beneath Yolanda's hand and accepted a second slice of cake without asking what it obligated her to become. It obligated her to carry a paper plate to the trash. Nothing more.

The coffee urn clicked empty. June stood to refill it.

Father Bram Delisle waited beside the supply table in his black shirt, looking more tired than any priest at a fish fry had a right to look.

"Miss Havlik," he said.

"Father."

"I need to tell you something before somebody else does."

Milo stopped behind her with the coffee can.

Delisle looked toward the crowded Hall, then back at June.

"It concerns the third copy."
