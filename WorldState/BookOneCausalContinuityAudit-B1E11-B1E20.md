# Book One Causal Continuity Audit — B1E11–B1E20

**Audit date:** 2026-08-21  
**Scope:** Finalized B1E11–B1E20 manuscript prose, with direct follow-up on the B1E01–B1E10 audit.
Later prose was consulted only when needed to determine whether a debt receives a planned payoff.  
**Mode:** Report only. No episode manuscript was modified.

## Overall verdict

The second ten episodes remain strong at the level of scene motivation and episode event. Tess's evidence
causes June to examine the Articles; the moon deadline causes the safe-room purchase; June's new literacy
changes her professional decisions; client interference causes Cyd's office policy; Milo's leak causes the
Hall reconstruction; clause two causes Elias's archive review; and June's need for a surety defense causes
the Kearse bargain.

The range also validates the need for a causal-continuity layer. Several episodes contain excellent local
prose while inheriting or creating global discontinuities: the Articles acquire a new drafting history,
June receives records and spends nights off page, a promised June–Elias conversation disappears at the
episode boundary, June knows an Elias-POV fact she was never told, and one bargain is executed a night
earlier than the fact June actually disclosed.

## Blocking and high-severity findings

### CON-006 — The opening recording moves from Grant Street to the Hall

- **B1E01:** June says `Clarification for the record` in the Grant Street deposition suite.
- **B1E11:** Tess plays that exact line, and June twice identifies it as a recording made `at the Hall`.
- **Effect:** The manuscript relocates the inciting incident to the wrong institution, recreating the same
  category of error previously corrected in CL-016.
- **Severity:** High location and source-record contradiction.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** Identify the recording as the Grant Street deposition record,
  or play an actual Hall statement if Tess's source is meant to be Hall surveillance.

### CON-007 — The Articles gain an incompatible drafting history

- **Locked canon, CL-001, B1E01, and the codex:** the Articles were executed in 1871 and contain exactly
  nine numbered clauses; the number never changes.
- **B1E11:** Tess says the Articles did not begin with nine clauses. Residence was added after bride one,
  surety after bride two, and later clauses accumulated through amendment and restatement.
- **B1E12:** Constance says she wrote every original line and accepted later constructions, preserving the
  idea of a later amendment trail.
- **Internal E11 arithmetic:** seven brides plus two pre-naming women are offered as nine origins, but
  Lenora, the seventh bride, causes a construction of clause two rather than a distinct seventh clause.
  The claimed one-clause-per-woman chain therefore still does not produce nine unique clauses.
- **Effect:** This changes what was signed in 1871, what June was named under, what `fixed sequence` means,
  and whether later language is amendment, construction, or original text.
- **Severity:** Blocking canon contradiction requiring an author decision.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** Choose one model: nine clauses executed in 1871 with later
  constructions created by bride precedents, or a smaller original instrument expanded by formal
  amendments. Then align E1, E11, E12, the codex, storyline, and ledger.

### CON-005 — Renn's bride number becomes an explicit character identity

E11 does not merely summarize Lenora as seventh. Tess identifies herself through the number, the title is
`The Seventh Sister`, the episode map repeats it, and later clause-two history depends on it. E9's item
order remains both numerically and chronologically reversed: Renn is item six in 2015, followed by Kovach
as item seven in 1992.

The apparent intended order remains Kovach sixth and Renn seventh, but no source was changed in report-only
mode.

### CON-008 — `First living bride in nineteen years` conflicts with Lenora's 2015 death

- Current story year: 2026.
- Lenora died in 2015, eleven years earlier.
- Tess says June is the first living bride in nineteen years.

Lenora necessarily remained a living bride until her 2015 death. The nineteen-year figure appears to have
been borrowed from attachment scarcity or Rose's roster gap. Unless `living bride` has a narrower technical
meaning not yet stated, the interval should be eleven years.

### CB-006 — The certified copy and third counted night occur off page

At B1E10's exit, June has spent two dramatized counted nights: B1E08 and B1E09, with B1E10 continuing before
dawn. In B1E11 Tess says June has spent three nights and made Marta produce the Local's certified copy in
eleven days. Neither the third night nor delivery of the book's central document occurs in prose.

Travel and routine nights can be compressed. Receipt of the Articles cannot: it creates knowledge,
evidence, possession, and future action. This is an accidental state jump unless a missing scene or explicit
retrospective bridge is supplied.

### CB-007 — Tess uses June's standing without obtaining authority

In E11 June agrees only to examine Tess's case and expressly states that examination creates no promise to
act. Forty minutes later, E12 says Tess requested certified amendment records `using Miss Havlik's
standing.` Pratt treats June's examination agreement as the basis.

Agreement to examine evidence does not by itself authorize Tess to file requests in June's name or exercise
her standing. This could intentionally show Tess overreaching, but Elias, Pratt, and Constance discuss it as
procedurally sufficient. The creating act for that authority is absent.

### CON-009 — The transcript's certifier changes from June to Pratt

- **B1E02 and CL-071:** June signs and certifies the transcript herself; that choice makes the event a
  record others can rely upon.
- **B1E12:** the transcript is described as `certified by Aloysius Pratt.`

Pratt may possess, order, or file the certified transcript, but the certification belongs to June. The E12
phrasing transfers one of June's decisive opening acts to counsel.

### CB-008 — E13's promised June–Elias scene never occurs

E13 ends with Elias arriving because he `need[s] to speak with [June] about Tess Renn.` June deliberately
reads fear and desire beneath his claim that the matter is entirely professional. E14 begins at the Hall on
the safe-room problem. No conversation, refusal, postponement, interruption, or later reference supplies
the missing bridge.

This is not ordinary scene compression. The omitted event would create knowledge about Tess, change the
June–Elias relationship, and pay the episode hook. It is an accidental gap.

### CON-010 — June receives Elias-POV knowledge she was never told

In B1E12, only Elias, Constance, and Pratt establish that Constance drafted the Articles. B1E11 tells June
only that `the Trust drafted.` The E13 June–Elias conversation is absent. Nevertheless, in June's E15
viewpoint she thinks: `Constance had proved it with nine clauses.`

June has not met Constance, has not been shown her authorship, and has no basis to attribute the clauses to
her. This is a direct world-bible/alternate-POV knowledge leak.

### CON-011 — The safe-room arithmetic is wrong

B1E14 gives June nine hundred forty dollars immediately before the purchase. She pays six hundred fifty.
The remainder is two hundred ninety, but the narration says the purchase leaves one hundred forty for
rent. No additional one-hundred-fifty-dollar expense is named.

Because rent and material scarcity are story stakes, the arithmetic must remain auditable.

### CON-012 — Milo changes from twenty-four to thirty

- **B1E03 and BookCodex:** Milo is twenty-four.
- **B1E17:** his fear smells young `despite his thirty years.`

This is a direct age contradiction in finalized prose.

### CON-013 — Kearse uses tomorrow night's fact tonight

In B1E20 June discloses that Elias will retrieve the clause file alone between eleven and eleven forty `the
following night.` She signs at ten fifty-seven. Elias enters Kearse's library at eleven twelve that same
night because Kearse has already used the information to serve him.

The disclosed interval and the performed service are separated by approximately twenty-four hours in the
language but fifteen minutes in the action. Either the fact should concern the current night, or the service
scene belongs on the following night.

## Medium-severity findings and ambiguities

### ED-005 — Tess's surveillance network knows more than its source can explain

By E11 Tess knows the residence argument, counted nights, Nadia's secret ledger, the client cancellation,
and June's unfulfilled private promise to Cyd. She also possesses audio from the deposition and says she
bought it. The breadth and speed of this intelligence are intentionally unsettling, but no source, method,
or payoff condition is currently recorded.

This should be tracked as explanation debt rather than treated as ordinary competence. In particular,
knowledge of Nadia's ledger and the Cyd promise requires access to private spaces or people.

### IG-004 — `Née Sabol` may misstate June's identity

The hostile client notice calls June `June Elizabeth Havlik, née Sabol`, while the prose calls Sabol her
mother's maiden name. `Née Sabol` ordinarily asserts that June herself was born Sabol. This may be the
caller's deliberate or ignorant error, but June reacts to its secrecy rather than its inaccuracy. Track the
distinction so the mistaken document does not silently become canon.

### IG-005 — Clause-two notice is coherent but its effective clock remains undated

E17 gives a valid causal trigger for the next clause: clause one has been entered satisfied, the required
interval will pass, and clause two will be presented at the Hall. E18 says the Hall will accept service
tomorrow evening. The procedure is causally clear, but the unresolved master calendar prevents placement
against the moon deadline.

### IG-006 — The Kearse property is declared non-residential by narration

E20 explains that Elias enters without invitation because the property belongs to the trust and is not
Kearse's dwelling. This is compatible with the threshold rule if nobody resides there. Because the location
is repeatedly called a house, later prose should preserve the office/non-dwelling fact and not assign a
resident without addressing the threshold consequence.

## Productive gaps that should remain intact

- The complete amendment trail and whether it proves bad faith. This is designed for the midpoint.
- Who supplied Tess with intelligence and what she will do with June's examination agreement, provided a
  real source and payoff are eventually assigned.
- Who is targeting June's clients through her maternal history.
- The identity of the third holder. Kearse proves an office answered without revealing the holder.
- Constance's competing aims: preserve the Trust, honor the Articles, and prevent another involuntary
  surety. E18 demonstrates all three without resolving them into secret benevolence or villainy.
- What Kearse compels Elias to answer after E20. The bargain creates this future consequence legitimately.

## Safe reader-inference and compression gaps

- **E10 to E11:** Tess has been seeking a living bride and contacts June for the diner meeting. The exact
  invitation may be omitted; the reason for the meeting is explicit.
- **E15 to E16:** a second client cancellation is an incoming event, not a missing decision.
- **E16 to E17:** Marta summons June to address the Trust filing and clause-two notice. The parking-lot
  opening and Marta's documents support one dominant inference.
- **E17 to E18:** clause two is coming, so Elias must prepare the reading and revisit the Renn execution.
- **E18 to E19:** June needs a party with standing and a surety argument, while Nadia has already taught her
  the usefulness strategy. Meeting Kearse follows from her current state even though first contact is
  compressed.
- **E19 to E20:** the bridge terms expressly defer the exchange until June can inspect the objections and
  the third-holder reply. E20 is the scheduled performance of that contingent bargain.

## Obligation changes through B1E20

- **June–Tess examination arrangement:** June agrees to examine, creates no promise to file, requires a
  neutral address, and bars contact with Cyd. Tess's later use of June's standing exceeds the authority
  expressly named.
- **Elias's knowledge condition:** In E12 the Trust accepts a written condition that no petition settled
  with Elias's cooperation may act upon June without her knowledge. It deliberately requires knowledge,
  not consent; June's refusal remains independently absolute.
- **Safe-room purchase:** June pays six hundred fifty dollars for room six and three dates under a human
  receipt. Local Nine has not accepted the transfer. Physical access and institutional entitlement remain
  separate.
- **June–Kenneth conditional return:** Kenneth will investigate the seller's actual plan; if he has nowhere
  safe, June will return the slot and waive repayment. This ordinary promise remains active after E14.
- **June–Cyd disclosure:** June renews the promise in E13 and knowingly violates it in E15. The relationship
  debt becomes explicit rather than forgotten.
- **June–Milo thirty-day restriction:** Milo voluntarily agrees not to repeat information about June unless
  she is present and to reconstruct earlier disclosures. The Hall records it as a voluntary human statement,
  not discipline or supernatural obligation.
- **Clause two surety:** Notice is served in E17. The clause demands a member in good standing of June's
  borne line who accepts the office before both parties' witnesses. Milo is the only apparent candidate.
- **Kearse bargain:** In exchange for one identified scheduling fact, Kearse grants access to objections,
  notice before asserting standing, protections concerning June's residence and employment, and limits on
  the fact's reuse. June performs; Kearse performs access and uses the fact for one service. Continuing
  notice/confidentiality duties remain active.

## Character knowledge and belief movement

### June

- **E11:** learns that clause language has a traceable history and agrees only to examine Tess's case.
  She does not authorize filing or promise action.
- **E13:** deliberately names and tests her lifelong borne-record literacy. She learns scent supplies a
  direction for questioning, not testimony or intent.
- **E14:** learns no Local process can house an uninitiated wolf and chooses an ethically compromised human
  transaction, then admits Kenneth into the consequence.
- **E15–E16:** proves the literacy can restore work, then chooses secrecy despite Cyd's material sacrifice.
  She also learns the client attack reaches back toward her mother.
- **E17:** reconstructs how spoken information crossed into a written Trust filing and chooses human repair
  over institutional discipline for Milo.
- **E19–E20:** learns how rival-trust interests can produce useful evidence, but underestimates the value of
  procedural location data and materially exposes Elias.

### Elias

- **E12:** learns Constance considers the attachment essential to the Trust's survival and that Tess has
  entered the matter. He turns a private protective desire into a written knowledge condition.
- **E18:** openly acknowledges his unauthorized ledger, changes his interpretation of the Renn execution
  from exact performance to exact wrongdoing, and helps create evidence that can prevent repetition.
- **E20:** learns June traded his vulnerable interval to Kearse. His lack of surprise follows from his prior
  warning that usefulness carries a price; the relationship injury is nevertheless new.

### Cyd

- **E13–E16:** knows June is concealing something connected to the Hall, Trust, her landlord, and her mother.
  She chooses June professionally while refusing to call the secrecy harmless.

### Tess

- **E11:** knows extensive private facts about June, needs her living standing, and accepts a narrow
  examination agreement instead of the filing promise she wants.
- **E12:** acts as though the examination agreement permits use of June's standing, which is the disputed
  permission gap in CB-007.

## Proposed repair order

No repair was applied. If the author later authorizes revision, the efficient order is:

1. Decide the Articles' drafting model and bride order, because both control multiple episodes and canon
   files.
2. Lock the calendar from naming through clause two and the moon.
3. Supply the missing distinction between vow freedom and Article liability, plus Pratt's satisfaction
   authority.
4. Resolve June's first-change history.
5. Restore the missing E13 June–Elias scene or explicitly prevent/postpone it.
6. Remove the E15 knowledge leak.
7. Correct local facts: deposition versus Hall recording, transcript certifier, safe-room arithmetic,
   Milo's age, and the E20 night.
8. Assign sources and payoff conditions to Tess's intelligence and the prepared-room mystery.

## Audit conclusion

The range advances character and institution exceptionally well when it stays inside a scene. Its most
successful continuity work is E17 and E18: both distinguish human promises from supernatural obligation,
and both show how the two record systems can produce a wrong result without either side secretly lying.
The highest-risk failures occur at episode boundaries and viewpoint boundaries—the exact places an
independently drafted serial is most likely to teleport state.
