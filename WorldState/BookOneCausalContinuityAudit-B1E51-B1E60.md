# Book One Causal Continuity Audit — B1E51–B1E60

Audit date: 2026-08-21  
Mode: report only; no episode prose changed  
Authority: locked canon and `BookCodex.json`, finalized episode prose, `ContinuityLedger.json`, then the episode map and storyline

## Overall verdict

The final ten episodes deliver a genuine Book One ending. June knowingly elects permanent adverse-party
standing, files and wins narrow relief, releases Milo, defeats the first three applications without
destroying the peace, records the individual prices, restores ordinary work and housing, and sets a
bounded alliance with Elias. The mother warning and unreported cut arrive only after that resolution and
operate as forward stings rather than substitutes for an ending.

The ending nevertheless inherits the unresolved pre-dawn filing gap from Episode Forty-Seven and adds
three repair needs: the precise authority and effect of “striking” an immutable original clause must be
defined; Kenneth's permanent exile occurs without the postponed disciplinary hearing ever resuming; and
Episode Fifty-Five says both records already say six while Episode Fifty-Eight opens because the Hall
ledger still says nine. Several Book-level mysteries from earlier blocks also remain unpaid at Episode
Sixty, most importantly the sender who activated clause three.

## Blocking and high-priority findings

### CON-021 — The filing occurs at midnight after a pre-dawn deadline

Episode Forty-Seven leaves one hour and forty-six minutes before dawn and says the action must be filed
before clause four names Milo's deed. Episode Fifty-One spends another hour drafting before June has even
signed; Episode Fifty-Two files at eleven fifty-eight at night. No same-day calendar can contain both
states, and no intervening stay or delayed deed is entered before filing.

Minimum repair: lodge an authorized protective filing before the Episode Forty-Seven deadline, then
amend or perfect it at eleven fifty-eight; or move the Episode Forty-Eight through Fifty-One material
before the stated countdown begins. This is the same missing bridge recorded as CB-015, now confirmed by
the filing timestamp.

### CON-022 — Both records say six, then the Hall ledger still says nine

Episode Fifty-Five states that “nine became six in both records” after Marta's Hall acknowledgment and
Constance's Trust notation. Episode Fifty-Eight opens because the Trust register says six while the Hall
ledger still says nine in three places. A hearing minute and the master sequence ledger can differ, but
the prose currently calls both states the Hall record without naming that distinction.

Minimum repair: specify in Episode Fifty-Five that the joint disposition and hearing minutes carry six
while the Hall's master ledger and annual cross-references still await ministerial reconciliation. Then
Episode Fifty-Eight performs that reconciliation rather than reversing an accomplished fact.

### CB-016 — Kenneth is exiled without the postponed discipline process

Episode Thirty-Three schedules Kenneth's discipline and Episode Forty delays it until after the moon
window. No resumed notice, charges, hearing, opportunity to answer, vote, or ruling appears before Episode
Fifty-Six declares that his continuing breach “required permanent exile.” The episode preserves the
important distinction that dissent alone is protected, but the institutional act removing membership
has not been causally created.

Minimum repair: resume the hearing before or during Episode Fifty-Six, name the charged post-vote acts,
let Kenneth answer, and record the authorized decision. Do not punish his lone no vote; the finalized
prose correctly grounds discipline in continuing conduct after the vote.

### Institutional mechanism — defeating without amending the Articles

Episodes Fifty-Three through Fifty-Eight mostly support the intended distinction: the visible nine-clause
text remains, adjudication is entered beneath it, and six clauses remain operative. However, phrases such
as “the count changed from nine,” “removed permanently,” and a future forum “draft[ing] a lawful
replacement” can sound as if the original instrument itself has been rewritten.

Under the locked rule, the clean model is: all nine clauses remain historically and textually present;
accepted joint adjudication defeats clauses one through three on their own terms and permanently bars
their future execution; the operative count is six; any future rule must be a separate instrument
accepted by both institutions and cannot replace text inside the 1871 Articles. The authority for the
joint forum to impose that non-execution status should be anchored in an original severability/defeat
provision, not in a later construction.

### Book-level explanation debts still open at closure

The protected mother and severed-attachment mysteries may properly continue. These other open Book
debts are not among the protected three and should be paid or expressly reclassified by author decision:

- **ED-001:** why June's first change occurs at thirty-one and what she knew before Episode Three;
- **ED-002:** the exact stated price of Elias formally abandoning his half-vow;
- **ED-003:** who prepared June's Sewickley room and expected her residence win;
- **ED-005/ED-006:** Tess's source network for private conversations, Nadia's ledger, and vault access;
- **ED-007:** which Trust office excluded June from work and why;
- **ED-009:** who sent the notice activating clause three.

ED-009 is especially important because it causes the Book One low point. Leaving the responsible actor
unknown makes the central forfeiture feel authorially unassigned rather than like a protected series
mystery. The sender should normally be revealed in Book One even if the source of their intelligence
continues into Book Two.

## Medium- and low-priority findings

### CON-023 — Elias leaves the wrong location

Episode Fifty-Seven says the seal remained warm during the drive from “Delisle's parish,” but the joint
hearing and office loss occurred at the Hall. Minimum repair: change the point of departure to the Hall.

### Wording risk — June “would not complete” versus later choice

Episode Sixty says the principal question has an answer: June “would not complete the vow.” The same
episode expressly preserves a possible later answer and states that she is not abandoning it. The intended
Book One answer is that she will not complete it now or as payment under present pressure. Add that scope
to the summary sentence so it cannot be read as a permanent series decision.

### Marta's automatic office loss arrives only at payment

Episode Fifty-Six first reveals a bylaw automatically removing a business agent who enforced a clause
later struck. The consequence is coherent and accepted by all parties, but earlier notice would make
Marta's risk part of her decisions rather than a climax rule introduced to collect a price. A single
earlier warning or bylaw citation is sufficient.

## Productive and reader-inference gaps to preserve

- The unreported severance is explicitly marked as impossible under both record systems. June refuses to
  accuse Vera without the missing instrument. This is productive Series-level mystery, not an error.
- The released bride's letter preserves source and deed while giving June one bounded sentence. It
  advances the protected mother mystery without purporting to prove who helped whom.
- Pratt's motive for helping the filing is ordinary career interest, not a supernatural debt. His request
  for later consideration is carefully limited and remains freely refusable.
- Elias's adverse testimony is written in Episode Forty-Nine but becomes effective only when he crosses
  to Delisle, signs, and offers it in Episode Fifty-Five. The price is created at the correct act.
- The Episode Fifty-Five testimony can reinforce rulings already announced because the hearing remains
  open and Kearse's foundation challenge threatens their survival on appeal. Preserve that provisional
  posture explicitly if revising.
- Episode Sixty closes the principal arc before introducing the severance sting, satisfying the book-first
  ending rule.

## Obligation and standing changes

- June freely elects permanent adverse-party status before Delisle, retaining refusal and acquiring no
  membership, office, property, bond completion, or authority over Elias.
- The filed action stays new clause execution, then accepted joint adjudication permanently defeats
  execution of clauses one through three.
- Milo's surety and custody end; Local Nine restores his membership standing while preserving ordinary
  claims arising from custody.
- Elias files adverse testimony, permanently loses every oath-bearing trustee office, and later accepts
  two limited human service contracts with no lineage title.
- Marta loses the Local Nine business agency but remains a member with vote, benefits, and kinship.
- Kenneth loses membership through permanent exile, though the creating adjudication is missing.
- June and Elias sign a revocable human alliance that creates no vow, office, invitation, or supernatural
  obligation.
- Six of the original nine clauses remain operative; all nine remain textually and historically present.

## Character knowledge and belief movement

### June

June learns the full price of permanent party standing, states it without borrowed language, and chooses
it as a third outcome. She moves from escaping jurisdiction to administering it adversely. After victory,
she restores ordinary work, rent, and bounded family contact instead of allowing supernatural procedure
to consume all meaning. She leaves the vow incomplete without pretending the choice is permanent.

### Elias

Elias converts prepared accountability into irreversible action by filing testimony against his office.
After losing title, he separates ordinary transactional authority from supernatural office and admits the
twenty-two-year lie of omission to Pratt. His Episode Sixty conduct demonstrates repeatable restraint:
he accepts current refusal, identifies lost authority, and offers information without purchasing intimacy.

### Marta and Kenneth

Marta accepts that good faith explains but does not preserve office after harmful administration. Her
membership and future disagreement remain intact. Kenneth accepts exile as a consequence of his acts and
does not mislabel it voluntary; however, the Local's decision producing that consequence remains absent.

### Milo, Cyd, Tess, and Pratt

Milo regains standing and preserves ordinary claims without converting release into waiver. Cyd rebuilds
the agency through ordinary contracts and maintains resident and business boundaries. Tess's eleven-year
case becomes usable only after June reorganizes it around clauses and declines Tess's preferred total
invalidation. Pratt's precise assistance exposes both professional ambition and an ordinary employment
interest while creating no supernatural claim.

## Episode-to-episode causal chain

- **E51 → E52:** After reading every permanent consequence, June elects standing before Delisle and files
  the clause-ordered action. The inherited filing-clock contradiction remains.
- **E52 → E53:** Filing stays clause four and convenes the joint hearing; June first proves standing and
  defeats clause one's exclusive-residence construction.
- **E53 → E54:** Clause order moves the forum from residence to circular surety and then the impossible
  clause-three cure; Milo is released and June declines total invalidation.
- **E54 → E55:** Kearse attacks trustee-side foundation, causing Elias to elect and file the adverse
  testimony that makes the narrow disposition survive challenge.
- **E55 → E56:** The accepted disposition causes each human and institutional price to be named before
  adjournment. Kenneth's exile lacks its own creating hearing.
- **E56 → E57:** Elias's loss of office causes inventory, limited ordinary work, and disclosure of the
  truth to Pratt. The departure location is misstated.
- **E57 → E58:** The ruling requires ministerial reconciliation, ordinary settlement, and restored work
  and housing. This works once the difference between hearing record and Hall master ledger is named.
- **E58 → E59:** Nadia's forwarding address and June's permanent-party purpose cause a bounded letter to
  the released bride; four days of ordinary life pass before the mother warning arrives.
- **E59 → E60:** June brings the warning into her decision without treating it as command, sets the
  current vow boundary and alliance, then discloses the unreported cut only after Book One's resolution
  has landed.
