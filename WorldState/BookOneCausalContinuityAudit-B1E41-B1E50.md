# Book One Causal Continuity Audit — B1E41–B1E50

Audit date: 2026-08-21  
Mode: report only; no episode prose changed  
Authority: locked canon and `BookCodex.json`, finalized episode prose, `ContinuityLedger.json`, then the episode map and storyline

## Overall verdict

This block has a strong emotional and procedural causal spine: the third holder presents clause three,
the missing witness makes forfeiture unavoidable, Milo is taken, Elias violates June's boundary while
preventing an invalid completion, and June deliberately chooses the bride-filed challenge. Episodes
Forty-Three, Forty-Four, Forty-Seven, and Fifty are especially clear about the difference between consent,
valid supernatural formation, and institutional consequence.

The block is not ready for manuscript repair without resolving three load-bearing transitions. The
nine-day clock is calculated from two incompatible starting points; an unauthorized alteration in one
counterpart is provisionally allowed to rewrite an original clause despite the newly locked Articles
rule; and the one-hour-forty-six-minute filing deadline disappears while Episodes Forty-Eight through
Fifty conduct lengthy conversations before June even reads the pleading. Cyd's residency also appears
only when it is needed to authorize Constance's threshold entry.

## Blocking and high-priority findings

### CON-019 — The nine-day term starts twice

In Episode Forty-One, Delisle received a valid trigger seven days before presentation, delayed six days,
and expressly says delay does not stop the term. Episodes Forty-Two and Forty-Six instead count nine full
days from the parish presentation; Episode Forty-Six calls that presentation “nine days earlier.” Both
cannot govern the same deadline. If Delisle's delay consumed the term, June should have had roughly two
days, not nine.

Minimum repair: choose one commencement event. The cleaner dramatic model is that notice compelled
presentation but the clause term commenced only when the required passage was read; remove the claim
that Delisle's delay did not stop the operative term. If the term truly began upon notice, every subsequent
date and the available response time must change.

### CON-020 — An altered counterpart changes an original clause

The sealed Articles say nineteen days. The parish counterpart was scraped and rewritten to say nine,
without acceptance by either original party. Episodes Forty-Two, Forty-Five, and Forty-Six nevertheless
treat nine as an active Article term until a tribunal defeats it. That conflicts with the locked rule that
all nine original clauses existed in 1871 and that later records, constructions, declarations, and
execution history cannot amend them.

This does not require losing the crisis. A deliberately falsified custodial copy could produce a
wrongful *administrative execution* that the parish and Trust refuse to suspend without adjudication,
while nineteen remains the actual Article term. The prose must distinguish “the clause changed” from
“an office executed a facially authenticated but false copy.” Author confirmation is required because
that distinction determines whether the supernatural effect itself follows the false record or only the
institutions do.

### CB-015 — The pre-dawn filing deadline vanishes

At the end of Episode Forty-Seven Elias says June must file before clause four names Milo's deed, estimated
at dawn, one hour and forty-six minutes away. Episodes Forty-Eight and Forty-Nine then contain two long,
separate examinations; Episode Forty-Nine refers to the previous night. Episode Fifty begins another
meeting, and only at its end does June request the full pleading. No stay, delayed clause-four
presentation, filing extension, or elapsed-time bridge is created.

Minimum repair: either move the motive and two-year disclosures after an emergency filing, or create a
specific act before dawn that preserves the challenge and delays clause four while June reviews or
perfects the pleading. Do not let “the filing can lodge without Elias's testimony” silently become
“the filing has lodged.”

### CB-014 — Cyd becomes a resident exactly when threshold authority is needed

Episode Forty-Five first establishes that Cyd “presently resides” in June's apartment and therefore can
invite Constance over June's objection. Episode Fifty later quantifies this as three nights a week.
Earlier prose establishes lease assistance and Cyd's new terms, but not the material act by which she
moved in, began sleeping there, or became a resident. This omitted bridge creates supernatural permission,
not merely domestic detail.

Minimum repair: before Constance's entry, establish Cyd bringing necessities, sleeping there under a
defined arrangement, and doing so to protect the lease; alternatively, remove Cyd's resident authority
and give Constance another causally valid meeting location.

## Medium-priority continuity debts

### ED-009 — Who invoked clause three?

The sender knew the Hall surety entry before the public index and knew which altered counterpart to wake.
Trust, Local Nine, and parish deny originating it; Kearse and Tess have access or motive, and Tess denies
causation. The episode correctly announces this as a mystery. The author-side answer and payoff are not
yet recorded.

### ED-010 — Who altered the parish counterpart, and how does that hand touch June's maternal record?

Elias recognizes a correspondence between the altered numeral and the hand that preserved June's roster
gap, but refuses attribution because the proof is incomplete. This is a productive series-level mystery,
provided the reserved truth identifies the hand, its access windows, and whether the resemblance is
genuine or copied.

### OB-024 status — When does clause three stop “executing” against June?

After forfeiture completes and June's standing is struck, Elias still says clause three is executing
against her, making completion void. The text needs a precise ending condition: entry of forfeiture,
Milo's release, lawful defeat, correction in both records, or another event. This matters for every later
attempt to complete the vow.

## Productive and safe inference gaps

- Delisle's permanent refusal is a productive reversal, not a gap. He accepts that a worse successor may
  follow and refuses to let that possibility operate as authority over his conscience.
- Tess's pre-drafted forfeiture pleadings intentionally reveal concealment rather than accidental
  knowledge. Her larger information network remains ED-006.
- Elias's mixed motive is deliberately withheld until Episode Forty-Eight and then paid in a voluntary,
  recorded answer. The delay changes the interpretation of his earlier conduct without contradicting it.
- June's attempted answer can be genuine consent while failing supernatural formation conditions. The
  prose repeatedly distinguishes those states and should retain that distinction.
- Elias's prepared testimony need not be filed immediately; Episode Forty-Nine correctly distinguishes
  writing from lodging it. The missing state is the emergency pleading, not his testimony.

## Obligation changes

- The parish custodial office presents clause three and preserves objections; Delisle separately and
  permanently refuses the personal act of certifying a completed vow.
- Clause three is administered on the disputed nine-day schedule; forfeiture removes June's provisional
  standing and converts Milo's surety into custody.
- Milo voluntarily walks to Trust custody to avoid exposing the Hall charter, while preserving his
  objection to the altered term.
- June's attempted half-vow creates no completion because there is no accepted witness and a clause is
  executing against her.
- June revokes Elias's permission to touch her, later creates explicit ordinary and threshold terms for
  entry, and chooses to examine Tess's complete bride-standing action.
- Elias prepares testimony whose filing against Trust interest will permanently end his eligibility for
  office; as of Episode Fifty it remains unlodged.

## Character knowledge and belief movement

### June

June learns the third holder's role, the disputed terms, the absence of another accepted witness, Tess's
forfeiture strategy, Elias's mixed motive, and the permanent-standing price of the action. She moves from
seeking a clean exit to choosing an obligation whose costs she has demanded in full. Her anger after
Elias covers her mouth carries coherently into the span confrontation and into her later conditional
invitation.

### Elias

Elias verifies the altered counterpart, recognizes its protective purpose and maternal-record
connection, accepts that he violated June's autonomy, and voluntarily states both rescue and desire as
motives. He begins converting accountability into institutional cost by preparing adverse testimony,
but has not yet made the election that spends his office.

### Milo

Milo understands that surety converts to custody and chooses not to expose Local Nine's charter through
physical obstruction. His choice is pressured but not silently recast as unpressured consent.

### Tess

Tess reveals that she prepared for perfected forfeiture because only a living injured bride and surety
can create the case she needs. Her literal compliance with June's staged-access boundary is exposed as
strategic concealment, reducing trust without destroying the usefulness of her work.

## Episode-to-episode causal chain

- **E41 → E42:** Delisle's custodial duty forces presentation; Elias recognizes the wrong term.
- **E42 → E43:** The shortened deadline and completion condition cause June to seek witness refusal.
- **E43 → E44:** Delisle's permanent refusal causes an exhaustive search for another accepted witness.
- **E44 → E45:** No substitute exists; Elias investigates the altered term while Tess exposes the
  litigation value of forfeiture.
- **E45 → E46:** The conservation finding and failed joint waiver leave the disputed schedule operating;
  Milo is taken at the named deadline.
- **E46 → E47:** Elias's physical interruption causes June to impose a boundary and force accountability;
  the loss causes him to identify the bride-filed challenge.
- **E47 → E48:** The filing route and June's demand for reasons cause Elias's voluntary motive statement.
  This transition is emotionally earned but conflicts with the stated pre-dawn deadline.
- **E48 → E49:** Elias's two-year admission causes a detailed evidentiary accounting and reveals his
  prepared adverse testimony.
- **E49 → E50:** June separates mixed motive from entitlement, tests threshold authority, and chooses to
  read the full case. The filing-clock bridge remains absent.

## Proposed repair order

1. Lock the difference between immutable Article text and provisional institutional execution of a false
   counterpart.
2. Lock the clause-three commencement event and rebuild all dates from it.
3. Establish Cyd's residency before it grants threshold authority.
4. Create or relocate the emergency filing act before the pre-dawn deadline.
5. State when clause three ceases executing, so later completion conditions have a stable rule.
6. Preserve the Delisle, Tess, Elias, and June emotional turns; they work once the procedural bridges are
   repaired.
