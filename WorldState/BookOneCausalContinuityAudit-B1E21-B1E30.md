# Book One Causal Continuity Audit — B1E21–B1E30

**Audit date:** 2026-08-21  
**Scope:** Finalized B1E21–B1E30 manuscript prose, carrying forward the B1E01–B1E20 ledger.  
**Mode:** Report only. No episode manuscript was modified.

## Overall verdict

This is the strongest causal block so far at the scene level. Kearse's interrogation produces the
employment attack; clause two produces the stay, June's self-surety filing, and Milo's informed
acceptance; the cutter consultation produces June's recorded refusal; sleeplessness produces the bounded
intimacy scene; Kearse's continuing reach produces Elias's voluntary breach; and Tess's bad-faith theory
produces the vault inspection that disproves it.

The principal continuity risks are not random plot events. They concern ownership of knowledge and
creation of authority. Cyd begins helping draft a sophisticated supernatural filing without receiving the
explanation June still owes her, and Kearse's narrowly purchased service opportunity later behaves like a
continuing claim without a clearly dramatized creating act. Episode Thirty also makes the earlier Articles
history contradiction impossible to ignore by showing nine original folded clauses plus indexed later
constructions.

## Blocking and high-severity findings

### CB-009 — Cyd acquires the supernatural case before June explains it

E13 ends before the promised explanation. E15 confirms June lied again. Yet in E23 Cyd helps draft a
seven-page clause-two filing, discusses the two incompatible meanings of line, understands member scent,
reviews Trust precedent, witnesses dual-system service, and listens to Pratt dispose of June's
supernatural standing. E27 still says the explanation is overdue, and E38 later begins the full account at
the original deposition.

This is not safe inference. The missing conversation creates extensive knowledge, permission to involve
the agency, and a major relationship change. Either E23 must sharply limit Cyd to ordinary drafting help
while June supplies all supernatural propositions, or a bounded partial disclosure must occur before the
work begins and remain distinguishable from E38's full disclosure.

### CON-014 — The original confirms nine clauses while E11 says they accumulated

E30 describes the sealed original as executed in 1871, bearing nine distinct sets of fold marks, with
each clause once opened separately before binding. It then distinguishes operative original text from
later constructions indexed by date and witness. That architecture is compatible with locked canon,
CL-001, E1, and E6. It is not compatible with E11's statement that the Articles did not begin with nine
clauses and gained clauses in response to brides.

E30 supplies the cleanest apparent resolution: all nine clauses were original; later brides generated
constructions, precedents, warnings, and procedural corrections. Author approval is still required before
that interpretation is used to repair prose.

### CB-010 — Kearse's one service becomes a continuing claim

E20's human bargain permits one scheduling fact and one service. Pratt expressly says the location does
not authorize compulsion. E21 shows Kearse using a lawful demand while Elias is exposed. E29 then says
Kearse's claim was acquired when June sold the interval, treats it as older than Constance's instruction,
and lets breach of Kearse's command create an assignable derivative claim.

The likely intended chain is: June supplies the interval; Kearse uses it to serve a separate lawful
lineage demand; that service—not June's bargain—creates the claim; Elias's later refusal creates the
assignable derivative. The manuscript needs that creation stated cleanly so June's narrow contract is not
silently transformed into supernatural authority she never granted.

### CON-015 — Personal acceptance and automatic surety selection coexist uneasily

E22 establishes a new controlling instruction: silence, presence, fear, kinship, and prior volunteer
language cannot create surety; the proposed surety must personally accept after hearing every consequence.
The same hearing says that if nobody accepts within forty-eight hours, the clause will select the nearest
eligible member under the standing construction.

Selection can identify a candidate without attaching the office, but the prose presents it as the
consequence that will settle on Milo. If automatic selection creates surety, it contradicts the personal-
acceptance rule. If it merely nominates Milo for a later valid acceptance, then failure to accept still
needs a separate stated consequence. The distinction must be locked before later clauses execute against
him.

### ED-006 — Tess's access network now includes the sealed original

Tess knows the Marrow vault, a service entrance, its lock, the guard's rounds, the correct brass box, and
the presence of the original. June expressly notices that Tess will not explain the box knowledge. This is
productive only if the author knows the source and eventual payoff. Combined with E11's knowledge of
Nadia's ledger and Cyd's private promise, it now implies a substantial surveillance or inside-access
system rather than determined public research.

## Medium-priority findings and ambiguities

### IG-007 — E20's timing error carries into E21

E21 properly continues directly from Kearse's interrogation at the end of E20. Once CON-013 is repaired,
the two episodes form one continuous night. No separate E21 repair is needed.

### IG-008 — June's position outside the E24 speaking circle needs institutional precision

E22 permits June, as named party, to interrupt, question, move for a stay, and request amendments while
outside or at the edge of the circle. E24 describes her lack of standing as a physical eight-foot
exclusion, yet Elias still treats her advice as valid speech. This can be a productive distinction between
participation in Milo's acceptance and June's right to object, but the exact rule should not drift into a
general claim that the named party has no voice.

### IG-009 — Trustee elevation appears to transform a human's bodily condition

E29 states that elevation would remove daylight from Pratt's life. The codex establishes that Pratt wants
trustee elevation and that trustees cannot survive direct sun, so the implication is consistent with the
planned character thread. It remains a major supernatural transition and should eventually receive an
explicit rule or consequence rather than surviving only as Elias's internal shorthand.

## Character knowledge and belief movement

### June

June learns that the surety office requires a second life against which default can execute; that cutting
would remove the attachment but not the Articles; that refusing a cut can itself be recorded without
becoming consent; that Elias's exposure is an information channel into her ordinary life; and that the
Articles honestly stopped a slaughter. Her belief changes from `expose fraud` to `defeat sincere clauses
on their own terms.` She also begins accepting bounded help and proximity while preserving explicit exits.

### Elias

Elias experiences June's bargain as injury without treating it as her authorization of compulsion. He
learns that he may choose between conflicting valid commands only by paying damage to standing. His belief
that obedient administration is moral continues to fail: he gives Milo genuine room to refuse, suffers
memory of Lenora's surety, and chooses a documented breach to protect June's location.

### Cyd

Cyd's practical loyalty remains consistent, but her epistemic state is not. By E23 she possesses knowledge
the manuscript has not given her. E27 and E38 confirm that the full explanation remains unpaid.

### Tess

Tess moves from prepared advocate to compromised ally. Her bad-faith theory is disproven by the very
original she helps June reach. Her grief and motive remain coherent; her information sources do not.

## Proposed repair order

No repair was applied.

1. Approve the original-nine/later-constructions model or choose another Articles history.
2. Define whether automatic surety selection nominates a candidate or creates the office.
3. Restore a bounded Cyd disclosure before E23 or restrict her participation to knowledge actually given.
4. State the separate creation of Kearse's lineage demand and derivative claim.
5. Assign Tess's source network and payoff condition.
6. Preserve the strong E21, E24–E28, and E30 scene causality while correcting only their incoming rules.

## Audit conclusion

This block successfully turns institutional rules into character costs without making any institution
secretly evil. Its central repair need is provenance: who gave Cyd knowledge, what act gave Kearse a
continuing claim, and what source gives Tess access. Once those creating acts are supplied, most of the
block should remain structurally intact.
