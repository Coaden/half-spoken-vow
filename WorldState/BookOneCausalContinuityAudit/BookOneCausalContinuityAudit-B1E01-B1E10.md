# Book One Causal Continuity Audit — B1E01–B1E10

**Audit date:** 2026-08-21  
**Scope:** Finalized manuscript prose for B1E01–B1E10, checked against locked canon, the continuity
ledger, the Book One storyline, the episode map, and later finalized prose only where necessary to test
whether an apparent debt receives a planned payoff.  
**Mode:** Report only. No episode manuscript was modified.

## Overall verdict

The ten episodes have a strong immediate causal spine. Each episode has a comprehensible reason to happen,
and seven of the nine transitions are cleanly produced by the preceding exit state. June consistently acts
on information she has acquired: naming leads to renunciation, lack of standing leads to a demand, partial
answers lead to investigation, notice leads to research, residence leads to the house, and the voice in the
house leads to the private ledger.

The current weakness is not a lack of plot causality. It is that several load-bearing rules, dates, and
identities disagree underneath that plot. The opening ten contain four high-severity contradictions, two
high-severity causal or explanation debts, and several smaller institutional ambiguities. Because the
prose usually proceeds confidently, a reader is not always told that a bridge is missing.

## Blocking and high-severity findings

### CON-001 — The Articles' clause numbering conflicts inside the opening

- **B1E01:** Pratt reads two clauses, then reads `Clause the third` as the generational bride-naming
  obligation.
- **B1E06:** Pratt identifies clause one as residence, clause two as surety, and clause three as a term
  certain.
- **B1E07:** clause one is expressly the residence clause.
- **Effect:** These cannot all be the same fixed sequence of nine clauses. B1E01 sounds as though clauses
  one and two have already been read and clause three names June, while the remainder of the book treats
  naming as the condition that activates a separate first tranche beginning with residence.
- **Severity:** Blocking rule contradiction.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** Establish whether B1E01 is reading preliminary articles or
  recitals distinct from the numbered operative clauses, or align its numbering with the fixed sequence.

### CB-001 — The half-vow and June's Article obligations are not distinguished

- **Established:** B1E01 and B1E02 repeatedly state that Elias's half-vow binds only Elias and leaves June
  `entirely free`.
- **Later assumption:** B1E05–B1E07 proceed on the basis that June remains subject to clause one and must
  satisfy residence.
- **Missing bridge:** The prose never expressly distinguishes freedom from *Elias's half-vow* from June's
  separate status as the named bride under the Articles, nor does it explain why interrupting the clause
  suspends rather than eliminates that separate obligation.
- **Effect:** A reader can invent the intended distinction, but doing so requires a legal rule the opening
  has not actually supplied. `Entirely free` makes the later assumption feel like a changed rule.
- **Severity:** Blocking causal and institutional gap by B1E07.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** One precise clarification by an authoritative speaker could
  separate the two instruments: June owes nothing under the incomplete vow while remaining the named
  party against whom the interrupted Article sequence may resume.

### CON-002 — The opening countdowns do not share one calendar

- **B1E03:** the moon nights are the twenty-first through twenty-third and are nineteen days away.
- **B1E05:** four days have passed since the Monday naming; the moon is still nineteen days away; clause
  one has twenty-six days remaining.
- **B1E06:** the naming Monday was the fourth and clause one has twenty-six days remaining.
- **B1E07:** approximately a week has passed since the naming, yet the notice and Pratt's language make
  the clause appear to resume at nine and make the first counted night begin immediately.
- **B1E10:** the moon is eleven days away and another unnamed countdown is nineteen days away.
- **Effect:** The moon clock, thirty-day suspension, day-of-month references, and elapsed episode time
  cannot all be plotted on one calendar. The problem also obscures whether B1E07 is advance satisfaction
  or satisfaction after actual resumption.
- **Severity:** High temporal contradiction; blocking before any deadline-dependent revision.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** Build a one-page dated chronology beginning with the naming,
  then derive every spoken countdown from it. Decide explicitly whether the B1E07 filing occurs during
  suspension or on the morning of resumption.

### ED-001 — June's first transformation has no causal history

- **B1E03:** at age thirty-one, June is told that she will lose her human shape in nineteen days. Her
  response and the surrounding dialogue treat this as a coming event she has never experienced.
- **Locked canon and later prose:** all wolf-blood members change on the three moon nights, and B1E35–B1E37
  explicitly treat the coming event as June's first uninitiated change.
- **Missing answer:** Why has a thirty-one-year-old wolf by blood never changed before, and what did June
  understand about being a Havlik or wolf-line before B1E03?
- **Constraint:** Naming cannot casually activate a new biological power without contradicting the rule
  that paper cannot alter borne identity.
- **Severity:** High explanation debt originating in B1E03; must be resolved before the moon sequence.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** Requires an author-level rule or history decision, not a line
  of cosmetic exposition.

### CON-003 — The Hall's vote method conflicts with locked wolf-side epistemology

- **B1E04:** Marta says she handed June over `by a show of hands`.
- **Locked canon and later episodes:** Local Nine votes by open roll call because members cannot conceal
  their positions from one another face to face.
- **Effect:** The discrepancy weakens a load-bearing difference between Local Nine and the Marrow Trust.
- **Severity:** High institutional contradiction.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** Align the earlier vote description with whichever witnessed
  voting method is canonically intended.

### CON-004 — The three-o'clock reader changes identity without acknowledgment

- **B1E08:** June hears a woman's voice and states that it is not Elias and `not Nadia`.
- **B1E09:** the reader is Nadia, continuing the same house practice; June shows no surprise that her
  prior identification was wrong.
- **Effect:** This reads as an accidental identity change rather than a reveal. If June merely misidentified
  the voice, the later scene needs to register that update.
- **Severity:** High local contradiction across adjacent episodes.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** Either remove the confident exclusion or establish why June
  mistook Nadia's formal reading voice.

### CON-005 — Renn is both the sixth and seventh bride

- **B1E09:** the ledger explicitly lists Renn as item six and Kovach as item seven. It also places the
  2015 Renn entry before the 1992 Kovach entry, breaking the otherwise chronological list.
- **B1E11, the episode map, and CL-042:** Tess says her sister was number seven and the story repeatedly
  calls her the seventh bride.
- **Effect:** This changes Tess's identity, the chapter title `The Seventh Sister`, and the history of the
  unaccounted bride.
- **Severity:** High cross-manuscript contradiction.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** The apparent intended order is Kovach as item six in 1992 and
  Renn as item seven in 2015, but the author must confirm before any prose or ledger correction.

### ED-002 — The abandonment price is withheld but never paid off

- **B1E05:** Elias tells June the exact price. June knows it and reacts strongly, but the narration removes
  the words from the reader.
- **Map/storyline promise:** the price is described as stated and important to June's choice.
- **Later finalized prose:** the terms themselves are not disclosed through B1E60.
- **Effect:** This is not dramatic irony, because the viewpoint character knows while the reader is denied
  without a stated reason. The reader cannot evaluate June's refusal to let Elias pay.
- **Severity:** High Book-level explanation debt.
- **Minimum repair direction, not applied:** Either disclose the price when June receives it or deliberately
  classify the withholding, provide a reason for it, and assign a payoff before the Book One decision.

## Medium-severity institutional gaps

### IG-001 — Who can accept or complete clause-one satisfaction?

B1E06 says a trustee reads a disposition into a record before an accepted witness and Pratt files it.
In B1E07 Pratt, who repeatedly says he is counsel and not a party, accepts and stamps June's proposed
satisfaction without a trustee or Delisle present. The prose then treats clause one as satisfied and the
counted-night obligation as active.

The likely answer is that Pratt may accept an advance filing while supernatural satisfaction occurs by
performance or later disposition, but that distinction is not stated. Track this separately from CB-001.

### IG-002 — June's standing has several meanings

By B1E05 June has no Local Nine standing but does have standing as a named party to compel exposed Elias.
That distinction is coherent and useful, but it is distributed across Marta's closing statement, June's
inference, and Elias's confirmation. It should be preserved explicitly in the institutional interpretation
ledger so later prose does not collapse membership standing, Articles standing, attachment registration,
and ordinary civil capacity into one concept.

### IG-003 — The attachment enters both registers despite June's roster erasure

Elias says the attachment was entered in both registers within an hour. This can be true because an
attachment register need not be Local Nine's membership roster, but the distinction is not yet dramatized.
This is safe for now if the ledgers remain separate in later usage.

## Productive gaps that should remain intact

These omissions announce themselves as questions and the story demonstrably knows they are questions:

- Why Elias spoke and what the second part of his motive is.
- What the nineteen-year roster gap protects and why Marta closed Rose's line.
- Who holds the third enforceable copy of the Articles.
- Who arranged for Vera to contact June so quickly.
- Why Nadia's private ledger exists outside the Trust's official record.
- What happened to the unaccounted bride.

No explanatory repair should flatten these mysteries prematurely.

## Productive-looking gap that currently lacks a payoff

### ED-003 — Who prepared June's room and expected her to win?

B1E07 explicitly announces this as a mystery. B1E08 eliminates Elias, but neither the inspected canon nor
the finalized Book One prose supplies the answer, the knower, or a payoff condition. Because the prose
tells the reader to notice it, it is good suspense at entry. Without a later answer it becomes unpaid
Book-level continuity debt.

## Safe reader-inference and compression gaps

- **B1E02 to B1E03:** June goes to the Hall because she has been named through her Havlik line and wants
  to renounce. The action follows directly even though the travel and appointment are omitted.
- **B1E04 to B1E05:** June asks Kenneth where to meet someone she does not trust and arranges the bridge
  meeting with Elias off page. The prose reports Kenneth's advice and provides one dominant inference.
- **B1E05 to B1E06:** Elias's incomplete answer and the twenty-six-day deadline cause June to seek the
  document's shape from Pratt.
- **B1E08 to B1E09:** The reading voice and June's professional recognition cause her to return on the
  next counted night. Her three days of thought are compression, not missing motive.
- **B1E09 to B1E10:** Nadia promises to identify the two women she liked, and the next episode begins with
  that conversation before dawn.

## Open emotional and relationship debt

At B1E10 June promises Cyd that she will tell her everything the following day. Later prose shows that
June does not forget the promise: B1E15 says she has promised an explanation twice and chooses another
lie, and B1E38 finally pays the disclosure. This is therefore intentional character debt rather than a
forgotten thread. The missing cancellation or avoidance beat between B1E10 and B1E15 should remain tracked
so the delay reads as June's decision rather than scheduling drift.

## Knowledge and belief movement

### June

- **B1E01 entry:** knows her work, finances, family name, and ordinary life; does not understand trustees,
  the Articles, the attachment, or why the room recognizes her.
- **B1E02 exit:** knows Elias alone is exposed, abandonment exists, and the transcript makes the event
  usable by absent parties; believes she can still undo the problem cleanly.
- **B1E04 exit:** knows she has no Local standing, Marta deliberately closed Rose's line, the Hall chose
  its charter over her, and the moon has a practical housing cost; begins treating her named-party status
  as her only usable legal identity.
- **B1E05 exit:** knows the attachment is real but grants no entitlement, knows she can compel Elias, and
  knows his stated motive is truthful but incomplete; her belief that formal abandonment is a clean exit
  has been destroyed.
- **B1E07 exit:** believes exact period language can defeat at least one Article demand and that someone
  in the house can anticipate her reasoning.
- **B1E10 exit:** knows the earlier bride outcomes, believes usefulness to a third party may produce waiver,
  and has begun to recognize that refusing all help can itself be fatal.

### Elias

- **B1E01 exit:** knows the clause he read privately, knowingly chooses exposure, and knows June is not
  bound by his half.
- **B1E05 exit:** knows June can and will use compulsion under pressure; tells her what the experience does
  to him and preserves the unrevealed part of his motive.
- **B1E08 exit:** knows June defeated residence unexpectedly, knows she refuses to test his honesty by
  compulsion, and gives her access to one unauthorized personal truth.

### Marta

- **B1E02:** knows Elias's act creates danger for the Local and that June remains free of his vow.
- **B1E04:** admits she personally closed Rose's line and voted to honor the Articles; continues to believe
  three hundred eleven members and the charter justify her institutional choice.

## Obligation state through B1E10

- **Elias's half-vow exposure:** created in B1E01; active through B1E10; binds Elias only; answer or formal
  abandonment are the stated ending conditions.
- **June as named party under the Articles:** asserted in B1E01 and relied upon thereafter; its relationship
  to the half-vow remains insufficiently explained under CB-001.
- **Clause one residence:** interrupted in B1E01, described as suspended in B1E02, given a resumption rule in
  B1E06, and treated as satisfied through the B1E07 filing and counted nights. Exact effective date and
  satisfaction authority remain unclear.
- **Local Nine copy demand:** created by June in writing, aloud, before Yolanda in B1E04. Marta states an
  eleven-day response date. Still pending at B1E10.
- **Counted nights:** accepted in B1E07 at sixteen nights per quarter, beginning that night and continuing
  until clause one is satisfied, waived, or defeated. The prose simultaneously calls clause one satisfied,
  so status language requires clarification.
- **Cyd disclosure promise:** ordinary interpersonal promise created in B1E10, not a supernatural
  obligation. It becomes an intentional emotional debt and is not fulfilled in this audited range.

## Audit conclusion

The opening works at the level of intention and action: June's decisions reliably create the next episode.
The main repair work is beneath that surface. Clause numbering, the two simultaneous obligation systems,
the calendar, the first transformation, the reader's identity, and the bride order need authoritative
resolution before further continuity-sensitive revision. The roster gap, Elias's motive, and the third
holder are functioning mysteries and should not be explained early merely to make the ledger look tidy.
