## Q-001 — What is the authoritative drafting history of the Articles?

**Answered and locked, 2026-08-21:** All nine clauses existed in the 1871 Articles from the beginning.
Later brides created interpretations, constructions, precedents, depositions, statements, verbal
declarations, and execution history. Those supplementary records cannot amend, add, remove, reorder, or
rewrite the original Articles.

Choose the intended model:

1. All nine clauses were executed in 1871; later brides produced constructions, precedents, warnings, and
   procedural corrections. This matches locked canon, E1, E6, and especially the sealed original in E30.
2. The 1871 instrument began smaller and acquired formally executed amendments that became new clauses.

Current prose attempts both. Repair E11–E12 and any later amendment language to the locked first model.

## Q-011 — Can a falsified counterpart produce provisional supernatural execution?

**Answered and locked, 2026-08-21:** The authentic Clause Three term is nine calendar days. The parish
counterpart was altered to nineteen, which cannot amend the shared obligation. A timely challenge to the
material discrepancy triggers the Articles' mandatory ten-calendar-day stay of forfeiture. The stay
preserves the parties without changing or restarting the underlying term: nine plus the stay produces
nineteen days in practice.

The original Articles say nineteen days, and the parish copy was altered to nine without acceptance by
either original party. Under the locked immutable-Articles rule, does the false counterpart merely cause
the institutions to administer a wrongful nine-day forfeiture until adjudication, or does its facially
valid seal make the supernatural effect provisionally operate too? The answer must not let later records
amend the Articles; it should define the consequence of executing from a corrupted original counterpart.

## Q-019 — What is the exact price of Elias formally abandoning his half-vow?

**Answered and locked, 2026-08-21:** Formal abandonment ends Elias's spoken half and exposure but does
not sever the involuntary attachment. The price is permanent excommunication from the lineage record:
loss of every lineage office, succession, institutional protection, and enforcement standing, while
ordinary civil identity and individually held civil property remain. Elias does not abandon in Book One;
June does not answer and does not seek or compel his abandonment, so the story ends half-bound. Its
ultimate disposition is preserved for Volume Two.

The codex says abandonment remains possible and carries a stated price. Episode Sixty explicitly reminds
Elias that he may use it, but neither June nor the reader has been told what it costs. What is the price,
where is it stated in the original Articles or another valid record, and does June know it by the bridge
conversation?
