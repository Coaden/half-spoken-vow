# Book One Causal Continuity Audit — B1E31–B1E40

**Audit date:** 2026-08-21  
**Scope:** Finalized B1E31–B1E40 manuscript prose, carrying forward the B1E01–B1E30 ledger.  
**Mode:** Report only. No episode manuscript was modified.

## Overall verdict

The emotional and institutional causality is excellent. The honest original leads Constance to explain
necessity; that evidence leads the Hall to vote; the vote leads Kenneth to explain his dissent and exposes
the altered minutes; June's loss of shelter leads her to ask for help; the first change creates Elias's
injury; that injury creates a careful record separating desire from consent; the accumulated ordinary
damage finally causes full disclosure to Cyd; and Cyd's new authority over agency consequences produces a
real but limited employment negotiation.

The block's temporal causality is not viable as written. The moon moves from three days away to nine days
away, another child changes nine days outside the absolute public calendar, June's basement-to-Trust-house
decision changes off page, and only one of the three mandatory moon nights is dramatized or accounted for.
These are not four isolated corrections. They require one locked calendar and one explicit plan for the
three-night window.

## Blocking and high-severity findings

### CON-016 — The moon moves from three days away to nine

E32 says tonight's roster locks room assignments for the moon three nights from then. E35 occurs after
E32–E34 but repeatedly says June's change is nine days away. E36 then reaches moonrise after an implied
passage of those nine days, while E38 calls the hillside change `last night.` The sequence cannot be dated
without time moving backward.

This must be repaired from a master calendar. The safest likely model is to decide the moon date first,
then set E32's roster lock, E35's room return, E36's first night, and every clause deadline relative to it.

### CON-017 — A first change occurs outside the absolute lunar rule

E35's sixteen-year-old begins changing nine days before the public moon date, and the Hall nurse identifies
it as a first change that `sometimes` comes early. Locked canon says members change involuntarily on the
three nights bracketing the full moon, on a public calendar, with no exemption. An early change nine days
outside that window breaks the economic and institutional ecology: if bodies can change unpredictably,
safe rooms and work loss are no longer reliably schedulable.

The girl can still urgently need room six, but her event must fall inside the same public window or be a
non-change medical crisis that does not rewrite the rule.

### CB-011 — June's second and third moon nights disappear

E36 dramatizes one moonrise and one dawn. E37 is the immediate aftermath; E38 calls the event last night and
June intends to work; E40 later says Kenneth's hearing was continued until after the moon window. No prose
accounts for June's required second and third changes, where she was contained, whether Elias returned,
or whether the hillside protocol remained usable after the utility incident.

This missing bridge creates bodily safety, consent, injury, employment, and relationship state. The book
does not need two more full transformation episodes, but it must establish what happened on both nights
and how those events affected June and Elias.

### CON-018 — Kenneth's warning was both weeks ago and five years ago

In E33 Kenneth says he knew June's first change was approaching `a few weeks` before she did. He then says he
left a private-room card under her door when she was twenty-six. June is thirty-one. If the card was the
warning prompted by her imminent current first change, it cannot have been left five years earlier. If it
belonged to an earlier false approach, the story needs to explain why the change did not occur then.

This is part of ED-001 rather than a harmless age typo. The author must decide whether the card belongs to
the present month, whether June's age was meant to differ, or whether a real suppression/delay mechanism is
being reserved.

### CB-012 — Room six becomes a Trust-filed containment address without being filed

E14 makes the room-six transaction an ordinary human receipt explicitly not accepted by Local Nine. E35
says Yolanda's notice reaches the Trust because room six was the containment address associated with
June's filed counted-night plan. No episode creates that association. The clause-one counted-night address
is Sewickley; the moon safe room is at the Hall.

If June later filed room six as a safety condition, that act needs to exist because it gives the Trust
knowledge of her containment and prompts Elias's intervention. Otherwise Elias needs a different,
authorized source for learning the room was returned.

### CB-013 — June conditionally accepts inspection, then E36 says she refused the house

E35 ends with June requiring a daylight inspection, testing every gate and key, and reading a final
protocol into both records before entry. Elias agrees. E36 opens nine days later calling Sewickley `the
house she had refused` and says the hillside plan was prepared after refusing it. The inspection,
decision, and reason are absent.

This omission creates the emergency choice in E36. Either dramatize or retrospectively state that the
inspection failed a specific boundary, or let the basement failure prevent use of a still-contingent house
without falsely saying June refused it.

### ED-007 — Why is the Marrow Trust attacking June's employment?

E27 identifies Kearse as the source of the initial security pressure. E39 reveals that the Marrow Trust,
as property adviser and guarantor, then supplies an acceptable-reporter list excluding June and uses
continuity leverage against the client. This appears consistent with Constance's E31 promise to arrange
facts around completion, but neither June nor reader receives the responsible office, instruction, or
stated purpose.

Because the Trust needs June alive and solvent enough to remain a functioning adverse party, its choice to
damage her only ordinary source of standing requires an eventual explanation. This may be deliberate
pressure, rival infiltration, administrative risk management, or another office acting independently.

## Medium-priority findings and ambiguities

### ED-008 — The nineteen-year instruction and altered minutes

E34 intentionally announces a mystery: an old instruction preserved June's roster gap and later required
protective language in the minutes. Yolanda physically inserted the sentence but says the same unidentified
person `made` both the gap and alteration, then says it was not her. The likely distinction is originator
versus transmitting keeper, but the wording should remain precise.

The answer appears tied to the protected mystery of Rose and must not be prematurely revealed. The author
does need a reserved answer identifying the originator, custodian, activation conditions, and reason.

### IG-010 — Nine days may pass between E35 and E36

Routine inspection, construction attempts, and calendar time may be compressed. Once CON-016 and CB-013
are repaired, an explicit transition can safely carry the interval without dramatizing every day.

### IG-011 — Kenneth's discipline is not punishment for dissent alone

E33 first lets June fear exile for one vote, then Kenneth accurately explains the charge: repeated acts
against Local decisions, with the lone no making those acts impossible to ignore. This is a productive
clarification and should remain. Later summaries must not reduce his removal to punishment for voting no.

## Character knowledge and belief movement

### June

June loses the Hall vote without finding a procedural defect, learns Kenneth chose her without making her
an inherited debt, accepts help only after her basement plan physically fails, survives one first-change
night, and acknowledges desire while preserving non-consent. Her most important belief change occurs with
Cyd: deciding alone who may help is another form of assigning other people's limits.

### Elias

Elias learns the human cost omitted from the Articles and hears Constance admit the instrument requires
transition rather than permanence. He violates June's departure boundary for mixed motives and refuses to
turn injury into noble sacrifice. He also warns that public evidence of desire will be converted into
institutional pressure even though it cannot become consent.

### Cyd

E38 finally supplies the epistemic and emotional change missing earlier. Cyd learns the full supernatural
truth, remains friend and employer, identifies June's unilateral protection as control, and accepts only
ordinary authority over consequences that reach her agency and lease.

### Marta, Kenneth, and Yolanda

Marta votes for institutional survival without pretending June caused the failure. Kenneth distinguishes
love, promise, dissent, and practical help. Yolanda admits record tampering while preserving both the
protective sentence and June's dissent. None becomes secretly villainous.

3. Decide the true date and meaning of Kenneth's warning card.

## Audit conclusion

This block contains several of the book's best scenes and its least workable calendar. The repair should
not rewrite the emotional architecture to solve temporal errors. Lock dates and the three-night logistics
first, then make the smallest bridges necessary around those scenes.
