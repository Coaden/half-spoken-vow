## Premise

June Havlik is thirty-one years old and paid to listen. She transcribes depositions on subcontract —
no benefits, month-to-month lease, a car that will not pass inspection — and she is very good at the
one thing the job teaches, which is noticing exactly how people word things.

In 1871, after something happened that nobody now living will describe, a wolf mutual-aid local and a
vampire family trust signed articles of agreement to keep the peace between them. The document has
nine numbered clauses. It requires, in every generation, a bride from one side given to the other,
and it comes due on whoever is named. Seven women have been named since. Four died under clauses.
Two were released. One is unaccounted for.

June is the eighth, and she finds out the way she finds out everything — by transcribing it, in an
ordinary probate session she took because rent is due Friday. Her refusal is absolute, but she has no
standing to renounce the Articles, challenge them as a member, claim benefits, or demand protection:
her mother left the Hall before June could be initiated, the roster line was closed, and June was left
a wolf with no membership. When the trustee sent to read the first clause steps into the deposition
suite from a windowless corridor, he does not read it. He speaks the first half of the binding vow
instead.

The vow is meant to be spoken in two parts separated by an interval, precisely so that consent is deliberate; he did not exploit a loophole, he used the intended form at the worst possible moment. And once both halves are given it can never be undone — so what he has handed her is not an escape but a door that only opens one way.

That is not a rescue. Speaking one half binds only the speaker: it strips his protections and leaves
him exposed to every party with standing, while she stays entirely free. It also stops the clause,
buys her time she never asked for, and makes her the most legally valuable person in Pittsburgh.

He will not say why he did it. She has not answered. The story starts now rather than a year earlier
because her naming entered the record on a Tuesday morning, the clause reading was lawfully
interrupted, and the sequence has begun.

Two things make her position specifically a wolf's rather than a woman's. The first is that every member
of Local Nine can smell she is a Havlik, so the roster that erased her is a lie their own bodies refuse
to hold — they voted to hand her over knowing exactly whose daughter she was. The second is that she
has no safe room on the housing roster and no coverage from the benefit fund for the three nights each
month when her body stops being hers, which is not a metaphor: it is three nights of lost wages, a
shift she cannot explain, and a job she is about to lose.

And if she ever answers the vow, she can never be initiated. No one may stand in two conflicting
records. Her two ways of becoming a person with standing cancel each other out.

## Book One throughline

June must survive and defeat the first tranche of the Articles without surrendering her consent, while
deciding what Elias Marrow's half-spoken vow will mean on her own terms. That requires the full reason
he spoke, in his own words; a lawful answer to clauses one through three; and a choice that is neither
completion under pressure nor an escape purchased with somebody else's loss.

The external conflict is the first three clauses themselves: residence, surety, and a witnessed-vow
deadline, enforced while Local Nine protects its charter and the Marrow Trust seeks institutional
survival. Neither institution is secretly evil, which is why June must produce a remedy they can
recognize rather than merely expose wrongdoing.

## Continuity locks for the repair

- June first changed at sixteen and has survived the three public moon nights for fifteen years. Rose
  taught her only a bare safety routine before disappearing, and June later improvised unsafe private
  containment without an exterior lock, attendant, wage coverage, medical check, or reliable emergency
  release. Episode Three reveals the collective life and support system she was denied, not werewolf
  biology. Room six is her first purpose-built safe room. Cyd remains ignorant; Milo assumes June has a
  private arrangement; Kenneth left an anonymous private-room card when June was twenty-six, which she
  discarded without understanding who sent it.
- Clause Three's authentic 1871 term is nine days. The parish copy was protectively altered to nineteen.
  A timely counterpart challenge creates a mandatory ten-day stay, producing nineteen days in practice
  without amending the original Article.
- Tess lacks standing to invoke Clause Three. She gives Lionel Kearse the argument and motive; Lionel
  knowingly invokes through his recognized rival-trust claim because forfeiture will weaken the Marrow
  Trust. June learns Tess instigated it before electing to carry Tess's case.
- The vow ends Book One half-bound: Elias's half remains active, binding and exposing him alone. June has
  spoken nothing she can abandon. Formal abandonment would end his half-vow at the permanent price of
  excommunication but would not sever the underlying attachment. Neither of them takes that route in
  Book One; its ultimate disposition belongs to Volume Two.

## What Book One resolves

- Whether June answers the vow, seeks or compels Elias's abandonment, or forces a third outcome — settled
  for this book when she explicitly preserves the half-bound state.
- Elias's true reason for speaking first, delivered by his own mouth rather than by inference.
- Clauses one, two, and three — defeated on their own terms and permanently barred from execution. All nine original clauses remain visible; the operative count ends the book at six.
- Whether Local Nine keeps its charter, and what Marta spent to decide it.
- The identity of the third party holding the unread copy of the Articles.
- Tess Renn's eleven-year case: filed or dead.

## What Book One preserves

- Why June's mother left the Hall, and what she took with her. *(Earliest Book Two.)*
- Who is buying cut attachments, and what they are assembling. *(Earliest Book Three.)*
- Whether true attachments are genuinely dying out, or are being suppressed. *(Earliest Book Three; may remain open indefinitely.)*

## Protagonist arc

- **Misbelief:** That if she reads the terms carefully enough she can get free without owing anyone — that every relationship is a contract to be escaped rather than a debt worth carrying.
- **Cost of the misbelief:** She negotiates alone when she could ask, lies to the one friend who would help without conditions, treats Milo's loyalty as a liability instead of an asset, and repeatedly buys short-term escapes that cost other people more than a shared plan would have. Every clause she beats by herself, she beats late and expensively.
- **Turn:** Chapters twenty and twenty-one. When Elias finally tells her why he spoke — and both readings are true at once — she stops trying to find the exit and starts using the bond as an instrument. She files Tess's case, which requires her to *remain* the bride. Choosing the obligation is the only move the document did not anticipate.
- **Ending state:** She believes some debts are chosen, and that a chosen debt is not a trap. It costs her the ability to walk away clean, and it costs Marta her office, Kenneth his Local, and Elias any future office at all. She is a permanent party to the Articles and, for the first time, not alone in front of them.

## Relationship arc

June and Elias move from a public unilateral claim and coercive asymmetry to a bounded alliance. His
full motive is spoken voluntarily; his desire becomes accountable action rather than entitlement; her
refusal remains absolute; and Book One ends with the vow incomplete because that is the state June
affirmatively chooses, not because the story postponed her decision.

## Supporting character arcs

- **Elias:** Moves from correct instrument to accountable witness. He reveals both motives without
  compulsion, converts desire into testimony June can use, and permanently gives up eligibility for
  office to defend her narrower victory.
- **Marta:** Defends three hundred eleven memberships in good faith, preserves the charter, and pays
  for that choice by losing the business agency.
- **Cyd:** Rejects June's protective secrecy, chooses loyalty with full information, and absorbs a
  countable business loss without becoming an initiate, witness, or supernatural solution.
- **Tess:** Watches her bad-faith theory fail at the midpoint, adapts rather than becoming vindictive,
  and gets her eleven-year case filed by the first living bride able to cure its standing defect.
- **Milo:** Owns his voluntary surety choice, bears its lawful consequence, and is released without
  being reduced to passive collateral or relieved of responsibility for the leak that helped expose him.

## Macro chapters

Twenty-four chapters, each expanding into two or three release episodes. Every chapter has a want, an
obstacle, a change, and a consequence.

### Chapter One — On the Record *(Episodes 1–2)*
- **Want:** Finish the morning's transcript and make rent.
- **Obstacle:** The deposition she is recording turns out to be about her, and then a stranger walks in and binds himself to her in front of witnesses before anyone explains anything.
- **Change:** The clause that would have taken her life apart is not read. A half-spoken vow is spoken instead, and June's own clarification for the record is what forces the consequence onto the transcript.
- **Consequence:** She is bound to nothing and owes everything. The danger has been moved onto a man she has never met, she cannot hand it back, and a cutter is already leaving voicemails.

### Chapter Two — Good Standing *(Episodes 3–4)*
- **Want:** Renounce her place under lineage law and walk away.
- **Obstacle:** She was never initiated. Her mother's exile closed the line, so there is no entry to renounce — while every member in the building can smell exactly whose daughter she is.
- **Change:** She learns that the change she has survived privately for years is supported here by safe
  rooms, attendants, wage coverage and recovery infrastructure — none of which she may use because she
  has no membership, vote, benefits or standing. She also learns Marta personally signed the notation
  that excluded her.
- **Consequence:** The only body of law that recognizes she exists is the one trying to dispose of her — so she serves her first demand, in both record systems at once, and stops asking permission.

### Chapter Three — The Exposed Man *(Episodes 5–6)*
- **Want:** Undo what was done for her, and find out why he did it.
- **Obstacle:** He answers the literal question and never the meant one, and abandonment carries a price she will not let him pay.
- **Change:** She discovers she can compel an exposed trustee — and does, once, and hates herself for it. She also gets the shape of all nine clauses and learns a third holder exists whom nobody at the Trust can name.
- **Consequence:** Two clocks now run at once, she holds coercive power over the only person protecting her, and he has warned her that answering the vow would oblige him to tell her the rest.

### Chapter Four — Clause One *(Episodes 7–8)*
- **Want:** Keep her apartment and her own front door.
- **Obstacle:** Clause one requires the bride to reside under the Trust's roof within thirty days.
- **Change:** She finds the 1871 definition of residence, which requires only counted nights of record, and satisfies the clause by wording alone — her first outright win.
- **Consequence:** She sleeps in a jurisdiction on a technicality she wrote herself, discovers the house was told to expect her before she filed, and hears a voice reading a document aloud at three in the morning.

### Chapter Five — Nights of Record *(Episodes 9–10)*
- **Want:** Proof of what happened to the women before her.
- **Obstacle:** The evidence is in a room she is not permitted to enter, kept by a steward who has not decided who to show it to.
- **Change:** She reads the stewards' ledger: seven names, four clause numbers, and a strategy — the bride who got out survived by making herself indispensable to a third party.
- **Consequence:** She knows the body count and the method, and she learns one of the seven is alive with a forwarding address nobody has used in ninety years.

### Chapter Six — The Seventh Sister *(Episodes 11–12)*
- **Want:** Information she cannot get from the Trust.
- **Obstacle:** Tess Renn will trade it, and wants June to take a risk that will probably kill her, and says so plainly.
- **Change:** All nine clauses were present in the 1871 Articles, but later brides created interpretations, constructions, precedents, depositions, declarations, and execution history around them. Those supplementary records cannot amend the original Articles, but they create a paper trail June can use to challenge how a fixed clause is applied. Meanwhile the listener learns from Elias's side that the Trust is failing and needs a completed attachment to survive the century.
- **Consequence:** June has a route and an ally who is honest about using her; Elias has a maker who is preparing to spend him.

### Chapter Seven — The Borne Record *(Episodes 13–15)*
- **Want:** To secure a place for the approaching bodily event nobody will house her for, and to understand what she has started noticing about people.
- **Obstacle:** There is no form, no waiver and no lawful route to a safe room for someone off the roster — and the thing she has started noticing has no name she knows.
- **Change:** June realizes she has been reading the borne record unconsciously her whole career and begins doing it on purpose. She also buys a safe-room slot from a member with her own rent money, which works and which she will be judged for.
- **Consequence:** Her transformation begins as a literacy rather than a power, it immediately becomes a professional weapon, and it costs her the ability to be comfortable near the one friend she can still lie to.

### Chapter Eight — Dues *(Episodes 16–18)*
- **Want:** Hold on to her income while all of this happens.
- **Obstacle:** A second client drops her after an anonymous call, and Milo repeats something at the Hall that reaches the Trust within the hour.
- **Change:** Cyd keeps her publicly and eats the loss; June traces the leak and chooses not to destroy her cousin over it.
- **Consequence:** Her ordinary life is now paying for the supernatural one, clause two is coming, and the caller knew her mother's maiden name.

### Chapter Nine — The Span *(Episodes 19–21)*
- **Want:** An ally with standing.
- **Obstacle:** Lionel Kearse is hospitable, genuinely useful, and will spend her the moment their interests diverge.
- **Change:** She takes Nadia's advice and makes herself useful to a rival trust — the smartest move available and a betrayal of the only person protecting her.
- **Consequence:** She secures a real concession and learns what she traded only when Kearse thanks her for it in front of witnesses. Meanwhile Kearse compels Elias to name the one thing that would most hurt her, and Elias cannot refuse.

### Chapter Ten — Clause Two *(Episodes 22–24)*
- **Want:** Keep Milo out of it.
- **Obstacle:** Clause two requires a member of her line as surety, and Milo is the only one with standing.
- **Change:** She stands as her own surety, builds a genuinely sound filing, and is beaten on a technicality about standing she should have caught.
- **Consequence:** Milo is bound. She tried to protect him alone and failed, and he is proud of her anyway.

### Chapter Eleven — The Cutter's Price *(Episodes 25–26)*
- **Want:** Out, cleanly, now.
- **Obstacle:** Vera Odell's price is a deed that would cost Cyd something irreplaceable.
- **Change:** June refuses, then formally declines the cut on the record so nobody can do it on her behalf.
- **Consequence:** Declining is a documented election. She has chosen the bond once, publicly, which strengthens her position and destroys her deniability.

### Chapter Twelve — What He Owes *(Episodes 27–29)*
- **Want:** To stop needing him.
- **Obstacle:** Kearse acts on what he compelled out of Elias, and the blow lands where it hurts most.
- **Change:** June sees that his exposure endangers her, not only him, and they spend a counted night telling each other true things without compulsion.
- **Consequence:** Protecting herself now requires protecting him — the first obligation she cannot argue her way out of — and Constance has decided what he is for.

### Chapter Thirteen — The Record Room *(Episodes 30–31)* — **MIDPOINT**
- **Want:** A loophole in the sealed original.
- **Obstacle:** It is in the Trust's vault, and reading it means trespass and a witness.
- **Change:** She reads it and understands the Articles were drafted to stop a slaughter, that they worked, and that Constance still believes every clause was necessary.
- **Consequence:** Irreversible. There is no villain to expose and no fraud to reveal. Every strategy she had assumed bad faith, and Tess's bad-faith theory dies in front of her. The evidence survives; the case will have to become something harder.

### Chapter Fourteen — Sister Havlik *(Episodes 32–34)*
- **Want:** Her own people behind her.
- **Obstacle:** Marta has already decided three hundred eleven memberships outweigh one uninitiated niece.
- **Change:** The Hall votes by open roll call to honor the Articles. Kenneth Boyle says no, alone, and gives her the first account of her mother from someone who loved her.
- **Consequence:** Her own people spent her in a room with folding chairs, one man broke with his entire life to stand with her, and the published minutes have been altered by someone who is not Yolanda. The alteration protects June's future claim, tying it to the older protective gap in the roster without identifying the hand behind either.

### Chapter Fifteen — The Moon Roster *(Episodes 35–37)*
- **Want:** To survive her first moon window after being named, after giving away the first genuinely safe
  room she has ever obtained, without surrendering control of the plan.
- **Obstacle:** She gives the bought safe-room slot back to the man who needs it, leaving herself nothing, and the only offer that would work requires her to be inside a trustee's house on a night she cannot consent to anything.
- **Change:** She asks for help for the first time in the series, and survives the night under a perimeter she did not build alone.
- **Consequence:** The moon law is established as absolute, public and economically brutal; the attachment becomes something both have felt rather than been told about; and Elias is badly burned holding a position until sunrise.

### Chapter Sixteen — Aftermath *(Episodes 38–40)*
- **Want:** To keep one relationship in her life that is not transactional.
- **Obstacle:** She has been lying to Cyd since episode two, and Cyd has worked it out.
- **Change:** Cyd stays, furiously, on new and harder terms — and the agency loses a contract for it.
- **Consequence:** Cyd is exposed to lineage retaliation with no supernatural standing or protection, and it is entirely June's doing. Then a warmth beat, and then Delisle finds her by the coffee urn.

### Chapter Seventeen — Clause Three *(Episodes 41–42)*
- **Want:** More time.
- **Obstacle:** Delisle is required to read clause three from the parish copy: a witnessed vow within a term certain, or the bride forfeits. The Marrow Trust did not invoke it and does not know who did.
- **Change:** The third holder is revealed as Delisle's parish, by an enforceable 1871 custodial arrangement nobody now living remembers making.
- **Consequence:** The parish copy promises nineteen days, but Elias remembers nine in the sealed
  original. June's timely challenge triggers a mandatory ten-day stay at the authentic deadline, making
  the altered copy protective in practice without giving it power to amend the Articles.

### Chapter Eighteen — The Accomplice *(Episodes 43–45)*
- **Want:** Delisle to refuse to witness, so the vow cannot be completed under duress.
- **Obstacle:** Witnessing is his duty and he is procedurally rigid about duty.
- **Change:** He refuses — for his own reason. He has witnessed six and buried four, and will not be the instrument again.
- **Consequence:** Worse than she planned. With no accepted witness the vow cannot be completed at all, so
  forfeiture becomes automatic when the nine-day term and ten-day stay expire — and Tess has been waiting
  for exactly that. Elias proves the longer parish term was an old procedural attempt to protect a bride,
  not an amendment, but reaches June's apartment to find Constance already inside under a valid resident
  invitation.

### Chapter Nineteen — Forfeiture *(Episodes 46–47)* — **DARK NIGHT**
- **Want:** Get Milo back.
- **Obstacle:** The forfeiture is lawful, correctly executed, and carried out by people being scrupulously polite.
- **Change:** June begins to answer the vow aloud, freely and meaning it. Elias stops her before she can finish because no accepted witness is present and a clause is executing against her — then must admit he still overrode her physical autonomy.
- **Consequence:** The lowest point, because the forfeiture itself was executed correctly. Her cousin is in someone else's hands, the vow cannot lawfully restore him, and winning the moral argument with Elias changes none of those facts. Only a filing by the living bride remains.

### Chapter Twenty — The Reason *(Episodes 48–50)*
- **Want:** The truth, finally, in words she can quote back.
- **Obstacle:** Telling her risks what remains of her trust; turning that truth into formal testimony will permanently cost his office, and he has not yet decided where to lodge it.
- **Change:** He tells her without compulsion. He had read the clause that would have killed her that morning and speaking was the only lawful interruption — *and* he had wanted to for two years, from watching her work depositions he was sent to observe. Both true. He also reveals the prepared testimony that can support her filing.
- **Consequence:** The kindest reading is impossible and the cruellest is disproven, which is worse than either. June revokes and reissues his threshold invitation on her own terms, her misbelief breaks, and she calls Tess for the full case instead of looking for an exit.

### Chapter Twenty-One — The Third Outcome *(Episodes 51–52)*
- **Want:** Save Milo and defeat the active clauses without completing the vow or accepting forfeiture.
- **Obstacle:** Tess's case can only be filed by a living bride, so filing requires June to *remain* the
  bride — and before June chooses, Tess discloses that she supplied Lionel Kearse the theory that caused
  him to invoke Clause Three.
- **Change:** Knowing Tess helped create the injury she now asks June to use, June states her election,
  distinguishes witnessing a filing from witnessing a vow, and gets the case lodged before Delisle and
  Pratt.
- **Consequence:** She has chosen the obligation as an instrument — the one move the Articles never anticipated, because nobody had ever wanted the position before.

### Chapter Twenty-Two — In the Record *(Episodes 53–55)* — **CLIMAX**
- **Want:** Strike clauses one, two and three permanently.
- **Obstacle:** Constance, who wrote them, is not lying, is not cruel, and can still remember why each was necessary.
- **Change:** Before Delisle as witness to a filing rather than a vow, June reads the Articles back and defeats the first three clauses on their own terms. Elias's testimony makes the filing survive Kearse's challenge.
- **Consequence:** She wins, the count falls publicly to six, and Elias has disqualified himself from any office in perpetuity. Clause four remains Book Two's problem; the climax opens no new Book thread.

### Chapter Twenty-Three — Price Named *(Episodes 56–57)*
- **Want:** Make the victory durable by naming who paid for it before the room can celebrate or adjourn.
- **Obstacle:** The institutions would rather record the remedy than the people it cost, and Elias must face a future in which the only title he held is permanently unavailable.
- **Change:** Milo's release is confirmed. Marta loses the business agency. Kenneth is out of the Local for good. Elias's bar from office is repeated into the record. Marta transfers custody of the roster gap to June; Constance leaves Elias ordinary work, but no replacement title.
- **Consequence:** **The book's irreversible loss.** June is no longer a person things happen to, and four people paid for that. The people who lost their titles must enter the next book as people rather than offices.

### Chapter Twenty-Four — Six Left *(Episodes 58–60)*
- **Want:** Make the victory real in both registers, rebuild the ordinary life that survived it, and tell Elias exactly what she has chosen.
- **Obstacle:** The Trust register says six while the Hall ledger still says nine; Milo's release, June's election, the lease, and the agency all need concrete settlement before quiet can mean anything.
- **Change:** Both registers are reconciled at six. Milo comes home, Cyd stabilizes the agency, June
  negotiates her lease, and the released bride answers June's letter. On the span, June explicitly
  chooses to remain the bride without answering the vow or seeking Elias's abandonment; he accepts the
  boundary and retains his own costly choice.
- **Consequence:** The Book One ledger closes before the invitation forward. The letter advances the already-open maternal mystery: do not answer until she knows what her mother took. Only then does the final small sting land — an unreported severance involving a person neither register can name.

## Structural landmarks

- **Midpoint (Episode 30, Chapter Thirteen):** June reads the sealed original and learns the Articles are sincere. There is no villain to expose, no fraud to reveal, and no one to shame into stopping. Irreversible, because every strategy she had until this point assumed bad faith.
- **Dark night (Episodes 46–47, Chapter Nineteen):** Lawful forfeiture. Milo is taken as surety by people being polite; Delisle's refusal has closed the vow; and when June freely begins to answer, Elias stops her before completion because no accepted witness is present and a clause is executing against her. He still overrode her physical autonomy, she wins that moral argument, and it cannot restore Milo.
- **Climax (Episodes 53–55, Chapter Twenty-Two):** In the record, before a witness, against the woman who drafted the document. The choice inside it: June can break the Articles entirely by exposing what the 1871 signatories did, which would also void the peace and the charter — or defeat three clauses within the document's own logic and leave the peace standing. She takes the smaller, harder win.
- **Resolution (Chapters Twenty-Three and Twenty-Four):** The price is paid by four named people. Three clauses are struck. Six remain. June is a permanent party to the Articles by her own election.
- **Next-book turn:** The letter advances the already-open maternal mystery by warning June not to answer until she knows what her mother took. After the Book One resolution lands, the single final sting is the unreported cut in both registers — someone paid a cutter's price, and the deed was done to a person neither register can name.

## Five-movement design

The sixty-episode length is the declared Book One exception recorded in `BookCodex.json`: five
movements of twelve episodes. It is not permission for later books to drift past the fifty-episode
target.

1. **Setup and disruption — Episodes 1–12.** The half-vow lands in Episode One. June discovers her
   lack of standing, defeats clause one's residence demand, finds the seven-bride ledger, meets Tess,
   and learns from Elias's side that the Marrow Trust needs a completed attachment.
2. **Escalation and rising cost — Episodes 13–24.** June begins reading the borne record deliberately,
   her employment erodes, she compromises Elias through Kearse, and her attempt to defeat clause two
   alone ends with Milo bound as surety.
3. **Reversal and transformation — Episodes 25–36.** June rejects Vera's cut, learns the Articles are
   sincere, loses the Hall vote, gives back her purchased safe-room slot, and survives the moon only
   after asking for help. The midpoint reversal is Episode Thirty; the movement's behavioral turn is
   Episode Thirty-Six.
4. **Consequence and convergence — Episodes 37–48.** Cyd learns the truth, the third holder is
   identified, Delisle removes the only accepted witness, forfeiture takes Milo, and Elias gives his
   whole motive in his own words. No new Book thread opens after Episode Thirty-Seven.
5. **Final escalation, climax, and denouement — Episodes 49–60.** June chooses the third outcome,
   files Tess's case, permanently bars clauses one through three from execution, records every price, reconciles both
   registers at six, and states her continuing election before the small severance sting.

## Series horizon

**Book Two** turns on the missing thing. June's mother did not merely leave the Hall — she took
something out of it. Book One proves only that the act matters to the unanswered vow and is connected
to the protective gap Yolanda Bright has maintained for nineteen years; it does not yet establish what
was taken, why she left, or the full causal relationship to June's failed initiation. Clauses four through six come due
against a June who now has standing and allies, so the book's pressure shifts from *survival* to
*governance*: she is inside the institutions, which means her mistakes now cost other people first.
Marta, out of office, becomes either her best tactician or her most dangerous critic. Elias, barred
from office forever, has to become something other than an instrument, and neither of them knows what
that is. Book One plants: the roster gap, Yolanda's compromised minutes, Kenneth's
severance from the Local, Nadia's undisclosed house records, and Aloysius Pratt's twenty-two years of
being passed over.

**Book Three** opens the market. The cut trade stops being local tragedy and becomes visible as an
industry with brokers and a waiting list, and the accumulation of purchased attachments starts to
have a shape. This is where the slow-apocalypse question first gets touched — whether attachments are
dying out on their own or being suppressed — and it is never settled, only paid in instalments of
proven fact. The lineages begin competing openly for the few genuine attachments left, which converts
June from a contract obligation into a contested asset, and makes her half-spoken vow the most
valuable unfinished sentence in the country. Book One plants: Vera Odell's pricing
practice, the unreported cut in the register, Local Nine's two registrations in nineteen years, and
Kearse's interest in the Marrow Trust's failure.
