# Troy’s Bright Ideas

## Purpose

This is an idea bank for reader-facing bonus material, presentation experiments, and future production
choices for *The Half-Spoken Vow*. It is deliberately **not canon**. Nothing here changes a WorldState
fact, authorizes an episode reveal, or commits the series to a format until the author explicitly
approves it and records the decision in the appropriate production files.

The governing instinct: this series has an audience for both the romance-thriller surface and the
machinery underneath it. Some readers will enjoy the story without ever opening a supplement. Others will
want to metaphorically press F12 in the middle of a chapter and inspect the system that is trying to ruin
June’s life. Give the second group something real to find without making the first group do homework.

## The “F12” reader experience

The world should occasionally offer a reader-accessible technical layer: not a wiki dump, and not an
explanation the main narrative failed to provide, but an artifact that feels recovered from the world.

The current model is the Book One reader’s edition of the Articles:

- [The Articles of Agreement — Book One Reader’s Edition](../Assets/Articles/The-Articles-of-Agreement-1871-Book-One-Readers-Edition.md)

It is a good model because it gives readers the gears without handing them later-book spoilers. It
preserves the 1871 instrument’s recitals, governing rules, and the first three clauses. It also states
the Book One legal disposition: Clauses One through Three remain visible but are barred from execution.
The later clauses remain sealed.

The reader should feel that the book has handed them a restricted file, not a lore encyclopedia.

### Why this fits the series

The Articles are not merely a plot device. They are a one-hundred-and-fifty-year-old institutional system
with:

- an original purpose;
- competing records and interpretations;
- procedural constraints;
- bad actors and protective actors;
- precedent, tampering, and institutional memory; and
- consequences that remain real even when the people administering them mean well.

In software terms, they are an inherited, security-critical open-source codebase with multiple
maintainers, historical forks, contested behavior, and a specification nobody may casually rewrite.
Everybody knows the interface exists. Nobody has permission to treat a convenient interpretation as a
new implementation.

That is the series’ central legal-gothic pleasure. The documentation can make that pleasure tangible.

## Preferred format for in-world reader documents

When creating a bonus appendix, use the following structure where it fits:

```text
Appendix A
THE ARTICLES OF AGREEMENT
Executed 1871
Reader’s Reference Edition

Classification: Public Record / Partial Disclosure
Status: [current reader-safe status]
```

Then organize the artifact around what it does in the world, not around a generic lore taxonomy:

1. **Historical purpose** — why the document or institution exists.
2. **Governing principles** — the rules that make its logic legible.
3. **Material available for review** — the actual text, record, filing, minutes, or testimony.
4. **Status and access boundary** — what remains sealed, contested, unavailable, or outside the
   reader-safe record.

For the Articles, the historical purpose is the 1871 peace compact between Local Nine and the Marrow
Trust. The useful governing principles are:

- a supernatural obligation must enter the appropriate record before it attaches;
- trustees recognize written records, while members recognize borne and witnessed records;
- the Articles are the one instrument both systems accept;
- later practice may interpret an executed word but cannot amend the nine original clauses; and
- an attachment or bodily symptom never supplies consent.

## Spoiler boundary for the Articles

The reader edition must preserve a simple distinction:

| Material | Book One reader access |
| --- | --- |
| Recitals and reserved rights | Public in the appendix |
| Constitutional and procedural rules | Public in the appendix |
| Clauses One through Three | Public in the appendix |
| Book One disposition of Clauses One through Three | Public in the appendix |
| Clauses Four through Nine | Acknowledged as fixed and sealed; text withheld |
| Future-clause mechanisms, answers, and later-case outcomes | Not included |

The sealed material is not a tease pretending the author has not written it. It is a real boundary in the
instrument and a reader reward: the reader can inspect the rule that prevents a sealed clause from
executing against someone who has not received its text.

## Footnotes: document archaeology, not decoration

Footnotes could make later reader editions unusually fun. They should feel like records left by people
with different interests, not narration from above.

Good footnotes can:

1. reveal institutional bias;
2. clarify a rule a character or institution has misread; or
3. reward a careful reader who noticed a contradiction, alteration, or loaded word.

They should never exist merely because a footnote looks clever. They should not repeat the main text,
explain a joke, provide mandatory plot comprehension, or disclose a future payoff.

### Useful forms of annotation

- **Trust annotation:** formal, narrow, confident about writing, blind to bodily truth.
- **Local Nine annotation:** witnessed, practical, skeptical of paperwork, alert to what the body proves.
- **Custodial or parish annotation:** procedural, incomplete, concerned with preservation rather than
  outcome.
- **Editorial archival note:** clearly framed as a later reader edition, used sparingly to identify an
  alteration, missing page, or contested reading.

The ideal footnote creates a small second story about who has been arguing over a word for a century.
It is “git blame” for supernatural history.

### Footnote examples

> **1.** “Clause One” names sequence within the executed Articles, not importance. The distinction was
> repeatedly ignored during a later Trust review.

> **2.** The Marrow Trust accepts this sentence as written. Local Nine disputes the later construction,
> not the number of clauses.

> **3.** The third counterpart was unavailable for comparison at the time of this notation.

These examples demonstrate tone only. They are not canon and must not be inserted into a published
artifact without checking the governing source files and the reader’s spoiler position.

## Format rules across platforms

Traditional footnotes work best in ebook, paperback, WebNovel bonus chapters, a website, or a downloadable
reader packet. They do **not** belong in Pocket FM’s core episode-upload text, where a single narrator
would make them disruptive.

For audio, an optional future equivalent could be a separately labeled archival-note bonus episode or
reader supplement. It must remain optional: no listener may need it to understand the main story, a
character’s motivation, a rule that controls an episode, or the ending of a book.

The core principle is simple: artifacts deepen immersion; they may never repair missing exposition.

## Future artifact candidates

These are possibilities, not commitments:

- a later annotated edition of the Articles, once its annotations are reader-safe;
- selected Local Nine minutes, with their spoken-vote and witness logic intact;
- a Marrow Trust register excerpt showing offices, filings, and disputed entries;
- a parish custodial index or seal-inspection log;
- an estate inventory from the Sewickley house; or
- a limited “case file” packet that assembles documents already known to a reader at a specific book
  boundary.

Every artifact needs an authority label, a release point, and an explicit spoiler review before it goes
public.

## Working rule

The romance and thriller remain complete on the page. The document layer is forbidden candy for lore
goblins: optional, specific, slightly dangerous, and satisfying because the underlying story already
knows how its own systems work.
