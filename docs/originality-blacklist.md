# Originality Blacklist — Corpus Terms and Character Clusters to Avoid

**Status:** working document, assembled from the genre-research read of 21 works.
**Purpose:** a defensive checklist. Before any coined proper noun, faction name, taxonomy term,
rank, honorific, or signature phrase enters `WorldState/BookCodex.json`, check it against this file.

Genre conventions are shared inheritance and remain fair to use: fated mates, blood debts, arranged
marriage, clan politics, the moon cycle, courts, territories, bride-price, hostage-exchange.
What is listed below is *authored property* — a specific writer's invented vocabulary, taxonomy, or
recognizable character cluster.

Ordinary human given names are **not** collisions on their own. They become collisions only when
paired with the same role a source character holds. Those pairings are listed separately at the end.

## 2. Sourcebook vocabulary

See `notes/wod-sourcebooks.md` for the full blacklist once that read completes. Standing rule
regardless: avoid the *naming patterns* as well as the terms — compound animal-plus-noun faction
names, and a tiered breed/auspice/tribe taxonomy, read as derivative even with fresh words.

## 3. Signature phrases and formulas — DO NOT ECHO

- "I am the vampire ___" as an opening self-introduction (Rice)
- "the Dark Gift is given / taken"; "go into the fire" as the idiom for immortal suicide (Rice)
- "Law the First / Law the Second…" as a numbered legal liturgy (Kristoff)
- "Let the dark know my name and despair… And I am silver." (Kristoff's Vow, in whole or part)
- "The dead cannot kill the Dead" and the Five Laws as a set (Kristoff)
- "A blood foe is a blood foe is a blood foe"; "It is done."; "milaya" (Cole)
- "And may you never feel a Valkyrie's breath at your back" (Cole)
- "I stand Sentinel"; "Do you pledge fealty and allegiance…"; "Well played" as the protagonist's
  acknowledgement of a rival's manoeuvre (Neill)
- "Consent is irrelevant" as the functionary's line (Neill)
- "little wolf" as a lover's address (Arthur)
- "Your coffin away from home"; "Fang or human?" (Harris)
- "Not Prey don't run, don't hide, don't use distance weapons" (Adams & Clamp)
- Name-mangling of a vampire grandee as the heroine's running gag (Davidson) — *the technique is
  reusable; her specific manglings are not*

## 4. Naming-pattern prohibitions

Imitating the pattern is nearly as recognizable as imitating the term.

- Deadly-sin or abstract-noun warrior names with decorative silent letters (Ward: Wrath, Rhage,
  Zsadist, Phury, Tohrment, Vishous). Also fails the audio test — near-homophones of common words.
- "Blood ___" or "of the Blood ___" for a lineage (Kristoff)
- "the ___ Gift" for a power (Rice)
- "The [Epithet]" as a standing character label: the Radiant, the Ice Maiden, the Coveted One,
  the Gravewalker, the Cruel (Cole)
- Compound animal-plus-noun faction names (sourcebook pattern)
- Mononymic vampire whose single name *is* her rank, enforced by a naming law (Neill)
- Pseudo-French or pseudo-archaic sprinkling inside otherwise modern English narration (Kristoff)
- Twin-wolf and twin-moon heraldry; any "Empress/Emperor of Wolves and Men" formation
  (**Kristoff's Blood Chastain — the single sharpest package collision for a werewolf/vampire
  dynastic story: avoid the crest, the title, and the house together and separately**)

## 6. Ages, turnings, and origin wounds already taken

Do not reuse a source character's apparent age, century of birth, age at turning, or the
distinctive specifics of their origin wound. Notable ones to steer clear of:

- Turned at ~25 in 1791, Louisiana, after mocking a visionary brother who then died (Rice, Louis)
- Turned in the 1780s Auvergne by a maker who walked into fire the same night (Rice, Lestat)
- Apparent 17 forever, stolen from a mentor and rebuilt by a satanic coven (Rice, Armand)
- Born 1st-century Rome, bound two millennia to guard two silent ancients (Rice, Marius)
- Made at sixteen; bastard son beaten by a father he resembled exactly (Kristoff, Gabriel)
- ~300 years dead, bargained on his own deathbed for his brothers' lives (Cole, Wroth)
- Sentenced at seventeen to wear an unbreakable chain for kissing a demigod (Cole, Myst)
- Parents murdered in front of him pre-transition; refuses an inherited throne (Ward, Wrath)
- Stolen as an infant, kept as a blood slave, tortured a century (Ward, Zsadist)
- Half-breed expelled from her pack at adulthood by her own mother (Arthur, Riley)
- Turned without consent to save her life, and stripped of a doctoral career the same week
  (Neill, Merit)
- Killed by a car while rescuing her cat, buried in a stepmother's cast-off shoes (Davidson, Betsy)
- Bitten while killing a vampire queen, leaving a permanent psychic buzz (Adams & Clamp, Kate)
- Murdered in his own house and bound to it, dying again each sunrise (Caine, Michael)

## 7. Structural setups to change if used at all

- **The testimony/interrogation frame.** Kristoff's exact setup is a vampire scribe recording a
  doomed narrator's confession in a locked cell. Rice's is a boy with a tape recorder in a rented
  room. If a testimony frame is used, change the interrogator, the stakes, and the location — and
  give it a countdown, since both sources use it to license unlimited digression.
- **A talented human woman both factions need in order to reproduce** (Adams & Clamp). The engine
  is reusable and excellent; every noun attached to it must be reinvented, and our scarcity should
  arise from something other than female sterility plus human surrogacy.
- **Consort-by-fine-print** — an intimacy that silently confers a crown (Davidson). Structurally a
  consent violation; do not reproduce.
- **A rulebook the protagonist is handed** (Neill's Canon). Convert to an oral instrument — a
  spoken oath or formula that must be said correctly — so it is performed aloud rather than read.

## 8. THE "BRIDE" PROBLEM — read before naming anything

Two works in the corpus sit directly on our premise's central word, and they constrain it from
opposite directions.

**Hamilton uses "Bride" as a taxonomic term.** In the late Anita Blake books a capital-B **Bride** is
a human psychically enslaved to a vampire — a thrall, explicitly expected to be thrown at enemies as
a disposable delaying tactic. That is a coined taxonomy label for chattel, and it is the single most
dangerous collision in the corpus for us, because it occupies the exact word our premise is built on.

**Roberts owns "The Lost Bride"** as a trilogy title and a manor name, attached to a curse in which a
bride of the line dies every generation and the witch keeps her stolen wedding ring.

**Resolution — the rule I will design to:**

1. **"Bride" stays descriptive, never taxonomic.** She is a bride the way a person is a widow: it
   names her situation, not her species or her rank. No capital-B "Brides" as a class of bonded
   servants.
2. **The bonded party gets its own coined term**, which must not be "Bride," and must not read as a
   synonym for thrall, servant, or property. The word should carry legal and ceremonial weight —
   something closer to an office or an oath than a possession.
3. **Do not name the estate, house, keep, or season after a bride.** No "Lost Bride," no
   "Bride of [place]," no manor named for a dead wife.
4. **Do not use the stolen-wedding-ring-per-victim curse mechanic.** A bounded countable collection
   is a superb engine and we should use one — but the tokens must not be rings taken from murdered
   brides.
5. Avoid **"Mother of ___"** primordial titles, **animal-clade rank words in a foreign language**
   (Hamilton's signature taxonomy), **italicised French common nouns as power names**, and
   **French diminutive pet names from a vampire lord to a mortal heroine**.

## 10. Ordinary names paired with supernatural court roles — avoid the pairing

These first names are ordinary and free to use in ordinary roles. What is claimed is the *pairing*
with a supernatural court function: **Willie** (vampire snitch), **Buzz** (vampire bouncer),
**Monica** (vampire-adjacent lawyer), **Rafael** (rodent-clade king), **Richard** (werewolf leader
who is also a doctor), **Nicky** (werelion bond-servant), **Larry**, **Jason**, **Kane**, **Angel**,

**Deuce** and **Ace** (small-town men), **Yoda** / **Mookie** (plot-bearing dogs).

## 11. Feehan and Briggs — the most-owned words in the subgenre

These two carry the highest collision risk of anything in the corpus, because they are the standard
vocabulary of exactly our premise.

**Feehan — absolute avoids:**
- **"lifemate"** — the single most owned word in the subgenre. Also *life mate*, *lifemates*,
  *true lifemate*, and near-clones (*lifebond*, *life-half*, *lifesworn*).
- **Carpathian** as a species name — and the *pattern* of naming a lineage after a real mountain range
- **"I claim you as my ___"** as a vow opener, and any close paraphrase of her binding words
- "Do you enter my home of your own free will?" as a threshold formula
- **the darkness** (as the encroaching emptiness in unmated males) · **loss of color vision** as its
  symptom · "the light to his darkness" · "my other half" / "his missing half"
- blood-red tears as grief · the welcoming earth / healing soil · **safeguards** (as the warding noun) ·
  **sentries** as the ruler's wolf guard · **mental push** vs. **command** as a named distinction ·
  **the undead** as the formal in-world noun for a fallen immortal
- **"Prince of the [species]"** as a ruling title · "the dark one" as an epithet
- Verbal tells: "little one" as the hero's constant endearment · a voice described as "black velvet" ·
  eyes as "black obsidian" hers "sapphire darkening to purple"
- Surname **Dubrinsky**

**Briggs — absolute avoids:**
- **Omega** as a werewolf rank outside the dominance hierarchy. She originated this usage in werewolf
  romance. Avoid the term, the "an Omega / Omegas are rare" framing, and her Alpha/Beta/Omega ladder.
  **The function is adaptable and valuable; the name and framing are not.**
- **the Marrok** — and lookalike single-word Welsh or Arthurian ruler-titles
- **"mating bond"** as the term (genre-generic largely *because* of her) · her formulation
  "love wasn't necessary — acceptance was, trust was"
- **Brother Wolf** — and "Brother/Sister [animal]" for the inner beast generally
- **the Beast** capitalised for the thing inside the ruler · **the Change / Changed by force**
- **Aspen Creek** · the Cabinet Wilderness / Montana-plus-Tri-Cities setting cluster
- A dead-language private defiance code (her Latin) — tempting and specifically hers
- Bond-theft by witches who gain longevity from a mate's tortured death, and the ashes-mixed-with-salt
  countermeasure. **The bond-harvesting engine is excellent and adaptable; this mechanism is not.**
- Surnames **Cornick** and **Latham** (the latter paired with a werewolf bride)

**Character clusters:** an ~1,300-year-old rose-growing Moorish wolf who wants to die (Asil) ·
a ruler who looks like a college student and deliberately married a woman he cannot love (Bran) ·
the first werewolf born rather than Changed, serving 150 years as his father's assassin (Charles) ·
a Vietnam-veteran hermit wolf who dies protecting the heroine (Walter) · a beloved veterinarian
Changed without consent whose funeral opens the book (Doc Wallace)

**Ages already taken:** turned by force three years ago in her early twenties (Anna) · born c. 1800s,
"almost two hundred" (Charles) · older than 1,300 years (Bran, Asil) · born c. 1300s and the oldest of
his kind (Mikhail) · converted mid-story as the last hope of a dying race (Raven)

Naturae · and the named animal totems (Falcon, Stag, Unicorn, Crow, Raven, Rat, Owl, Chimera)

### Naming *patterns* — imitating these is nearly as recognizable as reusing the words

1. `[Modifier] + [animal anatomy or agent-noun]` as a faction name — Silver Fangs, Red Talons, Bone
   Gnawers. "Iron Fangs" or "Pale Stalkers" read as theirs instantly. Avoid the construction.
2. `Get of [mythic name]` · `Children of [abstraction]` · `[Plural] of [Place]` faction formulas.
3. Archaic-spelled abstract nouns as cosmic forces sharing an initial letter (Wyld / Weaver / Wyrm).
   No alliterating trinity of pseudo-archaic abstractions.
4. Coined stem + **-ling** for tiers of otherworld being.
5. Latinate cosmological coinages in **-a / -an / -ium** — especially any Latin word for shade or
   shadow used as the name of the other world.
6. A rank ladder of mixed Gaelic and Greek loanwords; a ladder of Old-Irish-looking monosyllables.
7. Hyphenated honorific suffixes appended to names to mark relative station — the *mechanism*.
8. Personal names as hyphenated epithet-phrases: `[Noun]-of-[Noun]` and `[Noun][Verb-er]` compounds
   (Heart-of-Fury; Thunderstrike, Skyhowler).
9. Moon phase determining personality and social role, with roles named for the phases.
10. `Rite of ___` / `Howl of ___` / `Song of ___` / `Dirge for ___` as the ceremonial convention.
11. Single-syllable pseudo-Celtic place-nouns for sacred sites.
12. A tri-syllabic Latin or Greek word as the capitalized proper name of the law code. "The Litany" —
    and its obvious swaps "the Canticle," "the Rubric," "the Dictum," are equally out.
    *(Note: an ordinary legal term such as "articles of agreement" is a different register — mundane
    contract law, not mystical scripture — and is acceptable.)*
13. Number-locked taxonomies used as branding ("the Thirteen Tribes"). Our counts must differ **and**
    the count must not be part of the name.
14. `[Enemy]-tainted` as the corruption adjective.
15. A tri-part reputation currency of abstract capitalized virtues.
