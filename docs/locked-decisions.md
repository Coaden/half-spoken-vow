# Locked Series Decisions

**Status:** approved by the author, 2026-08-18. These are inputs to `WorldState/BookCodex.json`
and `AGENTS.md`. Do not re-litigate; changing one requires an author decision and a CHANGELOG entry.

## 0. Title — *The Half-Spoken Vow*

`title_status: "working"`. Author-chosen, 2026-08-18. The working folder name
`WerewolfVampireBride` is a label, not the title, and must not appear in any published material.

Names the duet-vow mechanic without assigning a pronoun, so it stays accurate whichever half is
currently unanswered, and describes the vow itself rather than one beat in episode one. Passes
`docs/originality-blacklist.md`: no "Blood ___", "Dark ___", "Lover ___", or "Lost Bride" pattern.
Three stresses, no phonetic collisions, clean truncation in an app list.

## 1. Heat and sensuality

Source: `docs/sex-sensuallity-policy.md` (authoritative — read it directly, this is a summary).

Steamy at the upper end of the tier, cut at the threshold. Pocket FM does carry explicitly adult
supernatural and dark-romance material, so sex is not prohibited — but no sufficiently specific
public author guideline defines the graphic line, so **the series is not architected to test that
boundary.**

On page / on audio: attraction, sexual tension, kissing, undressing, intimate touching, desire in
dialogue, waking together, aftermath, bite and mark imagery, vulnerability, and the emotional
consequences of sex. When a scene crosses into sustained graphic description of the act itself,
**transition through it** rather than narrating physical detail.

This is **not** a fade-to-black-when-clothes-come-off rule. Intimate scenes have beginnings,
escalation, emotional turns, and aftermath, and real character or plot development may occur inside
them. The restraint targets graphic anatomical and mechanical description, not the fact of sex.

Heat escalates **across the series**: early episodes make proximity and almost-contact valuable;
consummation is earned, not spent in the acquisition runway. Apply the **read-it-aloud test** —
sensual and intense is good; language that becomes awkwardly anatomical when spoken is rewritten
or elided.

## 2. Setting — contemporary hidden world

Now, our world, supernatural courts operating behind ordinary institutions.

**The ordinary modern world stays materially consequential.** Housing, money, work, hospitals, law
enforcement, paperwork, phones, travel, reputation, family obligations, and comparable pressures
**cannot disappear because supernatural politics are happening.** These are load-bearing, not
set dressing: they supply the practical-pressure layer `AGENTS.md` requires beneath every
supernatural beat, and the supernatural world's inability to simply magic them away is the point.

Secondary benefit: modern institutions give the lineages' ancient customs something to collide with.

## 3. Mate-bond — real but incomplete

**The body-layer is objectively real and arrives involuntarily.** Physical and emotional symptoms
are facts: ache across distance, scent-fixation, insomnia apart, involuntary tracking, rage when the
other is touched. Nobody can argue these away.

**The person-layer requires an affirmative, spoken, witnessed act from both parties, freely and
knowingly given.** The bond does not complete without it. **Before completion, refusal is absolute** —
either party may withhold their half indefinitely, for any reason or none, and no pressure, symptom,
clause, or debt can supply it for them.

**Completion is irrevocable** (author revision, 2026-08-18). Once both halves are given the bond can
never be dissolved: no annulment, no severance, no cutter reaches it. Because it cannot be undone the
consent conditions are correspondingly strict — both halves spoken freely and knowingly before an
accepted witness, separated by an interval, with each speaker able to state the five permanent facts in
their own words; void if either party is compelled, exposed and compellable, intoxicated, dying,
unaware of the permanence, or has a clause executing against them.

**What actually becomes permanent, narrowly:** direction-and-distance awareness of each other; knowing
when the other is dying; the mortal party ceasing to age while remaining entirely killable; the
inability to ever form another attachment; and a permanent entry in both registers conferring standing
that cannot be struck. It guarantees **no** love, obedience, fidelity, compatibility, allegiance,
proximity, or happiness, and no telepathy, shared emotion, or compulsion. Bonded people may separate,
betray each other, or become enemies. Completion also forfeits both parties' capacity to swear any
other binding oath, so a bonded trustee can never hold office and a bonded member can never be
initiated. The permanence creates story; it never settles one.

**Symptoms are never consent.** The supernatural phenomenon is fact; everything societies claim
those symptoms *entitle* anyone to — law, religion, custom, propaganda, politics — is
interpretation, and may simply be wrong. What a completed bond obliges, which court holds
jurisdiction, whether an incomplete bond can be sold, taxed, inherited, dissolved, or harvested,
whether marrying over a bond is a sacrament or a seizure: all contested, all arguable, all
re-arguable. **The magic is fact; the law is fiction; the bride stands where the two meet.**

### The duet vow

The claiming vow has two halves. **Speaking one half binds only the speaker** — stripping that
person's protections and exposing them before the other has responded. Speaking first is
vulnerability, not possession.

**Consequence discipline (author note, binding):** if an unanswered vow persists across many
episodes, the intervening episodes must deliver **new consequences and payoffs created by the
unanswered vow** — altered alliances, protections, legal standing, enemies, family pressure — rather
than repeating the question of whether the second party will answer. The question survives because
other things keep paying off around it.

## 4. POV — dual, bride-weighted

- Dual POV, **approximately 70/30 across the series**, possibly closer to **80/20 early in Book One**
  while her counterpart remains more mysterious, loosening as the series proceeds.
- **Third person limited, past tense.**
- His POV exists to **reveal information unavailable from hers** — never to replay a scene we just
  watched from her side, and never to prematurely explain away his ambiguity.
- **One POV per scene; preferably one POV per episode where practical. No head-hopping.**
- On a POV change, the opening **sentence or two identifies the viewpoint character naturally.** The
  episode map labels POV already, so the prose reads as prose, not as a database export.

## 5. Durability rules (Hamilton-derived, with author adjustments)

- **At least one major irreversible loss or consequence per season.** Death may satisfy it but is
  **never a quota** — a scheduled death becomes predictable and therefore costless. Other valid
  satisfactions: exile, permanent estrangement, loss of status or territory, destruction of a
  sanctuary, a relationship that genuinely cannot be repaired, forfeiture of a power, or another
  irreversible cost.
- **"Nothing new opens in a finale"** means finales introduce **no new major mystery, antagonist, or
  story engine.** They close the current ledger. A **small forward-looking sting after the payoff is
  allowed**, so the serial still invites the next episode or season. Finales overwhelmingly pay
  debts; they do not take out new loans.
- Never grant a power without retiring one. Fixed slot count.
- Escalate proximity, intimacy of betrayal, publicity, irreversibility, and moral compromise —
  never raw threat level.
- Three inviolable physical laws, named in canon, never excepted even as a reward.
- One title per character, forever. Titles may be lost, never accumulated.
- Ensemble discipline: a named recurring roster of 16 (7 principals plus 9 recurring), with at most 6
  appearing in any mini-arc and the rest explicitly elsewhere on named business. At most 6 foreground
  speaking voices and 6 spoken names per episode. Additions only against retirements, two per season
  maximum. The binding constraint is per-episode and per-arc presence, not roster size — the failure
  being prevented is the greeting tax, not the headcount.
- Closure discipline: close three threads for every one opened; never more than 7 open at once, of
  which at most 3 are protected mysteries and the other 4 each carry a written closing episode number.
- Attention-concentration floor: the top 5 characters hold at least 55% of name-mentions per season.

## 6. The lineage-swap test — canon rule

**If a major supernatural scene would play essentially the same with the wolf and vampire roles
exchanged, their cultural ecology is insufficiently differentiated.**

The two lineages are not character classes with different combat stats. Their ideas about **time,
family, authority, punishment, intimacy, and obligation** must actually generate different behavior.
Working axis from the research: one lineage's time is cyclical, public, and calendar-fixed; the
other's is linear, secret, and accrues with age. One exiles, the other excommunicates. One believes
love is *found*; the other believes it is *granted*.

## 7. The "bride" vocabulary constraint

See `docs/originality-blacklist.md` §8. "Bride" stays **descriptive** — naming her situation the way
"widow" does — never taxonomic. Hamilton owns capital-B "Bride" as a term for a psychically enslaved
human; Roberts owns "The Lost Bride." The bonded office therefore needs its own coined term reading
as an oath or office rather than a possession. No estate named for a bride. No
stolen-token-per-murdered-bride curse mechanic.

## 8. The Articles are immutable text

All nine operative clauses existed in the three counterparts executed in 1871. Later brides and later
offices may create interpretations, constructions, precedents, depositions, declarations, execution
records, and judgments. Those supplementary records may govern application, expose an error, or defeat
a clause, but they may never add, remove, reorder, rewrite, or amend one of the nine clauses.

Naming a bride is an antecedent act governed by the constitutional portion of the Articles. It is not
Clause Three and does not itself activate any numbered clause.

## 9. Clause Three's authentic term and procedural stay

Clause Three's authentic 1871 term is **nine calendar days**. The parish counterpart was later altered
to read **nineteen**. That alteration did not amend the shared obligation.

A timely challenge to a material discrepancy among the counterparts triggers the Articles' own mandatory
**ten-calendar-day stay** of the disputed consequence. The stay preserves the parties while authenticity
is adjudicated; it does not rewrite or restart the underlying nine-day term. Nine days plus the mandatory
stay therefore produces nineteen days in practice. The alteration was deliberately protective and was
made by somebody who understood this procedure. The person's identity and exact motive remain a tracked
answer rather than license to invent a new clause.

## 10. Clause Three's invocation chain

Tess Renn has no standing to invoke Clause Three. Lionel Kearse possesses the required standing through
his recognized rival-trust office and derivative claim. Tess identifies the opportunity and persuades
Lionel that activation and forfeiture will weaken the Marrow Trust. Lionel knowingly invokes for that
institutional purpose; Tess instigates because a perfected injury to a living bride is what her
eleven-year case requires.

This is revealed to June before she elects to remain the living bride and carry Tess's case. Tess is not
secretly evil: she repeats the institutions' moral failure by deciding that June and Milo's risk is
justified by a larger purpose. Lionel is not a dupe and Elias did not arrange June's naming or Clause
Three's invocation.

## 11. The three vow states and abandonment

The vow has exactly three legal states:

1. **Abandoned:** the speaker formally withdraws the recorded half and pays the stated price. The
   half-vow and its exposure end.
2. **Half-bound:** the spoken half remains in force. Its speaker is bound and exposed; the recipient is
   not bound and owes no answer. The involuntary attachment continues independently.
3. **Completed:** both people freely and validly speak their halves. The completed bond is irrevocable.

Formal abandonment before completion means **excommunication**: permanent removal from the speaker's
lineage record, including lineage office, succession, institutional protection, and enforcement
standing. It does not erase ordinary civil identity or individually held civil property, and it does
not sever the involuntary attachment; cutting remains a separate act.

Elias does not abandon his half in Book One. June cannot abandon words she never spoke. She chooses not
to answer and not to seek or compel his abandonment, leaving them half-bound. Book One closes that
immediate election cleanly while preserving the vow's ultimate disposition for Volume Two.

## 12. June's prior transformations — locked

June first changed at sixteen and has changed on the three public moon nights for fifteen years before
Episode One. Paperwork, roster status, and lack of initiation never delayed or suppressed her body.

Before disappearing when June was twelve, Rose taught her the warning signs and the bare physical rules
needed to survive: clear the room, remove glass and loose objects, anchor the door, leave water, and do not
trust changed speech as consent. Rose did not teach her Local Nine's institutions, the Articles, or why
the family line would be closed.

As an adult June recreated a poor private version of collective care. She cleared and reinforced a small
interior room, blocked all three nights from paid work, anchored the door from the human side, left water,
and recovered alone. The arrangement had no exterior lock, attendant, wage coverage, medical check, or
reliable emergency release. She knew how to survive changing. She did not know how wolves were meant to
live together.

Cyd believed June's recurring absences were a private medical condition and remained ignorant until the
existing Book One disclosure. Milo knew June changed but assumed she had a private arrangement and never
saw or assisted with it. Marta and the Hall knew from June's body that she changed and never investigated
how an uninitiated kinswoman contained herself.

When June moved into the Polish Hill apartment at twenty-six, Kenneth saw enough of the unsafe routine to
leave an anonymous card for a private containment room. He did not identify himself or explain Local Nine,
honoring Rose's demand that he not draw June back into the Hall. June read the card as predatory or
fraudulent solicitation and threw it away.

Room six is the first purpose-built safe room June has ever obtained. Episode Three reveals Local Nine's
safe rooms, attendants, housing roster, wage support, recovery help, and collective planning—not wolf
biology. After seeing that infrastructure, June will no longer accept her apartment routine as adequate;
giving room six back and asking for help are therefore genuine character growth rather than first-change
panic.
