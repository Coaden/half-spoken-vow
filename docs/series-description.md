# The Half-Spoken Vow — Series Description (Pocket FM listing)

## PRIMARY (use this one)

June Havlik is paid to notice exactly how people word things. Twelve years, eleven hundred depositions, not one transcript ever returned.

Then a witness reads a hundred-and-fifty-year-old agreement into the record — nine clauses, a bride owed in every generation — and the eighth name is hers.

June is a wolf. Nobody ever taught her what that means. Her mother's exile closed the line before she could be initiated, so she has no pack, no standing, and no safe room on the three nights a month her body stops being her own — while every member of the Hall can smell exactly whose daughter she is, and they voted to hand her over anyway.

The trustee sent to enforce the contract does not read the clause. He speaks half a binding vow instead. It strips his own protections, buys her time she never asked for, and makes her the most valuable woman in the city — and he will not say why he did it.

She has not answered him.

Until she does, the clauses come due on her one at a time. Four of the seven women before her died under them.

The bond is real, and it did not ask permission. But what anyone claims that bond entitles them to is only law — and law is just wording, and wording is the one thing June has never lost an argument about.

## SHORT (for character-limited placements)

A court reporter hears her own name read into the record as the eighth bride owed under a contract signed in 1871. June Havlik is a wolf with no pack, no standing, and no protection — and the vampire trustee sent to enforce the clause speaks half a binding vow instead, exposing himself to save her and refusing to say why. She has not answered. Four of the seven women before her died under those clauses. The bond is real. What it entitles anyone to is only law, and law is just wording.

## ONE-LINE LOGLINE

A court reporter named as the eighth bride under a century-and-a-half-old contract fights it with the only weapon she has: the exact wording.
