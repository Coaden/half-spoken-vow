  ## Story outline — working explanation

  June Havlik is a thirty-one-year-old deposition transcriptionist in present-day Pittsburgh. She is precise,
  financially insecure, and accustomed to being invisible while other people make statements that become legal
  records. During an ordinary probate deposition, a witness reads from the Articles, an agreement signed in 1871
  between Local Nine and the Marrow Trust. The Articles require a bride in every generation. Seven women have
  already been named; four died under its clauses, two were released, and one disappeared. June learns that she
  is the eighth.

  June is a wolf by blood, but she was never initiated into Local Nine because her mother left under unexplained
  circumstances. She therefore has no membership, vote, benefits, safe room, or legal standing within her own
  lineage. Everyone in the Hall can smell that she is a Havlik, yet their official roster says she does not
  exist.

  Elias Marrow is the vampire trustee sent to place the first clause into effect. Instead, he speaks the first
  half of a binding vow to June before an accepted witness. The attachment between them is real and involuntary,
  but completing it requires June’s freely spoken answer. Elias’s action does not bind June; it strips away his
  own protections and exposes him to compulsion by anyone with standing. It also interrupts a clause that would
  have killed her. June cannot determine whether he rescued her or claimed her for his own reasons, and he
  refuses to explain his full motive.

  The Articles contain nine clauses that come due in sequence. June begins fighting them through the skills she
  already possesses: exact wording, research, procedure, filings, and witnesses. She defeats the first clause,
  which requires her to reside in the Marrow Trust’s house, by finding an 1871 definition that allows her to
  satisfy it through a limited number of recorded nights without surrendering her apartment.

  Inside the house, June discovers a secret steward’s ledger recording the seven earlier brides and the clauses
  that killed four of them. She also meets Tess Renn, sister of the seventh bride. Tess has spent eleven years
  preparing a legal challenge that only a living bride can file. June learns that every clause was created in
  response to an earlier woman’s attempt to resist, turning the Articles into a history of precedent and damage
  rather than an unknowable curse.

  At the same time, June begins recognizing that what she has always called professional instinct is her ability
  to read the wolves’ “borne record”: fear, illness, violence, desire, and kinship carried by the body. This is
  not a new combat power. It is a literacy she has possessed without understanding, and learning to use it makes
  her capable of navigating both supernatural systems—the wolves’ witnessed bodily truth and the vampires’
  written records.

  The pressure spreads into June’s ordinary life. Clients abandon her, her income collapses, and her closest
  friend and employer, Cyd Toomey, suffers business losses because she refuses to fire June. The approaching full
  moon creates another deadline: members change involuntarily for three nights, but June has no place on Local
  Nine’s safe-room roster. She eventually survives her first unprotected change only by asking for help from
  Kenneth Boyle and Elias. Elias remains at his assigned boundary until sunrise and suffers serious burns from
  direct sunlight.

  The second clause requires a member of June’s bloodline to act as surety. June tries to protect her cousin Milo
  by serving as her own surety, but her argument fails because she lacks official standing. Milo volunteers and
  becomes bound in her place. June is then offered escape by Vera Odell, a cutter who can permanently sever an
  incomplete attachment. June refuses because Vera’s price is not money but a deed that would cause Cyd an
  irreplaceable loss. June formally records that refusal, unintentionally strengthening her position as someone
  choosing to remain attached for the moment.

  At the midpoint, June discovers that the Articles are not fraudulent. Constance Marrow drafted them after a
  slaughter between the two lineages, and the agreement genuinely stopped the violence. Constance still believes
  the clauses are necessary, while Local Nine believes honoring them is required to preserve the membership, safe
  rooms, benefits, and burial fund of its three hundred eleven members. June cannot expose a simple villain
  because both institutions are protecting real people while sacrificing her.

  Local Nine votes openly to honor the Articles. Kenneth is the only member who votes against surrendering June,
  and his dissent eventually costs him his place in the Local. June also discovers that somebody altered the
  Hall’s records in a way that secretly protects her future claim. The alteration is connected to a nineteen-year
  gap in the roster and to whatever happened when her mother left, but that mystery remains unresolved in Book
  One.

  The third copy of the Articles is eventually found in Father Bram Delisle’s parish. Its version of clause three
  imposes a deadline: June must complete a witnessed vow or face forfeiture. Delisle has witnessed six such
  marriages and buried four brides. He refuses ever to witness another vow. His moral refusal protects June from
  forced completion but also makes lawful completion impossible, causing forfeiture to become automatic.

  Milo is taken as June’s surety through a procedurally flawless execution. Desperate to save him, June begins
  speaking her half of the vow freely. Elias physically stops her because no accepted witness is present and a
  clause is actively executing against her, making completion invalid. June correctly confronts him for
  overriding her physical autonomy, but winning that argument cannot bring Milo home.

  Elias finally reveals his entire motive without compulsion. He had read the lethal clause scheduled for June,
  and the half-vow was the only lawful way to interrupt it. But he had also watched June work for two years and
  already wanted her. Rescue and desire were both involved, and even Elias cannot separate them. He then offers
  testimony that can support Tess’s case, knowing it will permanently disqualify him from holding office.

  June finds a third outcome. Instead of completing the vow or escaping the Articles, she knowingly elects to
  remain the living bride so she can file Tess’s case herself. She uses the two lineages’ incompatible
  definitions and the precedents of the earlier brides to defeat clauses one, two, and three on their own terms.
  She refuses the larger option of destroying the entire agreement because doing so could also destroy the peace
  and Local Nine’s charter. Milo is released, the first three clauses are permanently struck, and both
  supernatural registers record that six remain.

  The victory has named costs. Marta loses leadership of Local Nine. Kenneth is permanently exiled for his
  dissent. Elias loses eligibility for every future office. June becomes a permanent party to the Articles and
  cannot simply walk away from the remaining clauses.

  Book One ends with June deliberately leaving the vow incomplete. She neither answers Elias nor abandons him.
  Instead, they form a bounded alliance in which her refusal is respected and their relationship can develop
  without the bond deciding it for them. June contacts one of the released brides and receives a warning: she
  must not answer Elias until she discovers what her mother took from the Hall.

  The continuing series follows June as clauses four through six come due against someone who now has standing
  and allies. Book Two centers on her mother’s departure, the missing item or record, and June’s transition from
  merely surviving the institutions to influencing them. A larger thread involves a hidden market in severed
  attachments. By Book Three, June learns that someone is buying those severed bonds and that genuine attachments
  are becoming dangerously rare, making her unfinished vow one of the most valuable supernatural claims in the
  country.

  The cleanest skeleton for your eventual 3,500-character version is:

  1. June is named the eighth bride and Elias interrupts the lethal clause with half a vow.
  2. She has no wolf standing and must defeat the Articles through wording and procedure.
  3. Clauses one through three progressively cost her shelter, livelihood, allies, and Milo.
  4. She learns the Articles sincerely preserve peace, eliminating the easy-villain solution.
  5. Elias admits he acted from both protection and desire.
  6. June chooses a third outcome and defeats three clauses without completing the vow.
  7. The victory costs four people their positions, leaves six clauses, and opens the mystery of her mother and
     the severed-bond market.
