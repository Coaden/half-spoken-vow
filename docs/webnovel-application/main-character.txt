Volume One uses a dual third-person viewpoint, with 48 episodes told from June’s perspective and 12 from Elias’s.  Later volumes may give Elias more viewpoint as his independent story grows, but June remains the central protagonist, and Elias would never receive more than half of the narrative.

June Havlik is a 31-year-old deposition transcriptionist living in Pittsburgh. She is a wolf by blood but was never initiated into Local Nine after her mother left under unexplained circumstances. June therefore has no membership, vote, benefits, safe room, or standing within the institution. She is also financially vulnerable: she has an unstable income, a month-to-month lease, and a professional reputation that depends on remaining unnoticed in other people’s legal disputes.

June is precise, observant, stubborn, and quietly funny. She listens more than she speaks, remembers exact wording, and immediately notices when someone’s response doesn’t exactly answer a question. She dislikes being managed and assumes that accepting help will place her in someone else’s debt. She’s instinctively independent and prefers to handle problems alone, even when doing so makes the situation worse.

June does not become physically powerful or develop supernatural abilities. Her strength comes from the skills she already uses professionally: language, research, records, procedure, and witnesses. As the story progresses, she realizes she has also been unconsciously yielding innate wolf abilities, sensing fear, illness, recent violence, desire, and blood kinship, the way she reads deponents' misleading language as part of her job. Combining her abilities and professional skill allows her to understand both supernatural systems: the vampires’ written records and the wolves’ witnessed bodily truth, and the legal system that governs their peace.

Her central dilemma is that completing the vow could end her immediate danger, but it would be permanent and would prevent her from ever being initiated into Local Nine. June wants standing and protection, but her two routes to achieving these cancel each other out.

Her arc culminates when she elects to remain the living bride so she can file Tess Renn’s case and defeat the first three clauses, offering sacrifice for resolution.  She neither completes her half of the vow nor abandons the vow, leaving the law in a state of limbo while maintaining her consent and boundaries intact by accepting a responsibility chosen herself.

Elias Marrow is the story's male protagonist. He appears to be mid 30’s but is actually 158 years old, having become a vampire trustee in 1868. He is the Marrow Trust’s trustee responsible for reading clauses into effect and has participated in the deaths of earlier brides. Elias is formal, literal, controlled, and courteous enough to be intimidating. He answers the question asked rather than the question intended and has spent most of his existence treating obedience as morality.  Elias believes the Articles must be obeyed because they preserve the peace, even when their individual consequences are monstrous.  Now, we find his beliefs are evolving, though he doesn’t show his hand early or often.
