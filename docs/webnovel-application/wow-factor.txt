The protagonist, June Havlik, a 31-year-old deposition transcriptionist in present-day Pittsburgh, is a wolf by blood but has no affiliation with Local Nine, run by her aunt Marta, because her mother left under unexplained circumstances when she was young, and she was excluded before initiation.  Everyone in the Hall can smell that she's a Havlik, although the official roster denies she exists. June, therefore, has no membership, vote, benefits, safe room, or legal standing in Local Nine.

During a deposition, June is transcribing when she hears her full name entered into the record as the 8th bride owed under The Articles, a 9-clause agreement signed in 1871. The document has preserved peace between Pittsburgh’s werewolves and vampires for over 150 years. Before a lethal clause can be invoked, Elias Marrow  (Vampire Trustee & male protagonist) speaks half of a binding vow, exposing himself and buying June time to investigate The Articles, the earlier brides, and her rights and obligations.  He does so at his own peril without her knowing why.

Through Volume One, the Articles' clauses one, two, and three progressively cost her shelter, livelihood, and her cousin Milo, and slowly reveal the underlying premises existing in this underworld, and the consequences of the Articles as June discovers it, with clauses one and two dominating the first half of the volume and clause three later in the book. Clause three creates the deadline and lawful forfeiture, the low point.

We slowly reveal that Elias might not be acting maliciously and that the Articles were written to stop a slaughter and sincerely preserve peace, eliminating the easy villain approach.  It does not prove they are harmless.

June can physically sense Elias’s presence and feels drawn toward him, and Elias eventually admits he acted out of both protection and desire.

June, smart and resourceful, discovers and chooses a third outcome, defeating three clauses without completing the vow; eventually, she and Elias choose a path based on a consensual relationship, never completing the vow. At one pivot, June is ready to offer it, but Elias won't let her and stops her because there is no accepted witness and a clause is being executed against her. Either condition would invalidate completion. 

Tess is the seventh bride’s sister, and she has spent eleven years building a case requiring a living bride, whom June discovers about from a servant after residing in the Trust estate.  June elects to remain the living bride so she can file Tess Renn’s case, then defeats the first three clauses without destroying the peace agreement or surrendering her consent. Volume one does not establish that June and Elias will never complete the vow. It ends with June choosing to leave it incomplete while they form a bounded alliance.

By the end of Volume One, their victory costs four people dearly: Marta loses her position leading Local Nine, Kenneth loses his membership, Elias becomes permanently ineligible to hold office, and June knowingly elects to remain a party to the Articles. Six clauses remain.
Volume Two begins to unravel the mystery surrounding June's mother and brings clauses four through six into play. June receives a warning that she must discover what her mother took before answering Elias, while Vera introduces her to the trade in severed attachments. Evidence also emerges of an unreported severance involving someone neither supernatural register can identify.
